**CONFIDENTIAL PRIVILEGED MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Brightlane Software B.V.
**From:** mr. H. Yildiz, Immigration & Asylum Law Specialist
**Date:** 12 April 2025
**Subject:** Legal Opinion on the Credibility of the Asylum Account and Risk of Return for Mr. Kavian Farzadi

### 1. Introduction

This memo provides a preliminary legal assessment regarding the asylum application filed by Mr. Kavian Farzadi (passport no. F8920114, issued in Tehran, Iran) on behalf of his employer, Brightlane Software B.V., which is supporting his residence permit request for humanitarian reasons and potential subsidiary protection.

The central legal hurdle in this matter is the credibility assessment conducted by the Immigration and Naturalisation Service (IND). Under Dutch administrative law, the applicant bears the burden of proof to establish that they face a real risk of torture, inhuman or degrading treatment, or punishment upon return to their country of origin. This memo analyzes the current weaknesses in Mr. Farzadi’s account, the legal standard set by Article 29 of the Aliens Act 2000, and the specific documentation required to mitigate these risks.

### 2. Legal Framework: Article 29 Aliens Act 2000

Article 29 of the Aliens Act 2000 (Vreemdelingenwet 2000) governs the grant of temporary residence permits on humanitarian grounds or based on international obligations such as the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR). Specifically, we are assessing whether Mr. Farzadi’s return would violate Article 3 of the ECHR (prohibition of torture) or Article 8 of the ECHR (right to respect for private and family life), though the primary focus remains on the immediate risk of severe harm under the humanitarian clause.

The IND applies a strict credibility test. If the core facts of the asylum account are deemed not credible, the application is rejected unless there are compelling humanitarian reasons that outweigh the lack of credibility. In Mr. Farzadi’s case, his account centers on his alleged involvement in pro-democracy activism while working as a software developer at Brightlane Software B.V., and subsequent threats received from Iranian intelligence agencies (MOIS).

### 3. Analysis of Credibility Risks

#### 3.1. The "Tech Activist" Profile
The IND is highly skeptical of claims that IT professionals are targeted solely for political opinions unless there is concrete evidence of organized resistance activities. Mr. Farzadi’s initial statement described vague online posting and anonymous contributions to opposition blogs. The IND has noted inconsistencies between his claimed level of activity and the digital footprint analysis, which shows no direct links to known opposition infrastructure from his personal devices prior to 2023.

**Risk:** Without corroborating evidence, the IND may rule that Mr. Farzadi’s activities did not reach the threshold of "active participation" required to establish a well-founded fear of persecution.

#### 3.2. Inconsistencies in the Timeline
Mr. Farzadi stated that he first received threatening messages via Telegram in November 2023, yet his employment records with Brightlane show he took leave from October 15, 2023, to October 30, 2023, without reporting for work. The IND has questioned whether this gap was used to flee Iran or to engage in activities that triggered the surveillance, creating a narrative inconsistency regarding his location and intent at the time of the alleged threats.

### 4. Strategy: Strengthening the Case

To overcome the credibility deficit, we must shift the focus from Mr. Farzadi’s subjective account to objective, verifiable evidence. The following steps are critical:

#### 4.1. Expert Witness Report
We require an immediate engagement with **Dr. Elise van der Meer**, a specialist in Iranian political dynamics and digital surveillance at Leiden University. She must produce a report detailing:
*   The standard operating procedures of the MOIS regarding tech-sector critics.
*   Specific case studies of similar software developers who were targeted for minor online dissent.
*   An assessment of the technical feasibility of the threats described by Mr. Farzadi (e.g., whether the IP addresses mentioned could realistically be traced to MOIS servers).

#### 4.2. Digital Forensics Report
We must commission a forensic audit of Mr. Farzadi’s cloud backups and encrypted communications from 2023-2024. Although he claims deletion, metadata in backup services (e.g., iCloud or Google Drive) may reveal the existence of drafts or messages that were later deleted, proving his intent and activity during the critical period.

#### 4.3. Witness Statements
We need sworn affidavits from:
*   **Mr. Jansen**, former colleague at Brightlane Software B.V., who can attest to Mr. Farzadi’s sudden change in behavior and urgency to leave the Netherlands in late 2023.
*   **Ms. Lina Khorrami**, a known human rights lawyer based in Istanbul, who can confirm receipt of encrypted communications from Mr. Farzadi during his time in transit, detailing specific threats received.

#### 4.4. Country of Origin Information (COI) Update
We must submit the latest updates from Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch regarding the escalation of digital surveillance against Iranian expatriates in the Netherlands since January 2025. This contextualizes Mr. Farzadi’s fear as part of a broader, documented trend rather than an isolated incident.

### 5. Risk Assessment under Article 29 Aliens Act 2000

If the credibility assessment remains negative, we must rely on the humanitarian clause of Article 29. This requires demonstrating that Mr. Farzadi would face a **real risk** of severe harm if returned. Given his status as a former employee of a Dutch tech firm and his alleged digital footprint, the IND may consider him "low-risk" for targeted assassination but "high-risk" for arbitrary detention or enforced disappearance.

The risk is elevated by:
1.  The ongoing diplomatic tensions between Iran and the EU, leading to harsher treatment of Iranian nationals in the Netherlands.
2.  Mr. Farzadi’s dual citizenship status (if applicable) being ignored by Iranian authorities, who view him solely as an Iranian citizen and traitor.

### 6. Conclusion and Next Steps

Mr. Farzadi’s current file is vulnerable to rejection on credibility grounds. To prevent this, we must act immediately:

1.  **By 20 April 2025:** Submit the expert report from Dr. van der Meer.
2.  **By 27 April 2025:** File the forensic digital evidence summary.
3.  **By 4 May 2025:** Prepare a supplementary legal brief arguing that even if credibility is partially doubted, the residual risk under Article 29 of the Aliens Act 2000 is sufficient to warrant protection due to the severity of potential punishment in Iran.

Please confirm your instruction to proceed with the engagement of Dr. van der Meer and the forensic firm. This matter is time-sensitive as the IND’s deadline for decision on the initial application is 30 May 2025.
