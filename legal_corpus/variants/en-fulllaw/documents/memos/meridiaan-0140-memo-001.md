**Subject:** Legal Assessment: Defence of Defects vs. Rental Arrears (Matter 2025.0140)

### 1. Introduction and Facts

You have instructed Meridiaan Advocaten to assess your position regarding the ongoing dispute with Stroomdal Media B.V. ("Landlord"). Stroomdal Media has initiated proceedings for the dissolution of the lease agreement due to unpaid rent arrears amounting to €24,500 for the period January 2025 to March 2025.

Nimbus Retail B.V. ("Tenant") intends to defend against this claim by arguing that the leased premises at Kennedyplein 12, Eindhoven, suffer from significant defects. Specifically, you contend that the HVAC system has been non-functional since November 2024 and that there are persistent water infiltration issues in the basement storage area, rendering the premises partially unfit for the intended retail purpose. You wish to know if these defects legally justify the suspension of rental payments.

### 2. Legal Framework: Duty to Maintain vs. Obligation to Pay

Under Dutch law, the obligations of the landlord and tenant are distinct but interrelated. The core provision governing the landlord's duty to maintain the leased property is **Article 7:206 DCC**. This article stipulates that the landlord is obligated to deliver the premises in a condition suitable for the purpose agreed upon and to maintain that condition during the lease term, unless otherwise agreed.

However, the tenant's obligation to pay rent is independent of the landlord's performance regarding maintenance, unless specific legal thresholds are met. The general principle in Dutch tenancy law is that rent cannot be unilaterally withheld solely on the basis of alleged defects without a court order or an explicit agreement. This is known as the independence of obligations (*onafhankelijkheid van verbintenissen*).

### 3. Assessment of the Defence

#### 3.1. Existence of Defects under Article 7:206 DCC

To successfully rely on **Article 7:206 DCC**, Nimbus Retail must prove that:
1. Defects existed at the time of delivery or arose during the lease term;
2. These defects are attributable to the landlord (i.e., not caused by the tenant or normal wear and tear);
3. The defects make the premises wholly or partially unfit for the agreed purpose.

In your case, the HVAC failure since November 2024 constitutes a significant defect. If the retail space requires climate control for inventory preservation or customer comfort, this may render the premises partially unfit. Similarly, water infiltration in the basement storage area affecting goods stored there would support a claim of unfitness for that specific part of the lease.

However, mere inconvenience does not suffice. The tenant must demonstrate that the defects materially impair the use of the property.

#### 3.2. Justification for Suspension of Rent

Crucially, **Article 7:206 DCC** provides a right to demand repair (or self-repair at the landlord's expense) and potentially a reduction in rent (*huurverlaging*), but it does not automatically grant the right to suspend payments entirely.

Case law establishes that suspension of rent is only justified if:
1. The defects are so severe that the premises are wholly unfit for use; or
2. The landlord has failed to repair after being formally notified and given a reasonable period to do so, and the tenant has sought judicial relief.

Since Nimbus Retail continued to operate the store during the defect period without obtaining a court order reducing the rent, the unilateral suspension of payments is legally risky. The courts generally view withheld rent as a breach of contract by the tenant unless the unfitness is total.

#### 3.3. Risk of Dissolution under Article 7:274 DCC

Stroomdal Media relies on **Article 7:274 DCC**, which allows for the termination of a lease agreement if the tenant fails to pay rent. The arrears of €24,500 represent three months of rent. Under Dutch practice, failure to pay rent for two or more consecutive months is often considered a "serious reason" (*dringende reden*) for dissolution.

If Nimbus Retail argues that the defects justify non-payment, the court will examine whether the defects were severe enough to warrant total suspension. Given that the retail floor remained accessible and usable (despite HVAC issues), it is unlikely a court would accept total suspension. The defence might lead to a reduction of rent for the period in question, but it does not erase the debt entirely.

### 4. Strategic Advice

1. **Immediate Counter-Claim**: We must file a counter-claim asserting that the defects constitute a breach of **Article 7:206 DCC**. In this claim, we should request:
   - An order for Stroomdal Media to repair the HVAC and water damage;
   - A retrospective rent reduction (*huurverlaging*) for the period during which the premises were partially unfit.

2. **Mitigation of Arrears**: We should offer to pay the full arrears under protest, conditioned on the court ruling on the defect-related rent reduction. This demonstrates good faith and weakens Stroomdal Media's argument for dissolution under **Article 7:274 DCC**.

3. **Evidence Collection**: Immediately commission an independent expert report detailing the extent of the HVAC failure and water damage, linking them directly to operational losses or inability to use specific parts of the premises.

### 5. Conclusion

The reliance on defects as a defence against arrears is partially valid but insufficient for total suspension. Nimbus Retail B.V. cannot legally withhold all rent payments based solely on **Article 7:206 DCC** without risking dissolution under **Article 7:274 DCC**. The optimal strategy is to acknowledge the debt while simultaneously claiming a significant rent reduction due to unfitness, thereby reducing the net arrears and defeating the dissolution request.
