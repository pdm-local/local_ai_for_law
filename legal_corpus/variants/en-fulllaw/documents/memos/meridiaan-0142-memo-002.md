**SUBJECT:** Legal Advice Regarding the Disproportionality of the Penalty Notice Dated 28 February 2025 (Ref: MUN-EIN-2025-089)

### 1. Introduction and Context

This memorandum addresses the administrative penalty notice issued by the Municipality of Eindhoven (“the Authority”) against Maaslicht Interactive B.V. (“the Client”) on 28 February 2025. The Authority has levied a fine of EUR 4,500 for the failure to submit a final safety report for the “Neon Pulse Festival” held at Strijp-S, Eindhoven, on 15 January 2025. The Client was required to submit this report within seven days post-event pursuant to local ordinance Article 4.2(b). The report was submitted on 30 January 2025, eleven days after the event, constituting a late submission by four days.

The Client has instructed Meridiaan Advocaten B.V. to contest this penalty. Our legal strategy rests primarily on the principle of proportionality as codified in Article 3:4 of the General Administrative Law Act (Algemene wet bestuursrecht, or “GALA”). We argue that the imposition of a EUR 4,500 fine is excessive and disproportionate to the nature and gravity of the breach.

### 2. Legal Framework: The Principle of Proportionality

Article 3:4 GALA establishes that an administrative authority may only take measures that are necessary to achieve a legitimate public interest objective. Furthermore, the measure must not be disproportionate to the interests involved. This principle comprises three sub-principles: suitability, necessity, and proportionality in the strict sense (balancing).

In the context of administrative penalties, the Dutch Supreme Court (HR) has consistently held that the severity of a sanction must correspond to the culpability of the offender and the seriousness of the violation. A penalty is disproportionate if it fails to take into account relevant circumstances, such as the minor nature of the breach, the absence of actual harm to public safety, or the promptness with which the violation was rectified once identified.

### 3. Application to the Facts

#### 3.1. Nature and Severity of the Breach
The breach in question is purely procedural: a four-day delay in submitting an administrative report. The event itself took place without incident. There were no safety failures, no injuries to attendees, and no disruption to public order. The core objective of the local ordinance is to ensure public safety and proper oversight. Since the event concluded safely and the Authority was eventually provided with all necessary information, the substantive goal of the regulation was achieved despite the procedural delay.

Imposing a severe financial penalty for a minor administrative lapse that did not impact the underlying public interest served by the law violates the requirement of suitability in relation to the gravity of the offense.

#### 3.2. Absence of Precedent and Intent
There is no evidence that Maaslicht Interactive B.V. acted with intent or gross negligence. The delay was caused by an internal administrative oversight during the post-event debriefing process, which was immediately corrected upon discovery on 26 January 2025. The Client cooperated fully with the Authority’s inquiry in March 2025. In comparable cases involving minor procedural delays without safety implications, the Authority has frequently applied warnings or significantly lower fines (e.g., EUR 500 – EUR 1,000). A fine of EUR 4,500 represents a punitive measure more akin to sanctions for willful disregard or severe safety violations, which are not present here.

#### 3.3. Financial Impact and Disproportionality
The amount of EUR 4,500 constitutes a significant burden relative to the minor nature of the violation. While Maaslicht Interactive B.V. is a corporate entity, it is a small-to-medium enterprise (SME) with an annual turnover of approximately EUR 1.2 million in 2024. A fine of this magnitude, without mitigation, could be deemed excessive and may deter future compliance rather than encourage it, thereby undermining the educational function of administrative law. The penalty fails the proportionality test in the strict sense because the interference with the Client’s economic interests is manifestly disproportionate to the minor infringement committed.

### 4. Conclusion and Recommendation

The penalty notice of 28 February 2025 violates Article 3:4 GALA (Awb) due to its disproportionate nature. The sanction does not reflect the minor, non-harmful character of the breach nor the Client’s prompt remediation efforts.

We recommend lodging a formal objection (bezwaar) within the statutory period, specifically invoking Article 3:4 GALA (Awb) as the primary ground for annulment. We will request that the Authority either revoke the penalty entirely or reduce it to a symbolic amount of EUR 500 to reflect the procedural nature of the violation.

If the Authority maintains the full fine, we are prepared to appeal to the administrative court, where case law strongly supports our position on proportionality for minor administrative delays.

Please advise if you wish to proceed with the objection filing by 28 March 2025.
