**CONFIDENTIAL MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Management Team, Maaslicht Online B.V.
**From:** mr. T. Bergsma, ICT / Technology Contracts, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**Date:** 15 May 2025
**Re:** Matter 2025.0118 – Service Agreement with Vesting Systems B.V.

### 1. Introduction

This memorandum provides legal advice regarding the draft Service Agreement currently under negotiation between Maaslicht Online B.V. ("Maaslicht") and Vesting Systems B.V. ("Vesting"). The primary concern identified in our review of clause 12 (Limitation of Liability) is the internal inconsistency of the liability caps proposed by Vesting, combined with their overall restrictiveness relative to standard market practice for enterprise software services.

### 2. Analysis of Proposed Liability Caps

The draft contract proposes three distinct mechanisms for limiting Vesting’s financial exposure in the event of breach or negligence:

1.  **A fixed cap of 50.000** per incident;
2.  **A cumulative cap of 25.000** for all claims arising during the contractual term;
3.  **A cap equal to the annual fee** paid by Maaslicht in the preceding twelve months.

These three figures create a direct logical and legal conflict. Specifically, the proposal states that liability shall be limited to the *lower* of these amounts. Given that the "annual fee" for the current engagement is set at 30.000, and the fixed incident cap is 50.000, the operative cap effectively becomes the 25.000 cumulative limit or the annual fee, whichever is lower.

The most critical issue is the contradiction between the **50.000** per-incident figure and the **25.000** total annual cap. If Vesting suffers a data breach or service interruption that causes Maaslicht significant operational disruption, Vesting would argue that their total liability is capped at 25.000, regardless of whether multiple incidents occur. This renders the 50.000 figure meaningless and exposes Maaslicht to substantial unmitigated risk.

### 3. Reasonableness and Fairness (Article 6:248 DCC)

Under **Article 6:248 DCC**, clauses in standard terms or negotiated agreements must meet the requirements of reasonableness and fairness (*redelijkheid en billijkheid*). A liability cap that is disproportionately low compared to the value of the service and the potential risk is often deemed unreasonable.

In this matter, the total annual fee for Maaslicht’s use of Vesting’s platform is 30.000. However, the software is critical to Maaslicht’s core business operations. A cap of **25.000** represents less than one year’s service cost and fails to cover potential consequential damages (such as loss of revenue or reputational harm) that may arise from a system failure.

Furthermore, the conflict between the **50.000** and **25.000** figures demonstrates poor drafting by Vesting’s counsel. In legal interpretation, such ambiguity is typically construed against the drafter (*contra proferentem*). However, relying on judicial interpretation is risky. It is preferable to demand clarity and a higher cap.

### 4. Recommendations

We advise Maaslicht Online B.V. not to accept the current draft. We recommend the following counter-proposals:

1.  **Remove the conflicting caps.** Replace the three-tier structure with a single, clear liability cap.
2.  **Increase the cap.** The liability cap should be no less than **three times the annual fee**. Given the annual fee is 30.000, the cap should be set at **90.000**. This aligns with market standards for critical business software.
3.  **Exclude certain liabilities.** Ensure that the liability cap does not apply to:
    *   Gross negligence or willful misconduct (*opzet*);
    *   Breach of confidentiality;
    *   Infringement of intellectual property rights;
    *   Violations of data protection laws (GDPR).

### 5. Next Steps

Please instruct your procurement team to reject clause 12 as drafted. We have prepared a redline version of the contract with the recommended changes, including a unified liability cap of **90.000** and clear exclusions. We expect Vesting Systems B.V. to respond within ten business days. If they refuse to raise the cap above the current **25.000** limit, we recommend reconsidering the commercial viability of this partnership given the risk exposure.
