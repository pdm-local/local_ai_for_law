**From:** mr. F. Lindeman, Tax Law Specialist, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**Date:** 12 April 2025
**Re:** Matter 2025.0139 — Tax Analysis: Article 8 CIT Act 1969 and Article 3.8 Income Tax Act 2001

### 1. Introduction

This memorandum provides a legal analysis regarding the tax corrections applied to Klaver Logistics B.V. for the fiscal year ending 31 December 2024. The primary focus is on the adjustment of taxable income under **Article 8 Corporate Income Tax Act 1969** and the subsequent implications for the substantial-interest holder, governed by **Article 3.8 Income Tax Act 2001**.

### 2. Analysis of Article 8 Corporate Income Tax Act 1969

The correction stems from the reclassification of certain operational expenses as non-deductible under **Article 8 Corporate Income Tax Act 1969**. Specifically, the Dutch Tax Authority has determined that a portion of the logistics overhead costs incurred in Q3 2024 did not meet the strict "business purpose" requirement mandated by this article.

Consequently, an additional assessment has been levied on the corporate income tax return. The recalculation increases the taxable profit by EUR 145,000. This adjustment directly affects the overall corporate tax liability of Klaver Logistics B.V., requiring a supplementary payment of EUR 28,750 (calculated at the standard rate of 19% for the first bracket and 25.8% for the excess, adjusted for the specific fiscal period).

### 3. Impact on Substantial Interest Holder: Article 3.8 Income Tax Act 2001

The adjustment under **Article 8 Corporate Income Tax Act 1969** has direct repercussions for Mr. J. van der Meer, the substantial-interest holder of Klaver Logistics B.V., who holds a 45% share in the company. Under **Article 3.8 Income Tax Act 2001**, the taxable income from substantial interest is calculated based on the book value of the shares and the deemed return (forafvast rendement), adjusted for actual dividends received and the company's taxable profit.

The increase in Klaver Logistics B.V.'s taxable profit by EUR 145,000 results in a corresponding increase in the notional income attributed to Mr. van der Meer. Although no additional dividends were paid out, the deemed return calculation relies on the equity value and the statutory fixed return percentage. The correction does not alter the share price directly for valuation purposes under Section A of the Income Tax Act 2001, but it confirms the profitability of the entity, which stabilizes the equity base used for the box 2 taxation model.

However, if the correction leads to a reduction in distributable reserves due to the tax payment, this may affect future dividend capacity, indirectly influencing the actual income component of **Article 3.8 Income Tax Act 2001**. For now, the primary impact is the confirmation of the company's financial health under the corrected profit figure.

### 4. Conclusion and Recommendations

The correction under **Article 8 Corporate Income Tax Act 1969** is upheld based on current case law regarding deductibility of logistics overheads. The firm recommends that Klaver Logistics B.V. settles the additional tax assessment by 30 May 2025 to avoid interest charges.

For the substantial-interest holder, **Article 3.8 Income Tax Act 2001** remains applicable with the corrected profit figures incorporated into the annual return. We advise reviewing the dividend policy for 2025 to ensure compliance with both corporate governance standards and tax efficiency goals.
