### 1. Introduction and Background

This memo provides legal advice regarding the ongoing dispute between our client, Helix Networks B.V. ("Landlord"), and its tenant, Brightlane Retail B.V. ("Tenant"), concerning the leased premises located at Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven. The Tenant has ceased paying rent for the periods of January, February, and March 2025, citing significant defects in the property’s HVAC system and structural integrity as a legal defence against payment obligations.

The Landlord is considering initiating proceedings for termination of the lease agreement based on persistent non-payment. This memo assesses the legal viability of the Tenant’s defence under Article 7:206 DCC (Burgerlijk Wetboek) and the potential consequences for a termination claim under Article 7:274 DCC.

### 2. Legal Framework: The Landlord’s Duty to Maintain

Under **Article 7:206 DCC**, the landlord is obligated to deliver the leased property in good condition and keep it in that state during the term of the lease. This duty includes necessary repairs and maintenance required for the proper use of the property by the tenant.

For the Tenant’s defence to succeed, two conditions must be met:
1.  **Existence of Defects:** The defects must be such that they substantially hinder the Tenant’s use of the premises for its intended commercial purpose (retail operations).
2.  **Timely Notification:** The Tenant must have notified the Landlord of these defects within a reasonable time after becoming aware of them, allowing the Landlord an opportunity to remedy them.

Based on the file, Brightlane Retail B.V. submitted written notice regarding the HVAC failure on 15 December 2024 and the roof leakage on 02 January 2025. Our client received these notices but failed to initiate repairs until late March 2025. The delay in remediation is a critical factor. Under Dutch case law, if the landlord fails to act within a reasonable period after notification, the tenant may be entitled to suspend performance of their own obligations (payment of rent) either partially or fully, depending on the severity of the defect.

### 3. Assessment of the Suspension Right

The Tenant’s complete suspension of rent for three consecutive months is legally precarious. The principle of proportionality applies. A total suspension is generally only justified if the defects render the premises completely unusable. Given that the Tenant continued to occupy and operate parts of the retail space, a court would likely view a full suspension as disproportionate.

However, the Tenant does have a strong claim for a **rent reduction** (hurendaling) or a right to self-help remedies where they deduct repair costs from future rent, provided they followed proper procedures. The reliance on Article 7:206 DCC is therefore valid but likely overstated in terms of its effect on the entire debt.

### 4. Implications for Termination under Article 7:274 DCC

Our client intends to rely on **Article 7:274 DCC** to terminate the lease agreement due to non-payment. This article allows a landlord to seek termination if the tenant fails to fulfill their obligations, such as paying rent, and the breach is attributable to the tenant.

The critical legal risk here is that the court may find the breach *not* attributable to the Tenant if the defects were severe and unresolved for too long. If the court accepts that the Tenant’s non-payment was a justified response to the Landlord’s failure to maintain the property under Article 7:206 DCC, the grounds for termination under Article 7:274 DCC may fail.

Furthermore, if we proceed to litigation without first formally demanding payment and offering a cure period, or without addressing the maintenance claims, we risk the court ordering specific performance (forcing repairs) rather than termination. The courts prefer to preserve lease relationships when possible, especially in commercial tenancies, unless the breach is irreparable.

### 5. Strategic Recommendations

1.  **Immediate Negotiation:** Before filing for termination, we should engage Brightlane Retail B.V. in settlement talks. Acknowledge the delay in repairs and propose a retroactive rent reduction for the period of January–March 2025 to offset the defects, rather than admitting liability for full arrears.
2.  **Formal Notice:** Issue a final formal notice (ingebrekestelling) demanding payment of the undisputed portion of rent, while explicitly reserving our client’s right to contest any counterclaims regarding defects until they are substantiated with independent expert reports.
3.  **Evidence Preservation:** Commission an independent technical inspection report dated 10 April 2025 to document the current state of repairs and establish the timeline of negligence on both sides. This will be crucial if Article 7:274 DCC proceedings become unavoidable.

### 6. Conclusion

While Brightlane Retail B.V. has a valid defence under Article 7:206 DCC regarding the quality of the premises, their complete refusal to pay rent for three months is likely excessive. However, proceeding immediately with termination under Article 7:274 DCC carries a significant risk of failure if the court finds the landlord’s maintenance obligations were breached first. A negotiated settlement involving a partial rent waiver is the most cost-effective and legally secure path forward.
