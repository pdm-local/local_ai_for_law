**SUBJECT:** Analysis of Criminal Liability under Article 420bis Criminal Code regarding Funds from Global Freight Partners Ltd.

### 1. Introduction and Purpose of this Memo

You have instructed Meridiaan Advocaten B.V. to provide legal advice concerning a specific financial transaction received by Saffier Logistics B.V. (“the Company”) on 28 April 2025. The transaction involves a deposit of € 48.000 made by Global Freight Partners Ltd., a counterparty based in the Cayman Islands, for which no corresponding invoice or service description was provided in your internal accounting system at the time of receipt.

This memo is drafted in confidence under attorney-client privilege to assess whether the receipt and subsequent handling of these funds constitute the crime of money laundering under Article 420bis of the Dutch Criminal Code (Wetboek van Strafrecht, hereinafter: “StR”).

### 2. Factual Background

On 28 April 2025, the bank account of Saffier Logistics B.V., located at Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven, received a wire transfer in the amount of € 48.000. The remitter was identified as Global Freight Partners Ltd. (Registration Number: KY-9921). According to your internal records, this payment was categorized as “Miscellaneous Income” due to a lack of immediate documentation from the counterparty regarding the nature of the transfer.

On 3 May 2025, you contacted Global Freight Partners Ltd. via email (contact@globalfreightpartners.ky) requesting an explanation and supporting documentation for this payment. As of the date of this memo, no response has been received, and the funds remain in your operating account.

### 3. Legal Framework: Article 420bis Criminal Code

The relevant statutory provision is Article 420bis StR, which criminalizes money laundering. The article states that a person commits the crime if they:
1. Conceal or disguise the true nature, source, location, disposition, movement, or ownership of property; or
2. Acquires, possesses, or uses such property;

knowing at the time of receipt, on reasonable grounds, or in circumstances which ought to have indicated, that the property was derived from a criminal offense.

The core element for liability is **knowledge** (wetenschap) or **reasonable grounds** to believe the funds originate from illegal activities. Dutch case law establishes that this knowledge can be actual knowledge or "willful blindness" where the suspect deliberately ignores obvious signs of illegality.

### 4. Analysis of Liability for Saffier Logistics B.V.

#### 4.1 The Element of Knowledge (Kennis)

For a conviction under Article 420bis StR, it must be proven that the Company (through its directors or employees acting within the scope of their duties) knew that the € 48.000 was derived from crime. Given that no invoice was provided initially, we must assess whether the circumstances created "reasonable grounds" for suspicion.

Factors indicating potential suspicion include:
*   **Jurisdiction:** The counterparty is registered in the Cayman Islands, a jurisdiction often associated with offshore structuring and lower transparency requirements.
*   **Lack of Documentation:** The absence of an invoice or contract for a logistics service contradicts standard commercial practices.
*   **Amount:** While € 48.000 is not an exceptionally large sum in corporate terms, it exceeds the threshold for simplified due diligence in many internal compliance policies.

However, mere suspicion is not sufficient for criminal liability. There must be concrete indications that the funds are linked to predicate offenses such as fraud, tax evasion, or drug trafficking. At this stage, based solely on the lack of documentation, it is difficult to prove *actual knowledge* that the money came from crime. The risk lies in whether a court would deem the failure to investigate further as "willful blindness."

#### 4.2 The Act of Acquisition and Possession

By retaining the € 48.000 in your corporate account without clarification, Saffier Logistics B.V. is technically "possessing" property that may have a criminal origin. If it is later determined that Global Freight Partners Ltd. was involved in tax fraud or smuggling, your possession of these funds could be viewed as facilitating the laundering process by integrating illicit funds into the legitimate financial system.

#### 4.3 Corporate Criminal Liability

Under Dutch law, Saffier Logistics B.V. can be held criminally liable if the act is attributed to a board member who acted with intent or gross negligence regarding the compliance obligations. If a director ignored red flags (such as the offshore nature of the counterparty and lack of invoices), they could be personally liable for aiding and abetting money laundering, and the Company itself could face fines up to € 820.000 under Article 51 StR.

### 5. Risk Assessment

The primary risk is not immediate prosecution but rather regulatory scrutiny. The Financial Intelligence Service (FIU-Netherlands) may flag this transaction if it appears in future audits or if Global Freight Partners Ltd. becomes the subject of an investigation. If the funds are deemed proceeds of crime, they can be confiscated from your account under Article 10a of the Criminal Code.

Furthermore, retaining unexplained funds violates the Duty of Care and good governance standards expected of Dutch companies. It exposes the directors to civil liability for breach of their fiduciary duties.

### 6. Recommended Actions

To mitigate criminal and civil exposure, I advise the following immediate steps:

1.  **Freeze the Funds:** Do not spend or transfer the € 48.000. Keep the funds in a segregated account or ensure they are clearly marked as "pending clarification" in your ledgers.
2.  **Formal Written Inquiry:** Send a formal letter to Global Freight Partners Ltd. demanding immediate proof of the origin of funds (source of wealth) and the commercial basis for the payment. Set a deadline of five business days.
3.  **Internal Compliance Report:** Document all steps taken in response to this irregularity. This documentation will be crucial evidence of your lack of intent to launder money, should an investigation occur.
4.  **Return of Funds (if no satisfactory response):** If no valid commercial justification is provided within the deadline, return the funds to the originating account. This severs the link to potential illicit activity and demonstrates good faith.
5.  **Suspicious Transaction Report (STR):** If you suspect the funds are linked to crime after reviewing any provided documentation, you are legally obligated under the Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing Act (Wwft) to file a STR with the FIU-Netherlands. Failure to do so can result in administrative fines.

### 7. Conclusion

While the mere receipt of € 48.000 from an offshore entity without documentation does not automatically constitute money laundering under Article 420bis Criminal Code, it creates a significant legal risk. The lack of transparency raises reasonable grounds for suspicion. By retaining the funds without clarification, Saffier Logistics B.V. exposes itself to potential confiscation and criminal liability for its directors.

I recommend the immediate return of the funds or obtaining irrefutable proof of their lawful origin within five business days. Please contact me immediately if Global Freight Partners Ltd. responds or if further legal action is required.
