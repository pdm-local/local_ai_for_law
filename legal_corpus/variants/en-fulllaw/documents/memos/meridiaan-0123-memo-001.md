### 1. Introduction and Purpose

This memorandum provides legal advice concerning the maintenance obligations arising from the dissolution of the marital partnership between the stakeholders of Zenith Cloud B.V. and Meridiaan Technologies B.V. The analysis focuses on the calculation of spousal maintenance and child maintenance in accordance with Dutch statutory law, specifically evaluating the principles of need and capacity.

### 2. Spousal Maintenance (Article 1:157 DCC)

Under Article 1:157 of the Dutch Civil Code (DCC), a spouse may be entitled to spousal maintenance if they are unable to secure sufficient means of subsistence through their own assets or income, taking into account all circumstances of the case. The primary objective is to mitigate the economic consequences of the divorce and ensure both parties can maintain a standard of living reasonably comparable to that enjoyed during the marriage, subject to the paying party's capacity.

Based on the financial disclosure provided by Zenith Cloud B.V., we have assessed the net disposable income and the reasonable standard of living required for the receiving spouse. The calculation determines that a monthly amount of **€ 1.850** is both necessary for the recipient’s subsistence and within the paying party's capacity without causing undue hardship to the payer’s own essential needs or business operations. This figure reflects the current market rates for housing, insurance, and daily living expenses in Eindhoven, adjusted for inflation and tax implications.

### 3. Child Maintenance (Article 1:404 DCC)

Pursuant to Article 1:404 of the Dutch Civil Code (DCC), parents are obliged to provide maintenance for their children until they reach majority or complete their first educational program, whichever occurs later. The amount is determined by the principle of "need and capacity," where the child's need is weighed against the financial ability of both parents.

The standard guideline used by Dutch courts and legal practitioners indicates that child maintenance should cover a portion of the child's living expenses proportional to the parents' incomes. In this matter, considering the income disparity between the parties and the specific requirements for education, healthcare, and leisure activities for the minor children, we have calculated the obligation at **€ 390** per child per month. This amount is consistent with the prevailing judicial standards for families with combined annual incomes in the upper-middle bracket, ensuring the children's quality of life remains stable post-divorce.

### 4. Conclusion and Recommendations

1.  **Spousal Maintenance:** We advise formalizing the spousal maintenance obligation at **€ 1.850** per month, citing Article 1:157 DCC as the legal basis. This amount is defensible in court should enforcement become necessary.
2.  **Child Maintenance:** We advise setting the child maintenance at **€ 390** per child per month, citing Article 1:404 DCC. This aligns with statutory guidelines and ensures compliance with the best interests of the child principle.

Both figures are fixed monthly amounts, subject to annual adjustment in line with general wage and price developments as stipulated in the settlement agreement. We recommend proceeding with the drafting of the final divorce settlement incorporating these specific monetary values to avoid future disputes regarding the scope of maintenance obligations.
