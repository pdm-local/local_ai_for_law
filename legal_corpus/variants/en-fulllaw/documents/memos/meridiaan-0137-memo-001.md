**Re:** Matter 2025.0137 – Assessment of Maintenance Liability

### 1. Introduction and Context

We have received instructions from Novael Systems B.V. (hereinafter: "the Company") regarding ongoing family law proceedings involving the Company’s former director, Mr. Jan van der Meer, and his ex-spouse, Mrs. Elise van der Meer, as well as their two minor children. The opposing party has filed a claim for maintenance payments that directly implicate the Company due to its role in determining Mr. van der Meer’s taxable income and net salary.

This memo provides our legal assessment of the claimed amounts under Dutch law and the likelihood of success for the opposing party, given the statutory requirements for need and capacity. We note that Hanze Interactive B.V. is also a relevant party in this context, particularly regarding any shared liability or cross-referenced employment contracts that may affect the calculation of Mr. van der Meer’s total remuneration package.

### 2. Spousal Maintenance Claim (Article 1:157 DCC)

The opposing party claims spousal maintenance in the amount of **€ 2.400** per month. This figure is derived from the standard "one-third rule" often applied in preliminary assessments, based on Mr. van der Meer’s gross annual salary as recorded in the Company’s administrative records for the fiscal year ending 31 December 2024.

However, under **Article 1:157 DCC**, spousal maintenance is not an automatic right but depends strictly on two factors: the necessity of the claimant and the capacity of the obligor to pay. The Court will look beyond the gross salary to determine Mr. van der Meer’s *net* disposable income after all statutory deductions, including corporate tax liabilities and social security contributions.

Our analysis indicates that while the gross figure supports a claim for maintenance, the net capacity is significantly lower than the claimed € 2.400. The Company’s recent restructuring has impacted the bonus structure previously included in the calculation. We advise that we contest the € 2.400 figure on the grounds that it does not accurately reflect the current net income available for private use, particularly given the volatility of performance-based compensation which is no longer guaranteed under the revised employment contract dated 15 March 2024.

### 3. Child Maintenance Claim (Article 1:404 DCC)

The claim includes child maintenance for two children at **€ 485** per child per month, totaling € 970 monthly. Under **Article 1:404 DCC**, parents are obligated to provide for the needs of their children proportionate to their means. The amount of € 485 aligns with the current statutory table published by the Dutch government, which is often used as a baseline reference by courts.

Unlike spousal maintenance, child maintenance is considered a primary obligation. The Court will assess whether this amount is reasonable in light of the children’s specific needs (education, housing, etc.) and the parents' respective financial capacities. While the figure of € 485 is standard, we must ensure that Mr. van der Meer’s income calculation for tax purposes does not artificially inflate his apparent capacity. We recommend submitting an updated payroll administration to the Court to demonstrate that the actual net income available for child support is constrained by other legitimate business expenses and pension contributions mandated by the Company’s articles of association.

### 4. Strategic Recommendations

1.  **Documentation:** Provide us with the finalized annual accounts of Novael Systems B.V. for 2024 and the preliminary figures for 2025 to substantiate any arguments regarding reduced profitability or changes in executive compensation policy.
2.  **Negotiation:** We propose a settlement conference where we can argue that the € 2.400 spousal maintenance is excessive given the current net income, while accepting the € 485 child maintenance figure as legally sound but subject to review based on the children’s actual costs.
3.  **Litigation Stance:** If no agreement is reached, we will vigorously defend against the spousal maintenance claim by challenging the calculation basis used by the opposing counsel, specifically arguing that gross salary figures should not be dispositive under Article 1:157 DCC without deduction of necessary corporate-related costs borne personally by Mr. van der Meer.

We await your instructions to proceed with the submission of these financial documents to the District Court in Eindhoven.
