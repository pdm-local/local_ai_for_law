# PRIVILEGED AND CONFIDENTIAL
# LEGAL ADVICE MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Legal Department, Aurora Systems B.V., Eindhoven
**FROM:** mr. N. Vrugt, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V., Internet Law & E-commerce Group
**DATE:** 12 May 2025
**SUBJECT:** Liability exemption for hosting intermediaries and the effect of notice under Article 6:196c DCC

## 1. Executive Summary

Aurora Systems B.V. operates a user-generated content platform allowing businesses to display promotional material. This memorandum addresses whether Aurora qualifies as a "hosting service provider" exempt from liability for illegal content posted by users, specifically in light of recent complaints received from Zenith Networks B.V. regarding infringing advertisements on Aurora’s platform.

We conclude that Aurora is eligible for the hosting exemption under **Article 6:196c DCC** provided it maintains a "passive" and "neutral" role. However, this protection is contingent upon Aurora having no actual knowledge of the illegality. Upon receipt of a sufficiently precise notice from Zenith Networks B.V., Aurora acquires such knowledge. Consequently, to maintain its exemption, Aurora must act expeditiously to remove or disable access to the illegal content. Failure to do so may result in joint liability for damages sustained by Zenith Networks B.V.

## 2. Factual Background

Aurora Systems B.V. operates "MarketHub," a digital platform where third-party vendors can upload images and descriptions of goods for sale. The platform’s Terms of Service (effective 1 January 2025) explicitly prohibit the posting of counterfeit goods, defamatory material, or content violating intellectual property rights.

On 3 May 2025, Zenith Networks B.V., a competitor of one of Aurora’s vendors, submitted a formal notice to Aurora’s designated legal contact at Kennedypark 14, Eindhoven. The notice identified specific URLs containing images of Zenith’s proprietary product designs used without authorization. Zenith demands the immediate removal of these listings, asserting that Aurora is liable for hosting this infringing content.

## 3. Legal Analysis: The Hosting Exemption under Article 6:196c DCC

### 3.1 Qualification as a Hosting Provider

Under Dutch law, implementing the E-Commerce Directive (2000/31/EC), **Article 6:196c DCC** grants liability exemptions to information society service providers. To qualify for the hosting exemption, Aurora must demonstrate that it provides a service consisting of the storage of information provided by a recipient of the service.

Aurora’s activities are purely technical, automatic, and passive. The platform does not select or modify the content uploaded by users; it merely stores the data submitted via its standard interface. Therefore, Aurora acts as a neutral intermediary rather than a publisher. This neutrality is crucial because liability would attach if Aurora were involved in editing, selecting, or optimizing the content for commercial gain beyond mere storage fees.

### 3.2 Conditions for Exemption

The exemption under **Article 6:196c** applies only if two cumulative conditions are met:

1.  **Lack of Knowledge:** The provider does not have actual knowledge of illegal activity or information. If Aurora is aware that the content is unlawful, the exemption ceases to apply unless it acts to remove the content.
2.  **Expeditious Action:** Upon obtaining such knowledge, the provider must act expeditiously to remove the data or disable access to it.

Currently, before Zenith Networks B.V.’s notice on 3 May 2025, Aurora had no actual knowledge of the specific infringement. The general Terms of Service do not constitute constructive knowledge sufficient to impose liability under Dutch case law (*HR 19 March 2004, ECLI:NL:HR:2004:AK7686*).

## 4. Impact of Notice and Subsequent Liability

### 4.1 Acquisition of Knowledge

The notice received from Zenith Networks B.V. on 3 May 2025 constitutes "actual knowledge" within the meaning of **Article 6:196c DCC**. The notice was specific, identifying the infringing URLs and providing prima facie evidence of IP infringement. It is no longer sufficient for Aurora to claim ignorance.

### 4.2 Duty to Act Expeditiously

Once knowledge is acquired, the legal obligation shifts from passive storage to active monitoring of that specific content. Aurora must act "expeditiously." In the context of digital platforms, Dutch courts and prevailing legal doctrine interpret this as requiring action within a reasonable time frame, typically interpreted as 24 to 48 hours for clear-cut IP violations, or immediately if the illegality is obvious (e.g., child sexual abuse material).

Given that Zenith’s notice was received on 3 May 2025, Aurora was obligated to review and remove the content by no later than 4 May 2025. If Aurora delayed removal until after this window without a valid technical or legal justification (such as a pending counter-notice procedure under the Digital Services Act), it loses the protection of **Article 6:196c DCC**.

### 4.3 Consequences of Non-Compliance

If Aurora fails to remove the infringing content expeditiously after receiving notice, it may be held jointly liable for damages suffered by Zenith Networks B.V. under general tort law (**Article 6:162 DCC**). The exemption in **Article 6:196c DCC** is a shield against liability; it does not grant immunity from the duty to cease ongoing illegal acts once known.

Furthermore, continued hosting after notice may be viewed as "active" involvement, potentially disqualifying Aurora from the neutral intermediary status entirely, exposing it to claims for injunctions and compensation for lost licensing fees.

## 5. Recommendations

1.  **Immediate Removal:** Aurora must remove or disable access to the specific URLs cited in Zenith’s notice of 3 May 2025 immediately upon confirmation of the infringement. Do not delay pending a full internal investigation if the illegality is apparent from the notice.
2.  **Document the Process:** Maintain records of the receipt of Zenith’s notice, the time of review, and the timestamp of removal to demonstrate "expeditious" action as required by **Article 6:196c DCC**.
3.  **Counter-Notice Procedure:** If the vendor disputes the claim, Aurora should implement a transparent counter-notice procedure. However, this must not be used as a pretext to delay removal while the dispute is resolved, unless there is a clear legal basis to believe the claim is frivolous.
4.  **Platform Design:** Consider implementing automated filtering tools for future uploads to proactively identify potential infringements, though note that general monitoring obligations are limited under EU law (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065).

## 6. Conclusion

Aurora Systems B.V. is exempt from liability as a hosting intermediary under **Article 6:196c DCC** prior to obtaining knowledge of the illegal content. However, upon receiving Zenith Networks B.V.’s specific notice on 3 May 2025, Aurora acquired such knowledge. To preserve its exemption and avoid joint tort liability, Aurora must act expeditiously to remove the infringing material. Any delay beyond a reasonable period constitutes a breach of this duty, rendering Aurora liable for subsequent damages.

This advice is based on current Dutch statutory law and case law as of May 2025.
