**SUBJECT:** Legal Assessment: Article 8 ECHR Balancing Test in the Matter of Mr. David Chen (File No. 2025.0131)

### 1. Introduction and Background

This memorandum addresses the urgent legal situation concerning your employee, Mr. David Chen (passport number N8849201, born 14 March 1985), who is currently facing a final deportation order issued by the Immigration and Naturalisation Service (IND) on 10 May 2025. The IND has declared Mr. Chen undesirable under Article 67 of the Aliens Act 2000 due to a criminal conviction for fraud in 2019, despite his long-term residence in the Netherlands and his established family ties here.

Mr. Chen’s wife, Mrs. Lin Chen (Dutch national), and their two minor children reside at Kennedyplein 200, Apt 4B, Eindhoven. Mr. Chen argues that deportation would violate his right to respect for his private and family life under Article 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR).

The purpose of this document is to outline the legal framework governing the balancing test under Article 8 ECHR and to assess the prospects of success in challenging the deportation order before the Administrative Jurisdiction Division of the Council of State.

### 2. Legal Framework: The Balancing Test under Article 8 ECHR

Article 8 ECHR protects the right to respect for private and family life. While this right is not absolute, any interference by a public authority must be:
1. In accordance with the law;
2. Pursue a legitimate aim (in this case, the prevention of disorder or crime, and the economic well-being of the country); and
3. Necessary in a democratic society (proportionate).

The European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) has established that where an applicant claims expulsion would breach Article 8, the decision-maker must weigh the interests of the individual against those of the community at large. This is known as the "balancing test."

#### A. The Strength of Family Ties
To succeed, Mr. Chen must demonstrate that his family ties in the Netherlands are strong enough to outweigh the public interest in deportation. Relevant factors include:
*   **Duration of residence:** Mr. Chen has lived in the Netherlands since 2010 (over 15 years).
*   **Nature and stability of family life:** His wife is a Dutch national, and his children are minor citizens born and raised entirely within the Dutch education system. The bond between Mr. Chen and his children is central to their upbringing.
*   **Best interests of the child:** Under Article 24 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the EU and Article 3 of the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, the best interests of the child must be a primary consideration. Separating Mr. Chen from his minor children constitutes a severe interference with family life.

#### B. The Public Interest in Immigration Control
The Netherlands has a legitimate interest in enforcing its immigration laws and combating crime. The IND’s position relies on:
*   **Severity of the offense:** The 2019 fraud conviction, while serious, was a non-violent economic crime committed six years ago.
*   **Risk of reoffending:** Mr. Chen has led a law-abiding life since his release and holds a senior technical position at Brightlane Software B.V., where he is considered indispensable for ongoing EU-funded ICT projects.
*   **Proportionality of the measure:** The ECtHR often finds that permanent bans or deportation are disproportionate for non-violent crimes if the individual has strong family ties and has rehabilitated themselves.

### 3. Application to Mr. Chen’s Case

#### The Indispensability Factor
Mr. Chen’s role at Brightlane Software B.V. is not merely economic; it involves specialized knowledge in cross-border data compliance (GDPR) and ICT infrastructure that cannot be easily replaced within the required timeframe. His deportation would disrupt critical services for EU clients, potentially causing significant financial loss to your company and undermining trust in Dutch tech sector stability. This supports the argument that his presence contributes to the economic well-being of the country, a factor considered under Article 8 ECHR.

#### The "Insurmountable Obstacles" Test
We must also consider whether Mrs. Chen and the children could reasonably relocate to China (Mr. Chen’s country of origin). Given that:
*   Mrs. Chen has no professional ties to China;
*   The children are Dutch citizens with no fluency in Mandarin and no social network there;
*   Mr. Chen’s family remains in Eindhoven, indicating a lack of active integration support structures in China;

It is highly likely that relocation would impose insurmountable obstacles on the family unit. The ECtHR has consistently held that when one spouse and minor children are integrated into the host country, deportation of the other parent violates Article 8 ECHR unless there are very strong public policy reasons to justify it.

#### Precedent and Case Law
In *Jeunesse v. the Netherlands* (ECtHR, 2014), the Court emphasized that where children are nationals of the host state and have lived there since birth, the margin of appreciation for the state is narrow. Similarly, in *Boultif v. Switzerland*, the Court listed criteria including the nature and seriousness of the offense, but noted that deportation must be necessary in a democratic society. Given Mr. Chen’s lack of violent history and strong rehabilitative record, the threshold for "necessity" is not met.

### 4. Strategic Recommendations

1.  **File an Objection and Appeal:** We must file an immediate objection with the IND within six weeks (by 07 July 2025) citing Article 8 ECHR. Simultaneously, prepare an appeal to the Administrative Jurisdiction Division of the Council of State.
2.  **Gather Supporting Evidence:**
    *   Letters from Brightlane Software B.V. confirming Mr. Chen’s indispensable role and the project deadlines affected by his absence.
    *   Statements from school teachers regarding the children’s emotional dependency on their father.
    *   Psychiatric evaluation of Mrs. Chen demonstrating the impact of separation on family stability.
3.  **Highlight Proportionality:** Argue that the penalty (permanent deportation) is disproportionate to the crime (non-violent fraud from 2019), especially given his 15-year clean record since then.

### 5. Conclusion

The balancing test under Article 8 ECHR strongly favors Mr. Chen. The interference with his family life is severe, and the public interest in deportation, while legitimate, does not outweigh the rights of his minor children and his wife to maintain their family unit in the Netherlands. We have a high probability of success if we can demonstrate that relocation is impossible and that his continued presence serves the economic well-being of the country through his technical expertise.

We recommend proceeding with the appeal immediately. The deadline for filing is strict, and any delay may result in the enforcement of the deportation order before judicial review is complete.
