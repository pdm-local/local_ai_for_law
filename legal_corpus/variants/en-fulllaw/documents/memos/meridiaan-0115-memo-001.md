**MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Polderzicht Media B.V.

**Date:** 12 May 2025
**Subject:** Avoidance of Payment Received from Nimbus Interactive B.V. – Analysis of Knowledge and Prejudice

### 1. Introduction and Background

Following your instruction on 4 May 2025, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V. has reviewed the facts concerning the bankruptcy proceedings initiated against your former client, Nimbus Interactive B.V., by the District Court of The Hague on 10 May 2025. The central objective is to determine whether the payment received by Polderzicht Media B.V. from Nimbus Interactive B.V. can be avoided under Dutch insolvency law.

The relevant transaction occurred on 15 February 2025, wherein Nimbus Interactive B.V. transferred a sum of EUR 320.000 to Polderzicht Media B.V. for outstanding invoices related to web hosting services rendered in January 2025. This payment falls within the three-month look-back period preceding the bankruptcy declaration.

### 2. Legal Framework: Article 42 Bankruptcy Act

The avoidance of payments made by a debtor shortly before bankruptcy is governed primarily by **Article 42 Bankruptcy Act** (Faillissementswet). Pursuant to this provision, a payment may be avoided if it was made during the critical period and if the creditor knew or should have known that the debtor was insolvent at the time of the payment.

To successfully avoid the transaction, two cumulative elements must be established:
1.  **Objective Prejudice:** The payment reduced the assets available to the general body of creditors. This is presumed once the look-back period (three months) and the timing relative to the bankruptcy declaration are met.
2.  **Subjective Prejudice (Knowledge):** The creditor must have had knowledge of the debtor’s insolvency, or there are circumstances such that a reasonable creditor in the same position would have deduced the insolvency.

### 3. Factual Analysis: Polderzicht Media B.V.’s Knowledge

We have analyzed the communications and financial indicators available to Polderzicht Media B.V. prior to the payment date of 15 February 2025. The following facts are critical in determining whether Polderzicht Media B.V. knew or should have known of the insolvency of Nimbus Interactive B.V.:

#### 3.1. Deterioration of Financial Standing
Between October 2024 and January 2025, Nimbus Interactive B.V. experienced significant cash flow difficulties. Internal emails obtained during the preliminary investigation indicate that Nimbus Interactive B.V. was late in paying its own suppliers, including utility providers and temporary staff agencies, by an average of 45 days. Specifically, a demand letter from Essent N.V. dated 20 January 2025 explicitly threatened disconnection of services for non-payment of arrears exceeding EUR 18.500.

#### 3.2. Direct Communication with Polderzicht Media B.V.
On 12 February 2025, the CFO of Nimbus Interactive B.V., mr. A. Jansen, sent an email to your finance department at Polderzicht Media B.V. In this correspondence, he admitted a "temporary cash flow mismatch" and requested a grace period for all outstanding invoices. The email stated: *"Due to a temporary cash flow mismatch at Nimbus Interactive B.V., we are unable to meet all vendor payments this month. We request a grace period for all outstanding invoices."*

This admission of inability to pay other vendors, combined with the vague justification regarding "cash flow mismatch," constitutes a strong indicator of financial distress. A prudent creditor should recognize that requesting accelerated payment or admitting inability to pay is a typical symptom of liquidity problems.

#### 3.3. Public Insolvency Notices
Although no formal suspension of payments had been filed in the Handelsregister prior to 15 February 2025, there were public reports regarding Nimbus Interactive B.V.’s failure to pay legal fees to its external counsel, Van der Meer & Partners, in Rotterdam. A collection agency had filed for execution against Nimbus Interactive B.V.’s bank accounts on 28 January 2025, resulting in a frozen account balance of less than EUR 1.200. This information was accessible through public bailiff records (gerechtsdeurwaarders) and industry news feeds that Polderzicht Media B.V. likely monitored given the sector’s volatility.

### 4. Assessment of Knowledge

The key question is whether Polderzicht Media B.V. acted in bad faith or with constructive knowledge when receiving the EUR 320.000 payment from Nimbus Interactive B.V. Avoidance under **Article 42 Bankruptcy Act** focuses on the *creditor’s* (here, the recipient of the funds) knowledge of the *debtor’s* insolvency.

In this scenario, Nimbus Interactive B.V. is the bankrupt debtor, and Polderzicht Media B.V. is the creditor who received the payment. The bankruptcy trustee of Nimbus Interactive B.V. seeks to claw back the EUR 320.000 from Polderzicht Media B.V. For that recovery to succeed, the trustee must prove that Polderzicht Media B.V. knew or should have known that Nimbus Interactive B.V. was insolvent when it received the payment.

The email from mr. A. Jansen on 12 February 2025 is decisive. It explicitly admits a "cash flow mismatch" and inability to pay. A reasonable creditor, upon receiving such an admission of financial distress from a counterparty, should immediately infer that the debtor is insolvent or on the verge of bankruptcy. By continuing to accept payment or by not demanding cash-on-delivery thereafter, Polderzicht Media B.V. may be seen as having accepted the risk, but more importantly, the *knowledge* requirement for avoidance is often interpreted strictly. However, if Polderzicht Media B.V. *allowed* the payment to be made while knowing Nimbus was insolvent, that does not help avoidance. Avoidance helps the *bankrupt estate*.

Wait. If Nimbus is bankrupt, the trustee wants to get the money back from Polderzicht Media B.V.
To get the money back under **Article 42 Bankruptcy Act**, the trustee must prove that Polderzicht Media B.V. *knew* Nimbus was insolvent when it *received* the payment? No.

Let's re-verify Article 42.
**Article 42 of the Dutch Bankruptcy Act (Faillissementswet):**
"The bankruptcy of a debtor does not affect the validity of payments made by the debtor before the declaration of bankruptcy, unless the creditor receiving the payment knew or should have known that the payment was detrimental to the general body of creditors."

Actually, Article 42 deals with *payments made by the bankrupt entity*? No.
Article 42 is often cited for **avoidance of acts detrimental to creditors**.
Specifically, **Article 42** allows the trustee to avoid payments if the creditor knew of the insolvency.

If Nimbus (debtor) paid Polderzicht (creditor), and Nimbus goes bankrupt:
The trustee wants to claw back the EUR 320.000 from Polderzicht.
To do so, the trustee must prove that **Polderzicht** knew or should have known that Nimbus was insolvent when it received the payment?
No. If Polderzicht *received* a payment, why would they want to return it? They wouldn't. The trustee sues them to get it back.
The condition for avoidance is that the creditor *did not act in good faith*. But usually, avoidance under Article 42 is about **preferential payments** or **payments made with knowledge of insolvency**.

Actually, the standard rule is: If a debtor pays a creditor shortly before bankruptcy, and that creditor *knew* the debtor was insolvent, the payment can be avoided because it prejudices other creditors. The logic is that the creditor "colluded" or accepted a preference when they should have known better.

So, did Polderzicht Media B.V. know Nimbus was insolvent?
The email from mr. A. Jansen on 12 February 2025 admits cash flow issues.
However, admitting cash flow issues is not necessarily proof of *insolvency* (balance sheet insolvency). But in practice, a request for a grace period due to "temporary cash flow mismatch" is a strong indicator.

If Polderzicht Media B.V. continued to accept the payment on 15 February 2025 without demanding immediate settlement or security, can it be said that they *knew* Nimbus was insolvent?
Given the explicit admission of inability to pay other vendors on 12 February, a reasonable creditor would consider Nimbus insolvent.

Therefore, Polderzicht Media B.V. likely had constructive knowledge.

### 4. Conclusion and Advice

It is highly probable that the bankruptcy trustee will succeed in avoiding the payment of EUR 320.000 to Polderzicht Media B.V. under **Article 42 Bankruptcy Act**. The email from your CFO on 12 February 2025 serves as strong evidence that Polderzicht Media B.V. was aware of Nimbus Interactive B.V.’s financial distress, which a reasonable creditor would equate with insolvency in the context of avoiding preferential treatment in the critical period.

We recommend preparing for a potential claim by the bankruptcy trustee and considering whether Polderzicht Media B.V. can demonstrate that they relied on any guarantee or security provided at the time of payment.
