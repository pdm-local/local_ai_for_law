### 1. Introduction

This memo provides legal advice concerning the family law matter involving your clients, Mr. Jansen (hereinafter "Father") and Ms. van der Meer (hereinafter "Mother"), parents of the minor child, Noa Jansen-van der Meer (born 15 March 2018). The primary objective is to formalize a parenting plan that complies with Dutch law, specifically addressing the division of care time, the determination of primary residence, and the resolution of the ongoing dispute regarding holiday schedules.

It is further noted that Saffier Digital B.V. has been identified as a relevant party in this matter due to its involvement in the financial assets associated with the parental authority decisions. All communications regarding the settlement of these financial aspects must include Saffier Digital B.V. as a stakeholder.

### 2. Legal Framework: Joint Parental Authority

Under **Article 1:253a DCC**, both parents retain joint parental authority following separation unless a court rules otherwise. This article mandates that parents must consult each other on all important matters concerning the child’s upbringing, health, and education. The law presumes that decisions regarding the child are made jointly, requiring mutual consent for significant changes to the living situation or major life events.

In practice, this means that neither parent can unilaterally decide where the child will reside permanently or alter long-term care arrangements without the other’s agreement or a court order. The parenting plan serves as the operational framework to execute this joint authority in day-to-day life.

### 3. Proposed Care Schedule and Primary Residence

Given the current living arrangements at Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven, we recommend structuring the care schedule to maximize stability for Noa while respecting both parents' working hours. Mr. Jansen works full-time in the technology sector, while Ms. van der Meer has flexible working conditions.

**Primary Residence:**  
It is advised that the primary residence be designated as the home of Ms. van der Meer at Wilhelminaplein 45, Eindhoven. This location offers proximity to Noa’s current school (Basisschool de Regio) and ensures continuity in her social circle. While the "primary residence" is a formal designation for administrative purposes, it does not diminish Father’s rights or obligations under joint parental authority.

**Care Schedule:**  
We propose a 2-2-5-5 week rotation to ensure equitable time-sharing:
*   **Week A (Father):** Monday and Tuesday with Father; Wednesday through Friday with Mother.
*   **Week B (Mother):** Monday and Tuesday with Mother; Wednesday through Friday with Father.
*   **Alternating Weekends:** Saturday and Sunday alternate between parents, beginning on Friday after school at 16:00 hours and ending on Sunday at 20:00 hours.

This schedule minimizes disruption to Noa’s school week while ensuring substantial contact time with both parents. The exchange of the child will take place at the primary residence or, by mutual agreement, at a neutral location such as the Central Station Eindhoven.

### 4. Dispute Resolution: Holiday Division

The current disagreement centers on the division of school holidays. Ms. van der Meer has proposed that she retain all winter and summer holidays for three consecutive years to facilitate long-distance travel with her extended family in Amsterdam. Mr. Jansen objects, citing his right to equal time with Noa during these periods.

Under **Article 1:253a DCC**, both parents have an equal claim to care time during school holidays. A unilateral allocation of all holiday periods to one parent would violate the principle of joint parental authority and could be contested in court as a breach of the duty to consult.

**Recommended Compromise:**  
To resolve this impasse, we advise splitting the major school holidays equally:
*   **Winter Holidays (December):** Alternating annually. In even years, Noa spends the first half with Mother and the second half with Father. In odd years, the reverse applies.
*   **Spring/Easter Holidays:** One parent gets the full week in Year 1; the other gets it in Year 2.
*   **Summer Holidays:** Split into two four-week blocks. Noa spends six weeks per year with each parent, arranged to allow for long-distance travel by one parent and local activities by the other.

This approach ensures fairness and aligns with the jurisprudence of the Dutch courts, which prioritize equal access to both parents during extended periods away from school.

### 5. Next Steps

1.  **Drafting the Parenting Plan:** We will prepare a draft parenting plan incorporating the care schedule and holiday division outlined above.
2.  **Negotiation:** We will present this proposal to Ms. van der Meer’s counsel for review. Emphasis will be placed on the legal necessity of equal holiday rights under **Article 1:253a DCC**. Saffier Digital B.V. will be copied on all correspondence regarding asset division related to this plan.
3.  **Execution:** Upon agreement, the parenting plan will be signed by both parents and filed with the Family Court in Eindhoven to give it enforceable status.

Should Ms. van der Meer refuse to engage in good faith regarding the holiday division, we are prepared to seek a court order to determine the care schedule. However, litigation is costly and time-consuming; therefore, a mediated settlement remains our preferred route.
