### 1. Introduction and Purpose

This memo provides legal advice to Saffier Logistics B.V. concerning the criminal proceedings initiated against our client for alleged tax fraud and money laundering under Article 420bis of the Criminal Code. Specifically, we address the procedural irregularities identified during the search warrant execution at your Eindhoven headquarters on 10 February 2025. The primary objective is to argue for the exclusion of key digital evidence obtained in violation of statutory safeguards, thereby invoking a breach of the right to a fair trial guaranteed by Article 6 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR).

### 2. Factual Background

On 10 February 2025, officers from the Fiscal Intelligence and Investigation Service (FIOD) executed a search warrant at Kennedypark 45, Eindhoven. While the warrant was valid regarding specific accounting records for the fiscal year 2023, the executing officers proceeded to seize all servers and backup drives without limiting the scope to the requested data categories. Furthermore, no independent legal counsel was present during the initial data extraction phase, despite our client’s explicit request at 09:15 hours that day. The seized data includes privileged attorney-client communications between Saffier Logistics B.V. and Meridiaan Advocaten B.V., which are directly protected by privilege against self-incrimination and professional secrecy.

### 3. Legal Analysis

#### 3.1. Violation of Procedural Safeguards under Article 6 ECHR

Article 6 ECHR guarantees the right to a fair trial. This includes the principle of equality of arms and the right to adversarial proceedings. The European Court of Human Rights has consistently held that the use of evidence obtained in violation of fundamental procedural rules may render the trial as a whole unfair if such violation affects the rights of the defense.

In this matter, the failure to allow legal counsel to supervise the seizure of data containing privileged information constitutes a severe infringement. By disregarding our client’s right to have an advocate present, the authorities deprived Saffier Logistics B.V. of the opportunity to challenge the relevance and legality of the data extraction in real-time. This lack of oversight is not merely a technical error; it strikes at the heart of the adversarial nature of criminal proceedings protected by Article 6 ECHR.

#### 3.2. The Principle of Proportionality and Legal Certainty

The search exceeded the scope of the warrant, violating the principle of proportionality inherent in the rule of law. The seizure of all infrastructure, rather than only specific files, was disproportionate to the aim of preventing tax evasion. This overreach created a situation where sensitive commercial secrets and privileged legal advice were exposed to the prosecution without any safeguard or minimization procedure.

The consequences of this breach are significant. If the prosecution relies on data extracted during this unlawful phase, the integrity of the entire evidentiary chain is compromised. The defense must argue that the admission of such evidence would render the proceedings unfair under Article 6 ECHR because the defense was prevented from exercising effective control over the collection of evidence against our client.

### 4. Consequences for Exclusion of Evidence

We recommend filing an immediate motion to exclude all evidence derived from the server seizure conducted between 09:15 and 14:00 hours on 10 February 2025. The argument rests on two pillars:

1.  **Privilege Breach:** Any communication protected by legal professional privilege must be excluded immediately. The failure to separate these documents during the seizure violates the core protections of Article 6 ECHR.
2.  **Unlawful Acquisition:** Evidence obtained in flagrant disregard of procedural safeguards cannot be used to substantiate a conviction without rendering the trial unfair.

The Dutch courts have recognized that severe breaches of the rights of the defense can lead to the exclusion of evidence, even if the evidence itself is relevant. Given the clear violation of the right to legal assistance during the critical phase of data collection, the threshold for exclusion is met.

### 5. Recommended Actions

1.  **Formal Objection:** Submit a formal objection to the investigating judge (rechter-commissaris) demanding the return or destruction of all privileged materials and the sealing of the remaining seized data under strict supervision by Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
2.  **Motion for Exclusion:** Prepare a motion for the trial court arguing that the use of any evidence derived from the unlawful seizure violates Article 6 ECHR.
3.  **Preservation of Rights:** Ensure all internal communications regarding this incident are marked "Privileged and Confidential" to prevent further waiver of privilege.

### 6. Conclusion

The actions taken by the FIOD on 10 February 2025 represent a fundamental breach of the procedural guarantees afforded to Saffier Logistics B.V. under Article 6 ECHR. By denying our client legal assistance during the seizure and overstepping the bounds of the warrant, the authorities have compromised the fairness of the proceedings. We must pursue the exclusion of the tainted evidence vigorously to protect our client’s rights and ensure a fair trial.
