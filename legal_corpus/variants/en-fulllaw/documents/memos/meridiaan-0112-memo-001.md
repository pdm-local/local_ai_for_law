**MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Mr. A. Koster
**FROM:** Legal Department
**DATE:** 14 March 2025
**SUBJECT:** Matter 2025.0112 — Brightlane Technologies B.V. vs. Polderzicht Digital B.V.

**1. Factual Background**

Polderzicht Digital B.V. (the "Landlord") has initiated termination proceedings against Brightlane Technologies B.V. ("Tenant") for non-payment of rent amounting to €45,000 covering the period from October 2024 through December 2024. The lease concerns commercial office space located at Kennedyplein 150, Eindhoven.

In its defense against the termination claim based on Article 7:274 DCC, Tenant argues that it has lawfully suspended rent payments due to significant defects in the leased premises. Specifically, Tenant alleges that the HVAC system failed completely in September 2024 and that persistent water infiltration occurred in the server room starting in October 2024, rendering parts of the premises unfit for the agreed commercial purpose. Tenant claims it notified Landlord of these defects immediately but received no adequate remedy within a reasonable timeframe.

**2. Legal Analysis: The Right to Suspend Rent under Article 7:206 DCC**

The core legal question is whether Tenant’s suspension of rent is justified under Article 7:206 DCC of the Dutch Civil Code (DCC). This article governs the lessor's liability for defects in the leased property.

Under established Dutch case law interpreting Article 7:206 DCC, a tenant may suspend rent payments only if the defect substantially impairs the use of the premises for its agreed purpose. The suspension must be proportional to the degree of impairment. A total suspension of rent is rarely justified unless the entire premise is unusable. A partial suspension corresponds to the loss of utility.

In this matter, the Tenant asserts that the server room was affected by water infiltration and the HVAC system failed. For the suspension to be legally valid:
1.  The defects must exist objectively and not be caused by the Tenant’s own actions.
2.  The Tenant must have formally notified the Landlord of the defects without undue delay.
3.  The Landlord must have failed to repair the defects within a reasonable period after notification.

The file indicates that Tenant sent an email on 5 October 2024 detailing the HVAC failure. However, there is no record in the current correspondence chain confirming that Tenant provided formal written notice of the water infiltration until 15 October 2024. The Landlord argues that the delay in reporting the water issue constitutes a waiver of the right to suspend rent for the period prior to notification.

Furthermore, proportionality is key. Even if the server room was temporarily inaccessible, the rest of the office space remained usable. Case law suggests that rent suspension should be limited to the value of the impaired portion (e.g., 20-30% of monthly rent) rather than a full 100% withholding. Tenant’s decision to withhold the entire €45,000 is legally precarious under Article 7:206 DCC because it lacks proportionality unless they can prove total unfitness of the premises, which is contradicted by the fact that operations continued with reduced capacity.

**3. Interaction with Termination Grounds under Article 7:274 DCC**

Article 7:274 DCC allows the landlord to terminate the lease without notice if the tenant fails to perform its obligations, such as paying rent, and continues to do so after being put in default.

If the court finds that Tenant’s suspension was unjustified (either due to lack of proportionality or delayed notification), the arrears remain due. The Landlord has issued a formal notice of default on 15 January 2025 demanding payment within two weeks. Since Tenant has not paid, the ground for termination under Article 7:274 DCC is solidly established.

However, if the court finds the suspension partially justified, the arrears would be recalculated. For instance, if a 30% reduction is applied to the €15,000 monthly rent for the three-month period, the actual debt would be €31,500 rather than €45,000. This still leaves a significant balance of unpaid rent, which likely still justifies termination under Article 7:274 DCC unless the Tenant pays the difference immediately.

**4. Strategic Assessment and Conclusion**

The defense based on defects is weak regarding the full suspension of rent. The lack of proportionality in withholding 100% of the rent creates a high risk that the court will reject the suspension claim entirely, or at best, reduce the debt by a minor percentage. This does not stop the termination proceedings under Article 7:274 DCC.

The Landlord’s position is strong. The Tenant’s reliance on Article 7:206 DCC fails to excuse the primary obligation to pay rent in full unless the premises were entirely uninhabitable, which they were not.

**Recommendation:**
We should proceed with the termination claim under Article 7:274 DCC. In settlement negotiations, we may acknowledge that the defects caused some inconvenience and agree to a slight reduction of the arrears (e.g., €5,000) to expedite the vacating process, but we must firmly reject the Tenant’s argument that they were entitled to withhold all rent payments.
