**SUBJECT:** Liability exposure of Aurora Media B.V. as a hosting intermediary under the Dutch Civil Code and the Digital Services Act

### 1. Introduction

This memo provides legal advice regarding the potential liability of Aurora Media B.V. ("Aurora") for content uploaded by third-party users on its platform, specifically in light of complaints received from Helix Online B.V. ("Helix"). The analysis focuses on the exemption from liability available to hosting service providers under Article 6:196c of the Dutch Civil Code (DCC), which implements the E-Commerce Directive (Directive 2000/31/EC). Additionally, this memo addresses the procedural obligations imposed by Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (the Digital Services Act, or DSA), which applies directly to Aurora as a provider of intermediary services within the European Union.

### 2. The Hosting Exemption under Article 6:196c DCC and the DSA

Aurora Media B.V. operates a platform that allows users to store and display information provided by third parties. For Aurora to benefit from the liability exemption for hosting services, it must meet specific conditions derived from both domestic law and EU regulation.

#### 2.1. Conditions under Article 6:196c DCC (E-Commerce Directive Implementation)
Under Dutch law, implementing the E-Commerce Directive, Aurora is not liable for information stored at the request of a user if it meets three cumulative conditions:

1.  **Nature of the Service:** Aurora acts merely as a passive intermediary. It does not originate the illegal content, nor does it have actual knowledge of facts or circumstances from which the illegal activity is apparent.
2.  **No Knowledge:** Upon obtaining such knowledge or awareness, Aurora acts expeditiously to remove the content or disable access to it.
3.  **No Active Role:** Aurora must not play an active role that gives it knowledge of or control over the data stored. If Aurora optimizes the presentation of the content for commercial gain in a way that implies endorsement or active curation beyond mere technical, automatic, and passive processing, the exemption may be lost.

#### 2.2. Obligations under Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (DSA)
While Article 6:196c DCC provides the liability shield, Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 imposes specific procedural duties on "Very Large Online Platforms" and other hosting providers. Aurora must comply with the DSA’s requirements for notice-and-action mechanisms. Unlike the DCC, which focuses on liability exemption, the DSA mandates transparent procedures for receiving notices from users or authorities regarding illegal content. Failure to adhere to these procedural standards can result in administrative fines under the DSA, independent of civil liability under Dutch tort law.

### 3. Application to Current Facts

Currently, Aurora’s platform functions as a user-generated content repository. The terms of service clearly state that users are responsible for their own uploads. Technical measures employed by Aurora include automated spam filters but do not involve human editorial review of the specific content in question prior to publication. Therefore, at this stage, Aurora likely qualifies as a hosting intermediary under Article 6:196c DCC and is not liable for the initial posting of the infringing material by Helix Online B.V.’s users.

### 4. The Impact of Notice and "Actual Knowledge"

The critical turning point in liability assessment occurs when Aurora receives notice from a third party, such as Helix Online B.V., alleging infringement.

Under Article 6:196c DCC, the exemption ceases to apply if Aurora obtains **actual knowledge** of the illegal activity. Actual knowledge is generally established through a sufficiently precise and substantiated notice from the rights holder or an injured party. Simultaneously, under the DSA, Aurora must acknowledge receipt of such notices and provide a statement of reasons for any decision to remove or keep content up.

#### 4.1. The Notice from Helix Online B.V.

On 10 May 2025, Helix Online B.V. sent a formal takedown notice to Aurora’s designated contact email (legal@auroramedia.nl). The notice identified:
*   The specific URLs containing the allegedly infringing content;
*   A clear explanation of why the content violates Helix’s intellectual property rights;
*   Contact details for verification.

Upon receipt of this notice, Aurora’s legal obligation shifts from passive hosting to active monitoring of that specific instance. If the notice meets the threshold of providing sufficient detail to allow Aurora to identify the illegal nature of the content without extensive investigation, Aurora is deemed to have actual knowledge under Dutch law and has triggered its procedural obligations under the DSA.

#### 4.2. Expeditious Action Requirement

Once actual knowledge is established via the notice from Helix Online B.V., Aurora must act **expeditiously** to remove the content or disable access to it. "Expeditiously" is a factual standard that depends on the complexity of the case and the technical feasibility of removal. It does not require immediate removal within minutes, but it does preclude unreasonable delays (e.g., waiting weeks for internal review if no complex legal analysis is required).

If Aurora fails to remove the content within a reasonable timeframe after receiving the notice from Helix Online B.V., it will lose the liability protection afforded by Article 6:196c DCC. In such a scenario, Aurora may be held jointly liable for damages suffered by Helix Online B.V. resulting from the continued availability of the infringing material. Additionally, non-compliance with DSA notice-and-action timelines could lead to regulatory scrutiny.

### 5. Recommendations and Immediate Actions

To maintain the hosting exemption under Article 6:196c DCC and comply with Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, Aurora Media B.V. should take the following steps immediately:

1.  **Acknowledge Receipt:** Confirm receipt of Helix Online B.V.’s notice dated 10 May 2025 within 24 hours to demonstrate good faith and procedural compliance with DSA requirements.
2.  **Preliminary Assessment:** Conduct a rapid internal review to determine if the content is clearly illegal on its face. Given that the notice from Helix Online B.V. is sufficiently detailed, there is no need for extensive legal investigation before acting.
3.  **Temporary Suspension or Removal:** To mitigate risk, consider temporarily suspending access to the specific URLs identified by Helix Online B.V. pending a final decision. This aligns with the "expeditious action" requirement of Article 6:196c DCC and the DSA’s obligation to act on clearly illegal content.
4.  **Documentation:** Preserve all records of the notice, the internal review process, and the actions taken. This documentation is crucial to prove that Aurora did not have prior actual knowledge and acted promptly upon notification.

### 6. Conclusion

Aurora Media B.V. is currently protected from liability as a hosting intermediary under Article 6:196c DCC, provided it remains passive and lacks actual knowledge of the specific infringement. However, the receipt of the detailed notice from Helix Online B.V. on 10 May 2025 establishes actual knowledge. Aurora must act expeditiously to remove or disable access to the infringing content to retain this exemption. Failure to do so will expose Aurora to direct liability for damages under general tort law principles (Article 6:162 DCC).

We recommend proceeding with the immediate removal of the identified content to avoid any potential claim for injunctive relief or damages from Helix Online B.V., and to ensure compliance with both Dutch civil law and EU regulatory standards.
