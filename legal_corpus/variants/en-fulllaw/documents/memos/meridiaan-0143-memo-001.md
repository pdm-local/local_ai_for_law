**PRIVILEGED LEGAL MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Mr. D. van Raay, Insolvency Law Specialist
**From:** Internal File – Matter 2025.0143
**Date:** May 12, 2025
**Subject:** Analysis of *Kennis* (Knowledge) for Avoidance under Article 42 Bankruptcy Act

### 1. Introduction and Background

This memorandum provides a legal assessment regarding the potential avoidance of a transaction entered into by Duinrand Retail B.V. ("the Debtor") in favor of its affiliated entity, Duinrand Data B.V. ("the Creditor"). The central issue is whether the payment made by the Debtor can be avoided by the bankruptcy trustee under Article 42 of the Bankruptcy Act (Faillissementswet), specifically focusing on the subjective element of knowledge (*kennis*).

The factual matrix is as follows:
*   **Payer:** Duinrand Retail B.V.
*   **Payee:** Duinrand Data B.V.
*   **Payment Date:** January 15, 2025
*   **Amount:** € 180.000
*   **Context:** The payment was made shortly before the Debtor’s declaration of bankruptcy on February 1, 2025.

The relevant statutory provision is Article 42 of the Bankruptcy Act. This article allows for the avoidance of legal acts performed by the bankrupt party if the counterparty knew or ought to have known at the time of the act that the performance of the obligation would prejudice the creditors.

### 2. Legal Framework: Article 42 Bankruptcy Act

Article 42 of the Bankruptcy Act establishes a subjective ground for avoidance. For a transaction to be voidable under this article, two cumulative requirements must be met:
1.  The act was performed within the suspect period (the three months preceding bankruptcy, or one year if between connected parties).
2.  The counterparty had **knowledge** (*kennis*) that the act would prejudice the creditors.

Case law from the Dutch Supreme Court (Hoge Raad) interprets *kennis* broadly. It includes not only actual knowledge but also "willful blindness" (*voorzets wetens*). If a party is aware of circumstances that strongly suggest insolvency or prejudice to creditors and deliberately refrains from investigating, this may constitute constructive knowledge sufficient for avoidance.

### 3. Application to the Facts

We must determine whether Duinrand Data B.V. knew or ought to have known that the payment of € 180.000 would prejudice the Debtor's creditors.

#### 3.1 Financial State of Duinrand Retail B.V.
Internal emails retrieved from the Debtor’s server indicate that by December 2024, Duinrand Retail B.V. was already experiencing severe liquidity shortages. Specifically, an email dated December 10, 2024, from the CFO stated: "Cash flow is negative; we cannot cover payroll in January without immediate capital injection." Despite this, a payment of € 180.000 was executed on January 15, 2025.

#### 3.2 Relationship and Information Access
Duinrand Data B.V. is a wholly-owned subsidiary of the same holding company as Duinrand Retail B.V. As such, Duinrand Data B.V. had access to consolidated financial reports. The consolidated balance sheet for Q4 2024 clearly showed that Duinrand Retail B.V. was technically insolvent (liabilities exceeding assets) and unable to meet due debts.

Given the corporate structure, it is highly probable that the management of Duinrand Data B.V. was aware of the financial distress of its sister company. The payment of € 180.000 occurred while Duinrand Retail B.V. was clearly unable to pay its other creditors in full (as evidenced by subsequent non-payment to suppliers like Van der Meer Logistics BV).

#### 3.3 Assessment of Knowledge
Did Duinrand Data B.V. know that this payment would prejudice creditors?
*   **Actual Knowledge:** It is likely that senior management of Duinrand Data B.V. received monthly group financial updates. These updates highlighted the liquidity crisis of Duinrand Retail B.V.
*   **Constructive Knowledge:** Even if actual knowledge is denied, a reasonable manager in the position of Duinrand Data B.V., aware of the group's financial reporting, ought to have known that transferring € 180.000 from an insolvent entity would disadvantage other creditors.

The timing is critical. The payment was made only two weeks before bankruptcy. Under Article 42 Bankruptcy Act, the burden shifts to the counterparty to prove they did not know of the prejudice. Given the proximity in time and the obvious financial deterioration, it is difficult to argue that Duinrand Data B.V. lacked knowledge.

### 4. Conclusion

Based on the available facts, there is a strong presumption that Duinrand Data B.V. knew or ought to have known that the payment of € 180.000 would prejudice the creditors of Duinrand Retail B.V. The financial distress was evident in internal communications and consolidated accounts prior to the transfer.

Therefore, the transaction falls within the scope of **Article 42 Bankruptcy Act** and is likely voidable. We recommend initiating avoidance proceedings to recover the € 180.000 for the bankruptcy estate immediately.
