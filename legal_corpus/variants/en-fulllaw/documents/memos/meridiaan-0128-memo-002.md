# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Mr. P. Slootweg, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V., Eindhoven
**From:** Kempen Networks B.V., Kennedyplein 200, 5611 EZ Eindhoven
**Date:** 14 March 2025
**Subject:** Legal Strategy Regarding Administrative Penalty Case No. 2025-EIN-9942

## 1. Facts of the Matter

On 2 February 2025, the Municipality of Eindhoven issued a formal notice of violation against Kempen Networks B.V. concerning our recent installation of fiber-optic infrastructure in the Strijp-S district. The Municipality alleges that we failed to submit the mandatory "start-of-work" notification via their digital portal within the strict 24-hour window prescribed by Municipal Bylaw 2024/15, Section 8.3.

Our internal logs confirm that the notification was uploaded to the server at 23:45 on the day of commencement, but due to a temporary gateway error on the Municipality’s end, the confirmation receipt was not generated until 06:12 the following morning—exceeding the deadline by approximately 7 hours and 15 minutes. No construction activities commenced prior to this delay, and no public order or safety was jeopardized.

Despite our immediate corrective action and explanation regarding the technical glitch, the Municipality has imposed an administrative fine of €4,500. The notice cites repeated non-compliance with procedural obligations as the basis for this sanction.

## 2. Legal Issue

The central legal question is whether the imposition of a €4,500 administrative fine for a minor, technically caused delay in a purely procedural notification constitutes a disproportionate measure under Dutch administrative law, specifically regarding the principle of proportionality enshrined in **Article 3:4 GALA (Awb)**.

## 3. Analysis

### 3.1 The Principle of Proportionality under Article 3:4 GALA (Awb)

**Article 3:4 GALA (Awb)** mandates that the authorities shall not impose sanctions that are disproportionate to the seriousness of the breach. This principle requires a balancing test between the public interest served by the regulation and the individual burden placed on the offender. The sanction must be suitable, necessary, and proportionate *stricto sensu* (in the narrow sense).

In our case, the breach is purely formal. There was no substantive violation of safety standards, environmental regulations, or zoning laws. The core purpose of the notification requirement is to allow municipal inspectors to monitor active construction sites for safety compliance. Since our site was inactive during the 7-hour delay, and we had already secured all necessary permits, the regulatory interest that the penalty aims to protect was not actively undermined by our omission.

### 3.2 Disproportionality of the Sanction

The amount of €4,500 represents a significant financial burden for a procedural lapse that incurred no actual damage or risk to public safety. Under established jurisprudence, administrative fines must reflect the gravity of the conduct. The Municipality has treated a technical glitch in their own digital infrastructure with the same severity as intentional non-compliance or serious safety violations.

This fails the necessity test: less intrusive measures, such as a formal warning or a reduced nominal fine (e.g., €250–€500), would have sufficed to emphasize the importance of timely notifications without imposing a punitive burden that far exceeds the objective of the rule.

Furthermore, the penalty ignores our clean compliance record over the past five years and our immediate cooperation in resolving the technical issue. To impose a heavy financial sanction here lacks the requisite fairness and reasonableness demanded by **Article 3:4 GALA (Awb)**.

## 4. Conclusion and Recommendation

It is our legal opinion that the current administrative fine is disproportionate and therefore unlawful under **Article 3:4 GALA (Awb)**. We recommend filing a formal objection to the Municipality of Eindhoven within the statutory period, explicitly challenging the penalty on grounds of disproportionality.

We should argue that the sanction violates the principle of good governance and requests a reduction of the fine to a nominal amount or its complete withdrawal, citing the lack of substantive harm and the technical nature of the breach.

The following is critical to our argument:
— The delay was caused by the Municipality’s own system latency, not negligence on our part.
— No public safety risk existed during the period of non-compliance.
— The financial impact of the fine is punitive rather than corrective.

We await your instructions to draft the formal objection letter.
