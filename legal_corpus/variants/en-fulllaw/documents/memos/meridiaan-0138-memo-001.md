### 1. Introduction and Facts

We refer to your request for urgent legal advice regarding a specific financial transaction that has triggered internal compliance alarms at Orion Systems B.V. On 10 May 2025, Orion Systems B.V. received a wire transfer from its corporate account holder, located at Kennedyplein 200 in Eindhoven, Netherlands.

The details of the transaction are as follows:
*   **Sender:** Vertex Holdings Ltd., registered in Cyprus.
*   **Amount:** € 31.000 (thirty-one thousand euros).
*   **Purpose/Reference:** "Consulting services Q2 2025".
*   **Origin of Funds:** The funds were transferred from the bank account of Vertex Holdings Ltd., which itself received these funds approximately 48 hours earlier from an individual, Mr. A. Volkov, via a series of rapid internal transfers across multiple jurisdictions before arriving at the Cypriot entity.

Our compliance department noted that the reference "Consulting services Q2 2025" does not match any signed service agreement between Orion Systems B.V. and Vertex Holdings Ltd. Furthermore, the speed of the fund movement prior to arrival raises concerns regarding the layering technique often associated with illicit finance activities.

### 2. Legal Framework: Article 420bis Criminal Code

The primary legal provision under consideration is **Article 420bis Criminal Code** (Wetboek van Strafrecht). This article criminalizes money laundering (*witwassen*).

Under Dutch law, money laundering is defined as performing one or more of the following acts with respect to proceeds that are derived from a prior offense:
1.  Transferring or moving property;
2.  Concealing or disguising the true nature, source, location, disposition, movement, or ownership of such property; or
3.  Acquiring, possessing, or using such property.

Crucially, Article 420bis does not require proof that the defendant knew for certain that the funds were criminal proceeds. The threshold is **reasonable suspicion** (*redelijke verdenking*). It is sufficient if the perpetrator knows or should reasonably suspect that the property originates from a prior offense.

The "prior offense" can be any criminal act punishable by imprisonment of at least four years (or three years in certain specific cases defined by law, though generally the four-year threshold applies for full liability). Given the volume and routing of the funds, we must assess if the underlying activity generating these funds could constitute such a prior offense.

### 3. Analysis of the Origin of Funds

#### 3.1 The Reasonable Suspicion Standard

The key question is whether Orion Systems B.V., or more specifically its management who authorized or received the payment, had grounds to suspect that the € 31.000 originated from criminal activity.

Factors indicating a "red flag" under anti-money laundering (AML) guidelines include:
*   **Lack of Commercial Logic:** The payment was made by Vertex Holdings Ltd., an entity in Cyprus, for "consulting services" where no prior contract exists. This is inconsistent with standard business practices for Dutch SMEs.
*   **Rapid Movement of Funds:** The funds were routed through multiple jurisdictions shortly before arrival, a classic layering technique used to obscure the money trail.
*   **High-Risk Jurisdiction/Structure:** While Cyprus is an EU member, complex corporate structures involving rapid cross-border transfers from individuals often warrant enhanced due diligence.

#### 3.2 Application to Orion Systems B.V.

If the management of Orion Systems B.V. accepted the € 31.000 without requesting proof of the underlying transaction or verifying the source of funds, they may be deemed to have "used" proceeds with a reasonable suspicion that they were illicit.

It is not necessary that the funds are definitively proven to be from crime (e.g., drug trafficking or fraud) for Article 420bis to apply; it is sufficient that there are objective circumstances that would lead a reasonable person in Orion Systems' position to suspect criminal origin. The absence of a contract and the opaque funding chain create such objective circumstances.

### 4. Assessment of Liability

#### 4.1 Corporate vs. Personal Liability

Under Dutch law, legal persons (B.V.) can be held liable for offenses committed by their directors or employees within the scope of their duties, provided the offense was committed to benefit the company. However, Article 420bis is often prosecuted against natural persons who knowingly process illicit funds.

If the funds were deposited into the corporate account and used for business expenses without inquiry, the director(s) who authorized this use could face personal criminal liability. The company itself may also face administrative sanctions under the Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing Act (Wwft).

#### 4.2 Defenses

Possible defenses include:
*   **Lack of Knowledge/Suspicion:** Demonstrating that all reasonable due diligence was performed and no red flags were apparent to a prudent manager. However, the lack of a contract makes this defense weak.
*   **Reporting Obligation Fulfillment:** If Orion Systems B.V. is a reporting institution under Wwft (which many financial intermediaries are), failing to report a suspicious transaction to the Financial Intelligence Unit Netherlands (FIU-Netherlands) is itself a criminal offense. As a general company, while not strictly a "reporting institution" in the same sense as a bank, directors have a duty to avoid facilitating crime.

### 5. Recommendations and Next Steps

To mitigate legal risk for Orion Systems B.V. and its directors, we recommend the following immediate actions:

1.  **Freeze the Funds:** Do not spend or distribute the € 31.000 until this matter is resolved. Using the funds could be interpreted as "using" proceeds of crime.
2.  **Request Documentation:** Immediately contact Vertex Holdings Ltd. and demand:
    *   The signed service agreement justifying the € 31.000 payment.
    *   Proof of source of funds for Vertex Holdings Ltd.
    *   The identity of the ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) who initiated the transfer.
3.  **File a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR):** If the response is unsatisfactory or delayed, we must advise filing an STR with FIU-Netherlands. This demonstrates good faith and compliance with Dutch AML obligations.
4.  **Return of Funds:** If the source cannot be verified within 5 business days, consider returning the € 31.000 to the originating account via the same channel, documenting this clearly as a refusal to accept unverified funds due to compliance concerns.

### 6. Conclusion

The receipt of € 31.000 from Vertex Holdings Ltd. without a supporting contract and with an opaque funding history creates a reasonable suspicion that the funds may originate from a prior offense. Under Article 420bis Criminal Code, using such funds carries significant criminal risk for the directors of Orion Systems B.V.

We strongly advise against spending these funds and recommend initiating immediate due diligence or returning the funds to avoid any implication of complicity in money laundering.
