### 1. Introduction

This memorandum is prepared for Helix Media B.V. (the “Client”) regarding the ongoing dispute with Saffier Digital B.V. (“Counterparty”). The central issue concerns the determination of monthly maintenance payments due to the former spouse and minor children of the Counterparty’s director, following the dissolution of their marital community.

This advice is provided in confidence and constitutes privileged work product. It outlines the legal framework governing spousal maintenance and child maintenance under Dutch law, specifically addressing the amounts currently claimed by the Counterparty.

### 2. Applicable Legal Framework

#### 2.1 Spousal Maintenance (Article 1:157 DCC)

The obligation to provide spousal maintenance is governed by **Article 1:157 DCC**. Under this provision, each spouse has a duty to contribute to the household according to their own means and capabilities. Upon divorce or legal separation, the court may award alimony if one party lacks sufficient means of subsistence and the other party has the capacity to pay.

The key factors in determining the amount are:
1.  **Need:** The financial requirements of the claimant spouse, considering their standard of living during the marriage and their ability to become self-sufficient.
2.  **Capacity:** The financial means of the paying spouse, taking into account their own reasonable living expenses and obligations.

#### 2.2 Child Maintenance (Article 1:404 DCC)

The obligation to provide child maintenance is governed by **Article 1:404 DCC**. Both parents are jointly liable for the costs of raising and educating their children. The court determines the amount based on the needs of the child and the financial capacity of both parents. The standard reference for such calculations is often the *Richtlijnen Kinderbijslag* (Child Support Guidelines), which provide a baseline formula derived from parental income and the child’s age.

### 3. Analysis of Claimed Amounts

Saffier Digital B.V. has submitted an invoice claiming maintenance payments based on the following figures:
1.  **Spousal Maintenance:** € 3.100 per month.
2.  **Child Maintenance:** € 390 per child per month.

We have reviewed these figures against the statutory requirements and current case law.

#### 3.1 Spousal Maintenance Assessment

The claimed amount of **€ 3.100** per month is substantial. Under **Article 1:157 DCC**, spousal maintenance is generally intended to bridge a gap in income during a transition period, particularly if one spouse was financially dependent on the other during the marriage. However, Dutch courts typically cap spousal alimony at a percentage of the payer’s net income (often between 20% and 30%) unless exceptional circumstances justify a higher amount.

Our review of Saffier Digital B.V.’s financial statements indicates that while they have significant revenue, their operational costs are also high. The claimant spouse has recently secured new employment with a monthly gross salary of € 4,500. Consequently, the actual *need* for spousal maintenance has diminished significantly. We advise challenging the full € 3.100 figure as disproportionate to the current need and capacity dynamics. A more defensible range would be between € 800 and € 1,200 per month, subject to further forensic accounting of Saffier Digital B.V.’s profit distribution capabilities.

#### 3.2 Child Maintenance Assessment

The claimed amount of **€ 390** per child is calculated in line with the standard *Richtlijnen Kinderbijslag*. According to **Article 1:404 DCC**, this guideline is the primary starting point for judicial decisions. For a child aged 12–17, the standard contribution from the non-custodial parent’s side, based on joint parental income, often falls within the € 350–€ 450 range.

Given that Saffier Digital B.V.’s director has a documented net monthly income of approximately € 12,000, and the claimant spouse earns € 4,500 gross (approx. € 3,600 net), the combined household income supports a maintenance obligation near the guideline amount. Therefore, the figure of **€ 390** per child is legally defensible and likely to be upheld by the court if contested. However, we must verify that Saffier Digital B.V. is not artificially inflating the director’s taxable profit to reduce apparent capacity.

### 4. Strategic Recommendations

1.  **Negotiation on Spousal Maintenance:** Initiate settlement talks regarding the spousal maintenance. Argue that the claimant spouse has achieved financial independence through new employment, rendering the € 3.100 claim excessive under **Article 1:157 DCC**. Propose a reduced amount of € 1,000 per month for a limited period (e.g., 12 months) to facilitate transition.
2.  **Acceptance of Child Maintenance:** The child maintenance amount of **€ 390** is consistent with statutory guidelines under **Article 1:404 DCC**. We recommend accepting this figure to avoid unnecessary litigation costs and court delays.
3.  **Documentation:** Request full disclosure of Saffier Digital B.V.’s last three fiscal years’ financial statements to accurately assess the paying capacity for spousal maintenance purposes.

### 5. Conclusion

The claim for child maintenance is robust under Dutch law. The claim for spousal maintenance appears inflated relative to the claimant’s current earning capacity. We advise a strategy of partial acceptance (child support) and aggressive negotiation (spousal support) to minimize Helix Media B.V.’s financial exposure while remaining compliant with **Article 1:157 DCC** and **Article 1:404 DCC**.
