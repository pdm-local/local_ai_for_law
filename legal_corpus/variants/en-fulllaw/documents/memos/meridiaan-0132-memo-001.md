**Re:** Draft Service Agreement with Hanze Networks B.V. – Liability Clause Analysis

### 1. Introduction

We are instructed by Lumen Digital B.V. (“Lumen”) to review the draft Service Agreement currently under negotiation with Hanze Networks B.V. (“Hanze”). The primary focus of this memo is Article 12 (Limitation of Liability), which contains three distinct caps on liability. Our objective is to assess the legal enforceability and commercial implications of these caps, particularly regarding their internal consistency and compliance with the standards of reasonableness and fairness under Dutch law.

### 2. Analysis of Liability Caps

The draft contract proposes the following limitations on Lumen’s aggregate liability:

1.  **Cap A:** An amount equal to the **annual fee** paid by Hanze for the services in question.
2.  **Cap B:** An amount covering damages incurred during a period of **twelve months**.
3.  **Cap C:** A fixed absolute maximum liability sum of **€ 25.000** (twenty-five thousand euros).

#### 2.1 Conflict Between Cap A and Cap B

There is a significant structural conflict between the first two proposed caps. Cap A ties liability to the financial value of the contract (the annual fee), while Cap B ties it to a temporal duration (twelve months). In practice, these metrics may diverge substantially depending on the pricing model.

If Lumen charges a subscription-based fee, the "annual fee" might be € 50.000, whereas the damages accrued within any given "twelve month" period could theoretically exceed this if calculated against total project value rather than revenue. Conversely, if the contract is short-term, the "twelve months" cap might effectively allow for higher liability than the limited annual fee. This ambiguity creates legal uncertainty regarding which cap prevails in a dispute. We strongly recommend selecting one primary metric rather than maintaining two potentially contradictory standards.

#### 2.2 The Fixed Maximum Cap (€ 25.000)

The third cap establishes an absolute ceiling of **€ 25.000** regardless of the fee or time period. This is a common commercial practice in B2B contracts to limit exposure to catastrophic loss. However, under Dutch law, such caps are not automatically enforceable if they violate standards of reasonableness and fairness (*redelijkheid en billijkheid*).

### 3. Reasonableness and Fairness (Article 6:248 DCC)

According to **Article 6:248 DCC**, a term in standard terms or a unilateral act may be deemed unacceptable if it is so unfair that, under the given circumstances, it cannot await acceptance by the other party. Furthermore, general provisions of law require that contractual limitations of liability must not exclude liability for intentional misconduct (*opzet*) or gross negligence (*grove schuld*).

In our assessment:
*   The fixed sum of **€ 25.000** is generally acceptable for standard software development and hosting services, provided it reflects the contract value.
*   If the **annual fee** exceeds € 25.000 significantly (e.g., € 100.000), a cap limited to € 25.000 might be challenged as unreasonable for gross negligence, as it would disproportionately shift risk to Lumen.
*   Conversely, if the **annual fee** is lower than € 25.000, the fixed cap becomes irrelevant and should be replaced by the annual fee limit to ensure clarity.

### 4. Recommendations

To resolve the conflicts identified above, we propose the following revisions to Article 12:

1.  **Delete Cap B (Twelve Months):** Remove the reference to "twelve months" as a primary metric to eliminate ambiguity.
2.  **Align Caps:** Set the liability cap at the lower of:
    *   The total fees paid by Hanze in the **twelve months** preceding the claim; OR
    *   A fixed maximum of **€ 25.000**.
3.  **Explicit Exclusions:** Clearly state that these caps do not apply to liability arising from intentional misconduct, gross negligence, or breach of confidentiality obligations.

### 5. Conclusion

The current draft presents a high risk of dispute due to the conflicting definitions of liability limits. By harmonizing the temporal and financial metrics and ensuring the fixed cap aligns with the **annual fee**, Lumen can provide Hanze with a clear, enforceable, and commercially reasonable limitation of liability clause.

We await your instructions on how to proceed with the redline of this article.
