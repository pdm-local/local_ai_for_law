**MEMORANDUM**

**SUBJECT:** Urgent Defence Strategy: Motion to Exclude Illegally Obtained Evidence

**1. Introduction and Background**

This memorandum addresses the critical procedural defects in the investigation conducted by the Amsterdam Regional Police regarding the alleged financial irregularities at Orion Systems B.V. The prosecution intends to rely heavily on server logs extracted from our client’s primary data centre on 4 February 2025, without a valid judicial warrant or immediate cause for urgency as required by law.

We must immediately file a motion to exclude this evidence. The foundation of our defence rests on the assertion that the investigative methods employed violated fundamental procedural guarantees, directly infringing upon Article 6 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR). —

**2. Legal Framework: Article 6 ECHR and Fair Trial Guarantees**

Article 6 ECHR guarantees the right to a fair trial. The European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) has consistently held that this right implies not only the fairness of the proceedings as a whole but also the equality of arms between the prosecution and the defence.

In matters concerning the admissibility of evidence, Dutch law grants judges broad discretion; however, this discretion is bounded by the requirements of Article 6 ECHR. The use of evidence obtained in violation of the law may render the trial unfair if it deprives the defence of a meaningful opportunity to challenge the prosecution’s case or if the reliance on such evidence undermines the overall fairness of the proceedings.

Key principles include:
*   **Equality of Arms:** Both parties must have the same procedural opportunities to present their case under conditions that do not place one at a substantial disadvantage vis-à-vis the other.
*   **Adversarial Proceedings:** The defence must be given the opportunity to have knowledge of and comment on all evidence adduced or observations filed by the prosecution.

**3. Analysis of Procedural Violations**

The seizure of the server logs from Orion Systems B.V. occurred at 09:15 hours on 4 February 2025. The executing officers lacked a specific search warrant authorizing the extraction of digital forensic data. They relied instead on a general investigative order which, according to case law regarding digital evidence, is insufficient for the deep forensic analysis performed here.

Furthermore, the chain of custody for these digital files was compromised. No independent technical expert was present during the extraction, nor were cryptographic hashes verified at the source. This lack of integrity checks means we cannot verify that the data has not been altered or tampered with prior to its submission as evidence.

By allowing the prosecution to rely on this tainted evidence without rigorous scrutiny, the court would be facilitating a violation of Article 6 ECHR. The defence is effectively barred from challenging the authenticity of the logs because they were never provided in their raw, unaltered state at the time of seizure.

**4. Consequences for Exclusion of Evidence**

Under Dutch criminal procedure law, evidence obtained through serious violations of mandatory rules must be excluded if its admission would violate the requirements of a fair trial under Article 6 ECHR. Given that:
1.  The search exceeded the scope of the authorizing instrument;
2.  The integrity of the digital evidence cannot be guaranteed; and
3.  The violation prejudices the defence’s ability to prepare an adequate counter-argument;

We must argue for the automatic exclusion of all server logs and any derivative reports generated from them. Failure to exclude this evidence will constitute a reversible error in the assessment of fairness under Article 6 ECHR.

**5. Recommended Next Steps**

1.  Draft and file a formal motion to exclude evidence pursuant to the principles derived from Article 6 ECHR immediately.
2.  Commission an independent forensic audit of the prosecution’s data handling logs to substantiate claims of potential tampering.
3.  Request the court to hold a separate hearing on admissibility before the trial on the merits begins, ensuring that the jury (if applicable) or judges are not exposed to prejudicial information derived from the illicit seizure.

We await your instructions to proceed with these filings.
