**MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Management Board, Saffier Software B.V.
**From:** mr. F. Lindeman, Tax Law Department
**Date:** April 12, 2025
**Subject:** Analysis of Corporate Income Tax Correction and Substantial Interest Implications

### 1. Introduction and Background

Saffier Software B.V. (hereinafter: "the Company") recently requested legal advice regarding the Dutch Tax Administration’s preliminary assessment for the fiscal year ending December 31, 2024. The Tax Administration has proposed a correction to the taxable profit of the Company by adjusting the valuation of intangible assets arising from research and development activities. This adjustment triggers a re-evaluation of the Company’s financial position for the substantial-interest holder (hereinafter: "the Shareholder"), who holds 100% of the issued shares.

This memorandum outlines the legal framework governing this correction under the Corporate Income Tax Act 1969 (Wet op de vennootschapsbelasting 1969) and analyzes the consequent tax liability for the Shareholder under the Income Tax Act 2001 (Wet inkomstenbelasting 2001).

### 2. Corporate Income Tax: Article 8 Corporate Income Tax Act 1969

The core dispute concerns the applicability of the innovation box regime, which relies on the qualification of profits as "qualifying profits" under **Article 8 Corporate Income Tax Act 1969**.

#### 2.1 Legal Standard
Under **Article 8 Corporate Income Tax Act 1969**, qualifying profits are those attributable to self-developed intangible assets that meet specific statutory requirements regarding research and development (R&D) activities. The profit is calculated by determining the excess return over a routine return on tangible assets used in the R&D process.

#### 2.2 Application to Saffier Software
The Tax Administration argues that the Company failed to sufficiently document the causal link between its R&D expenditures and the resulting intangible assets for the fiscal year 2024. Specifically, they contend that:
1. The technical progress achieved does not meet the threshold of "new knowledge" required for qualification.
2. The allocation of costs to the specific patent applications was arbitrary.

Consequently, the Tax Administration has disallowed the deduction under **Article 8 Corporate Income Tax Act 1969** for the amount of €450,000. This results in an increase in the Company’s taxable profit by the same amount. The applicable corporate tax rate of 25% yields an additional tax liability of €112,500 for the Company.

#### 2.3 Legal Assessment
We assess that the Tax Administration’s position is legally tenable regarding the documentation standards. However, we identify grounds for appeal based on the principle of legal certainty and the consistency of past rulings granted to Saffier Software in fiscal years 2022 and 2023. We recommend filing a formal objection against the assessment, citing the continuity of the Company’s R&D methodology.

### 3. Substantial Interest Holder: Article 3.8 Income Tax Act 2001

The correction to the Company’s taxable profit directly impacts the Shareholder’s tax position, given that the Shareholder holds a substantial interest (defined as holding at least 5% of the shares) in Saffier Software B.V.

#### 3.1 Legal Standard
Under **Article 3.8 Income Tax Act 2001**, the income from a substantial interest is calculated based on the fair market value of the shares at the balance sheet date (the "day after the last day of the fiscal year"). The change in fair market value during the fiscal year constitutes the taxable income or loss for the Shareholder.

The fair market value of the shares is intrinsically linked to the net asset value of the Company, which includes its equity and retained earnings. Any increase in the Company’s taxable profit (or decrease in retained earnings) due to a tax correction will typically reduce the fair market value of the shares, thereby reducing the Shareholder’s taxable income from the substantial interest.

#### 3.2 Calculation of Impact
1. **Increase in Corporate Tax Liability:** As noted in Section 2.2, the Company faces an additional tax liability of €112,500 due to the disallowance under **Article 8 Corporate Income Tax Act 1969**.
2. **Reduction in Equity:** This additional liability reduces the Company’s net assets (equity) by €112,500.
3. **Effect on Share Value:** Assuming no other changes in asset valuation, the fair market value of the shares held by the Shareholder decreases by €112,500.
4. **Taxable Income for Shareholder:** Under **Article 3.8 Income Tax Act 2001**, this decrease in share value constitutes a negative change in fair market value. This results in a deductible loss of €112,500 for the Shareholder in their personal income tax return (Box 2).

#### 3.3 Net Effect
While the Company incurs a cash outflow of €112,500 in corporate taxes, the Shareholder benefits from a corresponding reduction in taxable income from the substantial interest. The effective marginal tax rate for the Shareholder on this specific loss depends on their personal tax bracket (typically 36% or 49.5% depending on total income). This creates a partial mitigation of the overall group tax burden, but it does not eliminate the cash flow impact on the Company.

### 4. Strategic Recommendations

1. **File Objection:** Immediately file an objection against the corporate income tax assessment for fiscal year 2024, focusing on the documentation of R&D activities and the consistency of the innovation box application.
2. **Document Share Valuation:** Ensure that the decrease in share value due to the tax liability is accurately reflected in the Shareholder’s annual financial statements and tax return under **Article 3.8 Income Tax Act 2001**.
3. **Monitor Cash Flow:** The Company must prepare for the cash payment of the additional €112,500 if the objection is not settled favorably. Consider requesting a deferral of payment pending the outcome of the objection procedure to preserve liquidity.

### 5. Conclusion

The Tax Administration’s correction under **Article 8 Corporate Income Tax Act 1969** is legally significant and results in an additional corporate tax liability for Saffier Software B.V. This correction triggers a corresponding reduction in the fair market value of the shares, resulting in a deductible loss for the substantial-interest holder under **Article 3.8 Income Tax Act 2001**. We strongly advise proceeding with the objection procedure to challenge the qualification of the R&D activities.
