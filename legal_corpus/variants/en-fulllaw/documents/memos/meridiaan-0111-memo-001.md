**CONFIDENTIAL LEGAL MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Saffier Software B.V., Mr. J. van der Meer, Managing Director
**From:** mr. F. Lindeman, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**Date:** April 28, 2025
**Subject:** Assessment of Validity of Additional Tax Assessment dated March 15, 2025

### 1. Introduction

You have instructed Meridiaan Advocaten B.V. to assess the legality of the additional tax assessment issued by the Dutch Tax Administration (Belastingdienst) on March 15, 2025. This assessment imposes a supplementary corporate income tax liability of € 460.000 for the fiscal year 2023. The Tax Administration justifies this reassessment by claiming that new facts have come to light which were not available during the original filing period and which materially alter your taxable profit.

The central legal question is whether the requirement of a "new fact" (*nieuw feit*) under Article 16 of the State Taxes Act (AwR) is satisfied. If this requirement is not met, the assessment is legally voidable and should be annulled.

### 2. Relevant Legal Framework

The primary statutory provision governing the issuance of additional assessments on the basis of new information is **Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act)**. This article stipulates that the tax authority may issue an additional assessment if a "new fact" or "new circumstance" has occurred that was unknown to both the taxpayer and the authority at the time the original return was filed, and which leads to a different determination of the tax liability.

The definition of "new fact" is strictly construed by Dutch case law (particularly rulings from the Hoge Raad). A fact is considered "new" only if:
1. It existed at the time of the original assessment but was not known to the taxpayer or the authority; OR
2. It arose after the filing of the return but relates back to the taxable period in a way that creates a new legal basis for taxation.

Crucially, facts that were already known to the taxpayer but simply omitted from the return do not qualify as "new." Furthermore, mere reinterpretations of existing facts by the tax inspector do not constitute a new fact. The burden of proof lies with the Tax Administration to demonstrate that the information constitutes a genuinely new fact under Article 16 AWR.

### 3. Analysis of the Current Case

In your matter, the Dutch Tax Administration has relied on internal emails from your former financial controller, Ms. A. de Vries, who left Saffier Software B.V. in November 2024. The inspector argues that these emails, discovered during a random audit initiated in January 2025, reveal that certain R&D expenditures were incorrectly classified as operational costs rather than qualifying innovation expenses.

We have reviewed the correspondence and the specific documents cited by the Tax Administration. Our assessment is as follows:

**3.1. Knowledge of the Taxpayer**
The emails in question date back to August 2023, during the fiscal year under review. At that time, the financial administration at Saffier Software B.V., including your former controller, was aware of the classification of these expenditures. The decision to classify them as operational costs was a deliberate accounting choice made by your management team. Since the facts (the nature of the expenses) and the knowledge of their classification were present within your organization during the relevant period, they cannot be considered "unknown" to you in the legal sense required by Article 16 AWR.

**3.2. No New Circumstance**
The Tax Administration is not discovering a new event that occurred after the filing. Rather, it is challenging the correctness of your self-assessment based on information that was already within your sphere of influence. The Hoge Raad has consistently held that an error in judgment or accounting policy by the taxpayer does not give rise to a "new fact" for the purposes of Article 16 AWR. The authority is essentially correcting a potential mistake, not responding to new external information.

**3.3. Procedural Defect**
Furthermore, the Tax Administration failed to inform you of its intention to deviate from your declared position before issuing the assessment. While this is a secondary procedural issue, it reinforces the argument that the basis for the reassessment was flawed from the outset. The reliance on **Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act)** is inappropriate because the prerequisite—a fact unknown to the taxpayer—does not exist.

### 4. Conclusion and Recommendation

Based on the foregoing, we are of the opinion that the Dutch Tax Administration has failed to meet the statutory requirement of a "new fact" under **Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act)**. The information used as the basis for the additional assessment was already known to Saffier Software B.V. at the time of filing. Consequently, the additional tax assessment demanding € 460.000 is legally unsustainable.

We recommend filing an immediate objection against the assessment within six weeks of its issuance (i.e., by May 15, 2025). The objection should explicitly state that no new fact exists within the meaning of Article 16 AWR and request the annulment of the assessment.
