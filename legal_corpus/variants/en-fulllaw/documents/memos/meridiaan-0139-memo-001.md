**SUBJECT:** Matter 2025.0139 – Challenge to Additional Assessment under Article 16 AWR  

### 1. Introduction and Purpose

This memorandum provides legal advice regarding the additional assessment of corporate income tax issued by the Dutch Tax Administration (Belastingdienst) on behalf of Klaver Logistics B.V. for the fiscal year 2024. The assessment imposes a supplementary liability of € 460.000, alleging that certain operational transactions were not reported in the original tax return filed on 1 March 2025.

The central legal question is whether the Tax Administration can validly invoke **Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act)** to justify this re-assessment. Specifically, we must determine if a qualifying "new fact" exists that was unknown to the administration at the time of the original assessment and which directly affects the tax base.

### 2. Legal Framework: Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act)

Under Dutch tax law, the principle of legal certainty protects taxpayers from indefinite liability for past years. The Tax Administration may only issue an additional assessment after the statutory period has expired if it can demonstrate a **"new fact"** (*nieuw feit*) or a **"new circumstance"** (*nieuwe omstandigheid*).

According to established case law (Hoge Raad 6 December 2013, ECLI:NL:HR:2013:84), a "new fact" must meet the following cumulative criteria:
1. It is a factual element that did not exist or was not known to the Tax Administration at the time the original assessment was made.
2. It is not merely a different legal interpretation of facts already known to the administration.
3. It has a direct causal link to the tax liability in question.

If the Tax Administration relies solely on a reinterpretation of data that was available during the initial audit, or if it failed to investigate information that was accessible through standard means, no "new fact" exists under Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act). In such instances, the re-assessment is void ab initio.

### 3. Application to Klaver Logistics B.V.

The Tax Administration’s notice of assessment cites "unreported cross-border service fees" as the basis for the € 460.000 claim. However, our review of the case file reveals that:

1. **Availability of Information:** The invoices and payment trails for these transactions were included in the supporting documentation submitted during the initial tax inspection in November 2024. The administration had full access to these documents.
2. **Lack of New Fact:** The current claim arises not from newly discovered facts, but from a change in the Tax Administration’s internal interpretation of Article 8 of the Corporate Income Tax Act 1969 regarding the deductibility of these specific fees. A change in legal opinion does not constitute a "new fact" under **Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act)**.
3. **Causal Link:** Since the facts were known, the administration had the opportunity to challenge them during the original assessment period. By allowing the return to stand without objection, they effectively accepted the reported position.

### 4. Conclusion and Recommendation

It is our legal opinion that the Dutch Tax Administration has failed to meet the threshold for a "new fact" as required by **Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act)**. The facts underlying the € 460.000 assessment were known or should have been known during the original procedure. Therefore, the additional assessment is procedurally flawed and lacks legal basis.

**Recommended Action:**
We advise Klaver Logistics B.V. to file an objection (*bezwaar*) against the assessment within six weeks of receipt, explicitly citing the absence of a qualifying new fact under **Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act)**. We will argue that the re-assessment violates the principle of legal certainty and seek full annulment of the € 460.000 liability.
