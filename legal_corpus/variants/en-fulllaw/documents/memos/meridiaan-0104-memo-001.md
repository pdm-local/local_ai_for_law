### 1. Introduction and Purpose

Following your instruction on 10 May 2025, I have reviewed the draft Service Agreement (the “Agreement”) circulated by Novael Networks B.V. (“Novael”). The primary objective of this memorandum is to analyze the liability limitation clauses, specifically focusing on the conflicting monetary caps and time-based limitations found in Articles 14.2 and 14.3 of the current draft. This advice is provided pursuant to our engagement for matter 2025.0104.

### 2. Factual Background

Orion Interactive B.V. (“Orion”) intends to engage Novael for the provision of cloud-based infrastructure services. The total annual value of the contract is estimated at EUR 75,000. During negotiations held on 5 May 2025 in Eindhoven, oral representations were made regarding liability limits. However, the written draft provided by Novael contains significant ambiguities that pose a risk to Orion’s commercial interests and legal position under Dutch law.

### 3. Analysis of Conflicting Liability Caps

The draft Agreement presents three distinct limitations on liability that are currently contradictory:

1.  **Article 14.2(a):** States that Novael’s total aggregate liability for any single claim shall not exceed EUR **100.000**.
2.  **Article 14.2(b):** States that Novael’s liability shall be capped at the amount actually paid by Orion in the twelve months preceding the event giving rise to the claim, which is calculated here as EUR **50.000**.
3.  **Article 14.3:** Introduces a temporal limitation, stating that no claims may be brought more than **twelve months** after the discovery of the defect.

#### 3.1 The Conflict Between EUR 100.000 and EUR 50.000

The most critical issue is the juxtaposition of Article 14.2(a) and 14.2(b). While Article 14.2(a) sets a fixed cap of EUR **100.000**, Article 14.2(b) effectively reduces this cap to the contract value paid in the prior year, which is EUR **50.000**.

Under general principles of contract interpretation, if two clauses are irreconcilable, the specific clause usually prevails over the general one. However, Dutch law imposes strict limits on liability waivers through the doctrine of reasonableness and fairness (*redelijkheid en billijkheid*).

#### 3.2 Application of Article 6:248 DCC

We must apply **Article 6:248 DCC**. According to this provision, a term in a standard form contract or a contract where one party relies heavily on the other’s professional expertise may be deemed unacceptable under reasonableness and fairness if it creates an unreasonable imbalance between the parties.

Furthermore, **Article 6:248(2) DCC** explicitly states that a term shall be regarded as unacceptable if it contradicts mandatory statutory provisions or general principles of law. A liability cap of EUR **50.000** for infrastructure services where Orion relies on Novael’s technical expertise for its core business operations is likely to be considered unreasonable by a Dutch court, especially given the potential for consequential damages that far exceed this amount.

The reference to **EUR 100.000** in Article 14.2(a) appears to be a leftover from a previous template or an error in drafting. It creates legal uncertainty because it suggests a higher liability exposure than what is actually contractually enforced by Article 14.2(b).

### 4. Risk Assessment

1.  **Enforceability Risk:** If the dispute arises, Novael will likely argue that Article 14.2(b) (EUR **50.000**) is the operative clause because it is more specific to the payment structure. A court may invalidate both caps if they are deemed too low relative to the service provided, applying **Article 6:248 DCC** to protect Orion as the stronger party in terms of need for protection, or conversely, if Novael can prove Orion is a sophisticated entity that accepted the risk.
2.  **Temporal Limitation:** The restriction of claims to within **twelve months** (Article 14.3) is standard in ICT contracts but must be reconciled with statutory limitation periods (which are generally five years under Dutch law for contractual claims). This clause is enforceable only if it is not unreasonable under **Article 6:248 DCC**. Given the technical nature of cloud infrastructure, defects may not be discovered immediately. A cap of **twelve months** is risky and should be extended to align with the statutory limitation period or at least five years.

### 5. Recommendations

To protect Orion’s interests and ensure legal certainty, I recommend the following actions before signing:

1.  **Remove Ambiguity:** Delete Article 14.2(a) entirely. Do not leave the EUR **100.000** figure in the contract as it creates a false expectation of higher liability coverage.
2.  **Negotiate Higher Cap:** Accepting a cap of EUR **50.000** is commercially dangerous. We should counter-propose a cap equal to two times the annual contract value (EUR 150,000) or at least retain the EUR **100.000** figure as a firm maximum for gross negligence and willful misconduct.
3.  **Clarify Reasonableness:** Include a recital in the Agreement stating that the parties have expressly negotiated the liability caps and that they are acceptable under **Article 6:248 DCC**. This helps rebut future challenges based on reasonableness.
4.  **Extend Time Limit:** Change the **twelve months** limitation period to five years, or at least three years, to align with the general statutory limitation period for contractual obligations in the Netherlands.

### 6. Conclusion

The current draft contains a fatal contradiction between the EUR **100.000** and EUR **50.000** caps. Under **Article 6:248 DCC**, this ambiguity exposes Orion to significant risk. We advise against signing the Agreement in its current form. Please instruct Novael’s legal counsel to revise Article 14 to reflect a single, clear liability cap of at least EUR **100.000** and remove the restrictive **twelve months** time bar for discovery of defects.
