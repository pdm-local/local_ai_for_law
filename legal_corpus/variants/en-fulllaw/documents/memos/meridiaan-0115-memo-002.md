**CONFIDENTIAL LEGAL MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Mr. J. de Vries, Managing Director, Nimbus Interactive B.V.
**From:** mr. D. van Raay, Partner at Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**Date:** 14 March 2025
**Re:** Legal consequences of the bankruptcy declaration on ongoing contractual obligations

### 1. Introduction and Context

Following the declaration of bankruptcy against Nimbus Interactive B.V. by the District Court of Eindhoven on 12 March 2025 (case number OMK 24/678), this memorandum addresses the immediate impact on our client’s active commercial relationships. The primary concern for the management team has been the status of service agreements, software licenses, and supply contracts currently in force.

As your insolvency specialist, I have analyzed the statutory framework governing these situations. The central provision applicable to your matter is **Article 40 Bankruptcy Act** (Faillissementswet). This article serves as the cornerstone for determining whether contractual ties are severed or maintained post-bankruptcy.

### 2. General Principle: Suspension of Obligations

Upon the declaration of bankruptcy, the bankrupt company loses its power to dispose of assets and manage its estate. However, regarding existing contracts, the law does not automatically terminate every agreement. Instead, it grants the trustee (curator) a period of evaluation. During this time, the contractual obligations of the bankrupt party are effectively suspended. Creditors cannot enforce payment or performance through ordinary legal channels while the bankruptcy proceedings are pending final resolution by the curator.

### 3. Termination Rights Under Article 40 Bankruptcy Act

The most critical tool available to you, as the director acting in the interest of preserving value during the transition, is the right to terminate contracts under **Article 40 Bankruptcy Act**. This provision allows for the unilateral termination of ongoing agreements if their continuation would be detrimental to the estate.

#### 3.1 Scope of Application
**Article 40 Bankruptcy Act** applies to bilateral contracts where performance has not yet been fully rendered by both parties. This includes:
*   Software maintenance and hosting contracts with third-party providers.
*   Lease agreements for office space in Eindhoven.
*   Supply contracts for hardware components that have not yet been delivered.

#### 3.2 Procedure for Termination
To effectively terminate a contract under **Article 40 Bankruptcy Act**, the following steps must be taken:
1.  **Written Notice:** A formal notice of termination must be sent to the counterparty. The notice must clearly state that it is issued pursuant to **Article 40 Bankruptcy Act** due to the bankruptcy proceedings.
2.  **Effective Date:** Termination generally takes effect immediately upon receipt or at a date specified in the notice, provided it does not violate mandatory statutory protection for the other party (e.g., certain employment contracts are protected under separate provisions).
3.  **No Waiver of Claim:** Sending this notice does not waive any claims Nimbus Interactive B.V. may have against these parties for unpaid invoices or services already rendered prior to the bankruptcy date.

#### 3.3 Counterparty Rights
It is important to note that counterparties also have rights under **Article 40 Bankruptcy Act**. If you do not terminate a contract, the counterparty may demand that the trustee decide whether to fulfill or reject the contract. If the trustee (or management acting under curator supervision) fails to respond within a reasonable period, the counterparty may consider the contract rejected. Therefore, proactive communication is essential to prevent uncertainty and potential liability for damages resulting from delay.

### 4. Specific Recommendations for Nimbus Interactive B.V.

Based on our review of your current portfolio, I advise the following immediate actions:

1.  **Inventory of Contracts:** Compile a complete list of all active contracts with remaining performance obligations. Categorize them by value and strategic importance.
2.  **Selective Termination:** Identify contracts where continuation poses a financial risk or operational burden. Issue termination notices under **Article 40 Bankruptcy Act** to these parties immediately. For example, the cloud hosting agreement with DataCore Netherlands B.V., which incurs monthly fees of €15,000 without immediate revenue generation, should be terminated forthwith.
3.  **Preservation of Assets:** For contracts involving the lease of premises or equipment, ensure that no new payments are made unless explicitly authorized by the curator. Continued payment may be viewed as fraudulent preference if it depletes the estate to the detriment of other creditors.
4.  **Documentation:** Keep detailed records of all communications and termination notices sent pursuant to **Article 40 Bankruptcy Act**. This documentation will be crucial for the final accounting of the estate and any potential inquiries by the supervisory judge (rechter-commissaris).

### 5. Conclusion

The bankruptcy declaration fundamentally alters Nimbus Interactive B.V.'s contractual landscape, but it does not leave you powerless. **Article 40 Bankruptcy Act** provides a clear legal mechanism to sever detrimental ties and stabilize the remaining estate. By actively utilizing this provision, we can mitigate ongoing liabilities and focus resources on the orderly winding down of business affairs.
