**Subject:** Legal Memorandum: Application of Article 8 ECHR in Administrative Proceedings Concerning Residence Permits

### 1. Introduction and Purpose

This memorandum provides legal advice to Aurora Software B.V. regarding the potential revocation of temporary residence permits issued to three key technical employees (the “Affected Employees”) by the Immigration and Naturalisation Service (IND). The IND has indicated that due to alleged irregularities in the employers’ file, it intends to withdraw the work-and-residence permits linked to these individuals.

The central legal issue is whether such revocation constitutes a disproportionate interference with the Affected Employees’ right to respect for their private and family life under Article 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR), particularly given their long-term integration in the Netherlands.

### 2. Legal Framework: Article 8 ECHR

Article 8 ECHR guarantees the right to respect for private and family life. Any interference by a public authority with this right must be:
1. In accordance with the law;
2. In pursuit of a legitimate aim (such as national security, economic well-being of the country, or prevention of disorder or crime); and
3. Necessary in a democratic society, meaning it is proportionate to the legitimate aim pursued.

In immigration cases, the European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) has established that the state must strike a fair balance between the individual’s right to private life and the state’s interest in enforcing immigration control. The “fair balance” test requires an assessment of the severity of the interference and whether less intrusive measures could have been taken.

### 3. Application to Aurora Software B.V. Case

#### 3.1. Existence of Private Life
The Affected Employees have resided in the Netherlands for periods ranging from five to seven years. During this time, they have established strong social, economic, and cultural ties. They own property in Eindhoven, have children enrolled in local schools, and hold significant professional roles within Aurora Software B.V.’s core R&D department. These factors collectively establish a robust “private life” under Article 8 ECHR.

#### 3.2. Legitimate Aim of the State
The IND’s primary justification for revocation is the maintenance of effective immigration control and the integrity of the permit system. The state argues that allowing permits to remain valid despite procedural irregularities undermines the rule of law. This aim is generally considered legitimate under Article 8 ECHR, provided the measures are proportionate.

#### 3.3. Proportionality Assessment
The critical question is whether the revocation is proportionate. We must weigh the interest of immigration control against the severity of the impact on the Affected Employees’ private lives.

**Arguments Favoring Revocation (State’s Position):**
*   The IND claims that Aurora Software B.V. failed to submit timely financial reports in Q3 2024, constituting a breach of the employer’s obligations under the Aliens Act 2000.
*   Precedent suggests that repeated administrative failures by employers justify stricter penalties to deter non-compliance.

**Arguments Against Revocation (Our Position):**
*   **Severity of Interference:** Revocation would force the Affected Employees to leave the Netherlands immediately, disrupting their careers, family unity, and social integration. The ECtHR requires that such severe measures be justified by compelling reasons.
*   **Proportionality of Measure:** The administrative breach (late reporting) is minor compared to the penalty (loss of residence). Less intrusive measures, such as a fine or a warning to the employer, would suffice to achieve the aim of immigration control without severing private life ties.
*   **Best Interests of Children:** Two of the Affected Employees have Dutch-born children. Their right to family unity is protected under Article 8 ECHR and must be given significant weight.

### 4. Conclusion and Recommendation

Based on the current facts, the revocation of residence permits for the Affected Employees appears disproportionate under Article 8 ECHR. The administrative irregularity by Aurora Software B.V., while actionable, does not justify the extreme consequence of expulsion when less severe sanctions are available.

We recommend the following immediate actions:
1.  File an objection with the IND within six weeks of the decision, explicitly invoking Article 8 ECHR.
2.  Submit evidence of the Affected Employees’ integration (school records, property deeds, tax returns).
3.  Propose a settlement where Aurora Software B.V. pays a nominal fine and implements corrective measures, rather than accepting permit revocation.

The balancing test under Article 8 ECHR favors the preservation of private life in this context, as the state’s interest in administrative perfection is outweighed by the profound impact on individual rights.
