**To:** Management Board, Lumen Cloud B.V.
**From:** mr. N. Vrugt, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V. (Internet Law & Platform Liability)
**Date:** 12 May 2025
**Subject:** Liability exemption for hosting intermediary services under Article 6:196c DCC and post-notice obligations

### 1. Introduction

This memorandum addresses the legal position of **Lumen Cloud B.V.** regarding potential liability for content hosted on its cloud infrastructure, specifically in relation to data processed or stored at the request of **Meridiaan Interactive B.V.** (the "Client"). The analysis focuses on the application of **Article 6:196c DCC**, which transposes the hosting intermediary exemption under Article 14 of the E-Commerce Directive into Dutch law.

### 2. General Principle: Exemption from Liability

Under **Article 6:196c DCC**, an information society service provider is not liable for damages caused by information stored at the request of a user, provided that the provider does not have actual knowledge of illegal activity or information and, upon obtaining such knowledge, acts expeditiously to remove or disable access to that information.

For Lumen Cloud B.V. to qualify for this exemption, three cumulative conditions must be met:

1.  **Nature of the Service:** The service must constitute "hosting." This requires that the provider does not initiate the transmission, select the receiver of the transmission, or select or modify the information contained in the transmission. Merely providing storage space at a client's request typically qualifies as hosting.
2.  **Lack of Actual Knowledge:** At the time the content was stored, Lumen Cloud B.V. must not have had actual knowledge that the activity or information is illegal.
3.  **Expeditious Action upon Notice:** If Lumen Cloud B.V. obtains actual knowledge of the illegality, it must act expeditiously to remove or disable access to the data.

### 3. The Threshold of "Actual Knowledge"

The pivotal question in this matter is when Lumen Cloud B.V. loses its immunity due to "actual knowledge."

**3.1. Passive Monitoring vs. Active Investigation**
Lumen Cloud B.V. does not have a general obligation to monitor the information it stores or to actively seek facts indicating illegal activity. Therefore, mere suspicion or the possibility that Meridiaan Interactive B.V. might be storing illegal content is insufficient to constitute actual knowledge.

**3.2. Notification by a Third Party**
Actual knowledge is generally established only when Lumen Cloud B.V. receives a sufficiently substantiated notification from a third party (e.g., a rights holder or a competent authority) regarding specific illegal content. A vague complaint stating that "some users are posting illegal material" does not trigger liability under **Article 6:196c DCC**. The notification must:
*   Identify the specific illegal information with sufficient precision;
*   Provide evidence supporting the claim of illegality (e.g., copyright infringement, defamation, or hate speech); and
*   Be directed to a designated contact point within Lumen Cloud B.V.

**3.3. Knowledge of Meridiaan Interactive B.V.**
It is crucial to distinguish between knowledge of the *platform* (Lumen Cloud) and knowledge of the *user* (Meridiaan Interactive). Under **Article 6:196c DCC**, liability hinges on whether Lumen Cloud itself knows of the illegality. If Meridiaan Interactive B.V. is aware that its stored data violates the law, this internal knowledge does not automatically transfer to Lumen Cloud B.V. unless Lumen Cloud is independently informed by a third party or becomes aware through its own active monitoring (which it is not required to do).

### 4. The Duty to Act Expeditiously

Once actual knowledge is established, Lumen Cloud B.V. must act "expeditiously." What constitutes expeditious action depends on the circumstances, including:
*   The nature of the illegal content;
*   The technical feasibility of removal; and
*   The urgency of the situation (e.g., content inciting immediate violence requires faster action than potential copyright infringement).

Failure to remove or disable access within a reasonable time after receiving a valid notice will result in the loss of liability exemption. In such cases, Lumen Cloud B.V. may be held jointly liable for damages resulting from the continued availability of the illegal content.

### 5. Application to Current Facts

In the current relationship between Lumen Cloud B.V. and Meridiaan Interactive B.V.:
*   Lumen Cloud provides passive storage services at the request of Meridiaan Interactive.
*   No third-party notification regarding specific illegal content has been received by Lumen Cloud as of 12 May 2025.
*   Therefore, Lumen Cloud B.V. currently enjoys full liability exemption under **Article 6:196c DCC**.

However, if a valid notice is received (e.g., from a copyright holder claiming infringement of copyrighted material stored on Meridiaan Interactive's account), Lumen Cloud must:
1.  Verify the validity and specificity of the notice;
2.  Assess whether the content is indeed illegal under Dutch law; and
3.  Remove or disable access to the specific content expeditiously, typically within a few business days for clear-cut cases.

### 6. Recommendations

To maintain its liability exemption:
1.  Implement a robust and clearly visible notice-and-takedown procedure.
2.  Ensure that all notifications are directed to a dedicated legal/compliance contact within Lumen Cloud B.V.
3.  Avoid any active monitoring of content unless legally required or technically necessary for security purposes, as this may be interpreted as having actual knowledge.
4.  Document all steps taken in response to notices to demonstrate expeditious action if challenged later.

### 7. Conclusion

Lumen Cloud B.V. is exempt from liability under **Article 6:196c DCC** for content stored at the request of Meridiaan Interactive B.V., provided it remains unaware of specific illegality and acts expeditiously upon receiving valid notice. Current facts do not indicate any loss of exemption.
