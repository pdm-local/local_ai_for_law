**MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Orion Media B.V.
**From:** mr. E. Nolte, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**Date:** 14 May 2025
**Subject:** Causation and Attribution of Consequential Damages in Matter 2025.0116

### 1. Introduction

This memorandum addresses the legal obstacles regarding the establishment of a sufficient causal link between the fault of Tinexus Media B.V. and the alleged damages suffered by Orion Media B.V. Specifically, we examine whether the consequential losses incurred during the three-month operational halt can be legally attributed to Tinexus under Dutch tort law.

### 2. Factual Background

On 10 January 2025, Tinexus Media B.V. failed to deliver the core infrastructure upgrade for Orion Media B.V.'s streaming platform, as stipulated in the Service Agreement dated 12 September 2024. This failure caused a complete outage of Orion’s live broadcasting services from 15 January 2025 until 15 April 2025.

Orion Media B.V. claims direct and consequential damages totaling € 310.000. This amount comprises:
*   € 85.000 in lost subscription revenues due to service unavailability;
*   € 145.000 in cancelled sponsorship contracts with third-party advertisers;
*   € 80.000 in emergency costs incurred to secure alternative temporary hosting solutions.

Tinexus Media B.V. has admitted to the breach of contract but denies liability for the consequential losses, arguing that the loss of sponsors was too remote and unforeseeable at the time of contracting.

### 3. Legal Analysis: Causation under Article 6:98 DCC

To hold Tinexus Media B.V. liable for the full amount of € 310.000, Orion Media B.V. must prove that there is a sufficient causal link between the breach and the damage. Under Dutch law, this is governed by Article 6:98 DCC.

Article 6:98 DCC establishes that causation is not merely a matter of factual "but-for" causality (*conditio sine qua non*), but requires a normative assessment of whether the damage can be attributed to the debtor as a consequence of the breach. The court must consider whether the causal link is sufficiently direct and whether the damage falls within the scope of the legal rule that has been violated or the professional duty breached.

#### 3.1 Direct Damages

The € 80.000 in emergency costs and the € 85.000 in lost subscription revenues are likely to be considered directly attributable to the breach. These losses are immediate, predictable consequences of a service outage for a media company. The causal link here is strong and clear under Article 6:98 DCC.

#### 3.2 Consequential Damages and Foreseeability

The significant legal hurdle lies in the € 145.000 loss from cancelled sponsorship contracts. According to established case law interpreting Article 6:98 DCC, for consequential damages to be recoverable, they must be reasonably foreseeable at the time the obligation was created or when the breach occurred.

Orion Media B.V. must demonstrate that Tinexus Media B.V., as a specialized ICT provider, knew or should have known that an outage of this duration would lead to the cancellation of high-value sponsorship deals. If Orion failed to disclose the critical nature of these contracts to Tinexus during the negotiation phase in September 2024, the causal link for this portion of the damage may be deemed insufficiently direct.

Tinexus may argue that the loss of sponsors was an indirect consequence, interrupted by the independent decisions of third-party advertisers. However, if Orion can prove that it had explicitly warned Tinexus about the financial impact of downtime in the annexes to the Service Agreement, the requirement of foreseeability under Article 6:98 DCC is met.

### 4. Conclusion and Advice

The causal link for the € 165.000 comprising emergency costs and lost subscriptions is robust. The challenge lies in securing the € 145.000 in consequential damages.

We advise Orion Media B.V. to:
1.  Compile all correspondence from September 2024 where the financial stakes of downtime were discussed with Tinexus.
2.  Obtain affidavits from the cancelled sponsors confirming that their cancellation was directly triggered by the January-April 2025 outage.

Without evidence of foreseeability, a court may rule that only direct damages are recoverable under Article 6:98 DCC, potentially reducing the claim to € 165.000. We recommend initiating settlement negotiations with Tinexus based on the full € 310.000 but remaining prepared to litigate the foreseeability aspect.
