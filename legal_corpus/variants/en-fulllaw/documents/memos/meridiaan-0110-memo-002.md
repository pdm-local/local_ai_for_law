**MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** mr. R. Bahadoer, Criminal Law Department, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**FROM:** Legal Counsel for Novael Cloud B.V.
**DATE:** 12 April 2025
**SUBJECT:** Defense Strategy: Exclusion of Evidence and Article 6 ECHR Violation

### 1. Introduction

This memorandum outlines the proposed defense strategy for Novael Cloud B.V. (hereinafter "the Defendant") in the ongoing criminal proceedings regarding allegations of corporate tax fraud and money laundering. The central pillar of our defense rests on challenging the admissibility of key evidence obtained by the Public Prosecution Service (Openbaar Ministerie) through procedural irregularities that constitute a violation of the right to a fair trial under Article 6 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR).

### 2. Factual Background

On 15 February 2025, investigators from the Fiscal Intelligence and Investigation Service (FIOD) executed a search warrant at the Defendant’s registered office located at Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven. The search targeted server logs and internal communication archives related to transactions dated between January 2023 and December 2024.

Crucially, the execution of the warrant deviated from the judicial authorization in two significant respects:
1. The warrant authorized the seizure of physical servers but did not explicitly authorize the remote extraction of encrypted backup data stored on third-party cloud infrastructure outside the Netherlands.
2. The investigators proceeded to access this remote data without obtaining a separate, specific legal basis under Dutch law for cross-border data acquisition at that moment.

The evidence obtained from these unauthorized extractions includes email chains and financial ledgers that form the core of the prosecution's case.

### 3. Legal Analysis: Breach of Article 6 ECHR

Article 6 of the European Convention on Human Rights guarantees the right to a fair trial. The European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) has established that the fairness of proceedings is not only concerned with whether the accused was given an opportunity to challenge the evidence but also with how that evidence was obtained.

#### 3.1. Unlawful Acquisition of Evidence

The use of evidence obtained in violation of domestic law or international standards may render a trial unfair if such violation is not sufficiently counterbalanced by procedural safeguards. In this matter, the extraction of remote data exceeded the scope of the search warrant issued by the examining judge on 10 February 2025. Under Dutch criminal procedure law, particularly Article 94a of the Code of Criminal Procedure (Wetboek van Strafvordering), evidence obtained in flagrant violation of the law may be excluded if its admission would undermine the fairness of the trial.

The investigators’ action of bypassing physical seizure limitations to access cloud backups constitutes a "flagrant violation" because it circumvented the specific judicial oversight intended to protect privacy and data integrity under Article 8 ECHR, which is intrinsically linked to the fairness guarantees of Article 6.

#### 3.2. Prejudice to the Defense

The Defendant’s ability to prepare a defense was severely prejudiced by this irregularity. Because the evidence was obtained without proper notification or legal basis at the time of extraction, the chain of custody for the digital forensics report dated 20 March 2025 is compromised. The defense cannot verify whether data integrity was maintained during the unauthorized remote access. This uncertainty directly impacts the reliability of the forensic findings, thereby violating the principle of equality of arms inherent in Article 6 ECHR.

#### 3.3. Consequences for Admissibility

According to established case law, if the admission of unlawfully obtained evidence would render the trial as a whole unfair, the court must exclude such evidence. Given that the core of the prosecution's case relies exclusively on the data extracted via the unauthorized remote access, excluding this evidence would likely lead to the dismissal of the charges or a significant weakening of the prosecution's position.

### 4. Strategic Recommendations

We recommend the following immediate actions:

1. **File an Objection to Admissibility:** Submit a formal motion to the Court in The Hague requesting the exclusion of all evidence derived from the remote extraction conducted on 15 February 2025, citing Article 6 ECHR and the principles of lawful evidence acquisition.
2. **Demand Forensic Audit:** Require an independent forensic audit to prove data tampering or integrity loss resulting from the unauthorized access.
3. **Highlight Proportionality Breach:** Argue that the investigators’ actions were disproportionate under general administrative law principles, reinforcing the unfairness of the trial process.

### 5. Conclusion

The reliance on evidence obtained through a manifest breach of procedural safeguards constitutes a violation of Article 6 ECHR. We must vigorously pursue the exclusion of this evidence to protect the Defendant’s right to a fair trial. Failure to challenge this admission at the earliest stage will waive these objections.

Please advise on the timing of the formal motion submission relative to the next hearing scheduled for 10 May 2025 in Rotterdam.
