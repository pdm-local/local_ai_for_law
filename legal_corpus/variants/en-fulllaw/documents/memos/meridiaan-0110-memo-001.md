### 1. Introduction and Scope

This memorandum provides privileged legal advice to Novael Cloud B.V. ("the Company") regarding the recent receipt of a significant financial transfer that has raised internal compliance concerns. Specifically, this advice addresses the potential criminal liability under Article 420bis of the Dutch Criminal Code (Wetboek van Strafrecht) concerning the laundering of proceeds of crime.

The facts under consideration are as follows: On 10 April 2025, Novael Cloud B.V. received a wire transfer in the amount of €72,500 from an entity known as "Vertex Holdings LLC," registered in Delaware, United States. The Company’s finance department noted that this payment did not correspond to any signed service agreement or invoice currently on file for Q1 2025. Furthermore, Vertex Holdings LLC does not appear in the Company’s standard customer due diligence database.

### 2. Legal Framework: Article 420bis Criminal Code

Article 420bis of the Dutch Criminal Code criminalizes money laundering. The provision states that whoever knowingly performs an act with respect to objects derived from a crime or pre-crime, and thereby conceals their origin or assists in avoiding measures taken under the law concerning the investigation of such proceeds, is guilty of money laundering.

To establish liability, the prosecution must prove two main elements:
1.  **Objective element:** The person performs an act with respect to objects derived from a crime.
2.  **Subjective element:** The person acts *knowingly* (opzet). This requires that the perpetrator is aware that the objects are derived from a criminal offense, or at least accepts this as a real possibility (conditional intent).

### 3. Analysis of the Current Situation

#### 3.1. The Object of the Offense
The amount in question is €72,500. If the funds transferred by Vertex Holdings LLC are derived from a predicate offense (e.g., fraud, tax evasion, or drug trafficking), they constitute "objects derived from a crime." As Novael Cloud B.V. operates as a technology service provider, it may inadvertently become a conduit for such illicit funds if proper due diligence is not exercised.

#### 3.2. The Act of Concealment
By accepting and processing the payment without verifying the source of funds, the Company (and its directors/officers acting on its behalf) may be seen as concealing the origin of the money. Under Dutch law, failing to report a suspicion of money laundering to the Financial Intelligence Unit Netherlands (FIU-Nederland) can itself constitute an act of concealment or at least hinder investigation measures.

#### 3.3. Subjective Intent and Due Diligence
The critical defense for Novael Cloud B.V. is the lack of *knowledge*. However, "willful blindness" is not a defense. If the circumstances were so suspicious that a reasonable person would suspect illicit origins, and the Company ignored these red flags, criminal liability may attach to the individuals who authorized the acceptance of the funds.

**Red Flags Present:**
*   **No Contractual Basis:** The payment lacks a corresponding invoice or contract.
*   **Opaque Counterparty:** Vertex Holdings LLC is a shell-like entity with no visible operational history in the Netherlands.
*   **Amount:** €72,500 exceeds typical small-business transaction thresholds and often triggers mandatory suspicious activity reporting obligations under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act (Wwft).

### 4. Recommendations and Immediate Actions

Given the serious nature of Article 420bis Criminal Code, which carries penalties of up to six years imprisonment for individuals and substantial fines for legal entities, Novael Cloud B.V. must take immediate corrective actions:

1.  **Freeze the Funds:** Do not credit the €72,500 to the Company’s operating account or distribute it until further notice. The funds should remain in a pending status or be held in a segregated suspense account if technically possible, effectively freezing the assets for investigation purposes.
2.  **Internal Investigation:** Immediately conduct an internal KYC (Know Your Customer) check on Vertex Holdings LLC. Request proof of income and the business rationale for the payment from the sender.
3.  **Suspicious Activity Report (SAR):** If the internal investigation confirms that the source of funds cannot be satisfactorily explained, or if the counterparty cannot be verified, a report must be filed with the FIU-Nederland within 24 hours of forming the suspicion. Failure to file this report is a separate criminal offense under Article 15a of the Wwft.
4.  **Legal Representation:** Ensure that all communications regarding this matter are directed through legal counsel to maintain privilege and avoid inadvertently waiving confidentiality or providing false information to authorities.

### 5. Conclusion

The receipt of €72,500 from an unverified source poses a significant risk of violating Article 420bis Criminal Code if the funds are later proven to be proceeds of crime and if Novael Cloud B.V. fails to act with due diligence. The Company must prioritize transparency and compliance over immediate revenue recognition.

We recommend scheduling an emergency meeting with our team to review the specific banking details and draft the necessary internal memos for board approval regarding the potential SAR filing.
