### 1. Introduction and Instructions

At the request of Aurora Software B.V., we have reviewed the current status of the asylum procedure concerning Mr. Aris Thorne, a senior software architect employed by your company since September 2022. Mr. Thorne has submitted an asylum application following the revocation of his residence permit due to changes in his employment status and subsequent immigration compliance issues.

The central legal question is whether Mr. Thorne’s account regarding his fear of persecution or ill-treatment upon return to Iran holds sufficient credibility to meet the threshold for refugee status under Article 29 of the Aliens Act 2000, and what specific documentary evidence can mitigate the risk of return in light of this provision.

### 2. Legal Framework: Credibility and Article 29 Aliens Act 2000

The determination of asylum claims rests heavily on the credibility of the applicant’s narrative. Under Dutch administrative law, the Immigration and Naturalisation Service (IND) assesses whether the applicant meets the definition of a refugee under the Geneva Convention, implemented in Dutch law via the Aliens Act 2000.

**Article 29 Aliens Act 2000** explicitly states that an application for asylum shall be deemed admissible only if the applicant satisfies the conditions for refugee status as defined in international law and domestic implementation. Crucially, the decision hinges on whether there is a well-founded fear of persecution. If the IND deems the account implausible or insufficiently substantiated, the application will be rejected, leading to a final order to leave the Netherlands.

In Mr. Thorne’s case, the core risk lies not in the technical violation of immigration rules, but in the substantive assessment of his personal safety if returned to Iran. The "risk on return" is evaluated based on:
1.  **Individual Circumstances:** Mr. Thorne’s political activities and professional role in the tech sector.
2.  **General Country Information:** The current human rights situation in Iran, particularly regarding IT professionals accused of espionage or intellectual property theft.

### 3. Assessment of Credibility Risks

Our preliminary review indicates two significant credibility challenges:

1.  **Inconsistencies in Timeline:** Mr. Thorne stated he left Tehran in March 2024, yet his digital footprint shows server access logs from his Iranian IP address until April 12, 2024. This discrepancy must be addressed immediately to prevent the IND from labeling his entire account as fabricated.
2.  **Lack of Corroborating Evidence:** While Mr. Thorne claims he was targeted by state security services due to his work for Aurora Software B.V., he has not provided threat letters or official notices prior to his departure. The IND typically requires concrete proof that the individual is specifically identified as a target, rather than merely belonging to a vulnerable professional group.

If credibility is denied, the risk on return becomes purely procedural: Mr. Thorne would be deported to Iran. Given the current geopolitical climate, this carries a high inherent risk of detention and potential harm, which our firm considers unacceptable without robust substantive grounds for protection.

### 4. Strategic Recommendations: Strengthening the Case

To uphold the claim under Article 29 Aliens Act 2000, we must shift the focus from general fear to specific, individualized persecution. The following steps are mandatory:

#### A. Corporate Attestation (Crucial)
Aurora Software B.V. must provide a formal attestation confirming:
*   Mr. Thorne’s specific role in developing encryption protocols for your clients.
*   Any explicit warnings received from Iranian state actors regarding his work.
*   The fact that his termination in January 2025 was directly linked to security concerns raised by Iranian intermediaries, not standard performance issues.

#### B. Expert Country Report
We will commission an updated expert report from a specialized Iran analyst focusing on the persecution of tech-sector professionals between 2023 and 2025. This report must cite specific cases where similar software engineers were detained or harmed upon return, establishing a pattern that directly implicates Mr. Thorne’s profile.

#### C. Witness Statements
Obtain sworn statements from:
1.  Former colleagues in Tehran who witnessed any surveillance or questioning by Iranian authorities prior to his departure.
2.  IT security consultants who can verify the sensitivity of the code Mr. Thorne handled and its potential strategic value to hostile states.

### 5. Conclusion and Next Steps

The risk on return for Mr. Thorne is substantial if the asylum claim is rejected based on credibility issues. To prevent this outcome under Article 29 Aliens Act 2000, we must construct a narrative that is internally consistent and externally corroborated by hard evidence from Aurora Software B.V.

**Immediate Action Required:**
1.  Ms. Vroegh must draft the corporate attestation by **15 April 2025**.
2.  Provide all internal communications between Mr. Thorne and Iranian clients/partners dating back to January 2024 for our forensic review.

Failure to provide this evidence will likely result in a rejection of the asylum application, leaving Mr. Thorne with no legal basis to remain in the Netherlands and exposing him to significant danger upon repatriation.
