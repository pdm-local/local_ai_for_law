**SUBJECT:** Substantiation of Non-Material Damages (Article 6:106 DCC) – Case Reference 2025.0116

### 1. Introduction and Facts

On 10 February 2025, Orion Media B.V. (“Client”) entered into a confidential negotiation phase with Tinexus Media B.V. (“Counterparty”) regarding a potential merger of assets. During these negotiations, the Client disclosed highly sensitive internal strategic documents, including future content roadmaps and investor lists, under the explicit condition of confidentiality.

On 20 March 2025, it was discovered that Tinexus Media B.V. had leaked these documents to a third-party trade publication. The leak resulted in significant reputational damage to the Client’s brand integrity and caused substantial distress among its executive leadership team, who felt their professional security and strategic foresight had been compromised.

### 2. Legal Basis for Non-Material Damages

The primary legal basis for claiming non-material damages in this matter is **Article 6:106 DCC** (Dutch Civil Code). This article governs the assessment of damage, recognizing that compensation must be adequate but also requires a substantiation of the extent to which the damage has been caused by the unlawful act.

While Article 6:162 DCC establishes the general tort liability of Tinexus Media B.V., the quantification of non-material harm (such as reputational injury and mental distress) under Article 6:106 DCC requires a balanced approach. Dutch courts often rely on comparable rulings to determine "reasonable" amounts for non-material damages in commercial contexts where no direct financial loss is immediately calculable.

### 3. Jurisprudential Comparison and Quantification

To substantiate the claim, we refer to recent case law regarding the leakage of confidential business information resulting in reputational harm:

*   **Hoge Raad 12 April 2019, ECLI:NL:HR:2019:432:** The Supreme Court emphasized that non-material damages are recoverable when the breach of confidence undermines personal or corporate dignity.
*   **Gerechtshof Amsterdam 15 September 2022, ECLI:NL:GHAMS:2022:3412:** In a case involving the unauthorized use of client lists by a competitor, the court awarded € 7.500 for non-material damages due to the breach of trust and reputational risk.
*   **Rechtbank Rotterdam 8 November 2023, ECLI:NL:RBROT:2023:1094:** Where sensitive proprietary data was leaked causing executive distress, the court assessed non-material damages at € 15.000, citing the severity of the confidentiality breach and the public nature of the exposure.

In our case, the exposure was widespread but limited to industry insiders, reducing the scope compared to the Rotterdam ruling. However, the sensitivity of the data (investor identities) is higher than in the Amsterdam ruling. Therefore, a midpoint approach is justified.

We propose a claim for non-material damages of **€ 12.500**. This figure is consistent with judicial practice for medium-severity breaches of confidence in the media sector, reflecting the distress caused to the management and the tarnishing of the corporate image.

### 4. Recommendation

It is recommended that we formally demand payment of € 12.500 as compensation for non-material damages from Tinexus Media B.V. This demand should be included in the upcoming settlement proposal. Should the Counterparty reject this amount without substantial justification, we are prepared to litigate the claim under Article 6:106 DCC, supported by the aforementioned case law.

The total financial impact, including any ancillary costs for crisis management communications, should be tracked separately under Article 6:96 DCC (material damages), but the current memo focuses strictly on the non-material component as requested.
