**MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Duinrand Data B.V.

**Date:** 15 March 2025
**Re:** Matter 2025.0143 – Legal effects of the bankruptcy declaration on ongoing contractual obligations

### 1. Introduction

Following the District Court of Eindhoven’s judgment of 10 March 2025 declaring Duinrand Data B.V. bankrupt, this memorandum addresses the immediate legal consequences for the company’s outstanding contracts. As the trustee in bankruptcy has not yet been formally appointed by the court, we are currently acting as counsel to the debtor in preparation for the transition. This advice focuses on your rights and obligations regarding active agreements under Dutch insolvency law.

### 2. General Principle: Continuation of Contracts

Under Dutch law, a declaration of bankruptcy does not automatically terminate existing contracts. In principle, all valid agreements remain in force post-bankruptcy. The trustee (curator) will subsequently decide which contracts to continue and which to terminate. However, until the curator is appointed, you retain certain powers to manage these relations to preserve value for the estate.

### 3. Termination Rights under Article 40 Bankruptcy Act

A critical provision governing your current position is **Article 40 Bankruptcy Act**. This article grants both the debtor (in the pre-trustee phase) and the trustee specific rights to terminate contracts that are not yet fully performed by both parties.

**3.1 Legal Basis**
Pursuant to Article 40 Bankruptcy Act, either party may terminate a contract if the other party has failed to perform its obligations, provided that such failure constitutes a ground for rescission under general contract law (Book 6 of the Dutch Civil Code). More importantly, the article serves as a procedural safeguard allowing for the unilateral termination of ongoing agreements where performance is impossible or commercially unreasonable due to the insolvency status.

**3.2 Application to Duinrand Data B.V.**
As Duinrand Data B.V., you may invoke this provision to terminate contracts that impose undue burdens on the estate. For example:
*   **Lease Agreements:** If the office lease at Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven, requires significant future payments, termination may be considered if the business premises are no longer viable.
*   **Service Contracts:** IT maintenance or software licensing agreements that do not contribute to the wind-down process can be terminated to halt recurring costs.

**3.3 Procedure for Termination**
To validly terminate a contract under this framework:
1.  Notice must be given in writing to the counterparty.
2.  The notice must clearly reference the bankruptcy declaration and the intention to terminate pursuant to statutory insolvency rights.
3.  The termination is effective upon receipt by the counterparty, unless a different period is stipulated in the original contract or required by law.

### 4. Practical Implications and Risks

*   **Counterparty Objections:** Counterparties may challenge the termination if they argue that Duinrand Data B.V. was not in material breach prior to the bankruptcy. However, case law generally supports the right to terminate non-essential contracts to prevent further depletion of assets.
*   **Claims for Damages:** If the counterparty suffers damages due to the termination, such claims will be treated as insolvency claims (concursschulden). These are only payable pro rata from the estate if assets remain after secured creditors are satisfied. Typically, no payment is recoverable by the counterparty.
*   **Employment Contracts:** While Article 40 applies to commercial contracts, employment relationships are governed by separate rules. The trustee will handle employee terminations in accordance with statutory transition payment obligations under Book 7 of the Dutch Civil Code.

### 5. Recommended Actions

We advise Duinrand Data B.V. to take the following steps immediately:

1.  **Inventory Review:** Compile a complete list of all active contracts with remaining performance obligations. Categorize them as essential (e.g., data preservation, critical security) or non-essential.
2.  **Draft Termination Notices:** For non-essential contracts, prepare formal letters citing the bankruptcy declaration and invoking termination rights. Ensure these documents are sent via registered mail to create a verifiable paper trail.
3.  **Communicate with Key Suppliers:** Engage in good faith negotiations with major suppliers (e.g., cloud hosting providers) to see if they offer wind-down packages or waivers of early termination fees, which may benefit the estate by reducing costs.
4.  **Preserve Documentation:** Keep records of all communications and decisions regarding contract management. This protects the directors from potential claims of mismanagement during the critical period before the curator’s appointment.

### 6. Conclusion

The bankruptcy of Duinrand Data B.V. provides a statutory exit route from burdensome contractual relationships via Article 40 Bankruptcy Act. Careful selection of which contracts to terminate is essential to maximize the value of the remaining estate for creditors. We are prepared to draft the necessary termination notices and advise on specific counterparty negotiations upon your instruction.

Please let us know if you wish to proceed with reviewing the lease agreement at Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven, as a priority matter.
