**PRIVILEGED COMMUNICATION / LEGAL ADVICE**

**To:** Mr. E. Nolte, Partner at Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**From:** Aurora Digital B.V. Legal Department
**Date:** March 14, 2025
**Re:** Matter 2025.0130 – Causation and Attribution of Consequential Damages

### 1. Executive Summary

This memo addresses the critical legal hurdle in our claim against Veldhoven Software B.V. regarding the failure to deliver the custom CRM integration module. While liability for the direct costs (€ 27.500) is established, we face significant challenges in proving that the subsequent loss of profit from delayed customer onboarding is legally attributable to Veldhoven Software’s fault. The central issue revolves around Article 6:98 DCC and the requirement of a "sufficient causal link" (toerekenbare oorzaak) for consequential damages.

### 2. Factual Background

On September 1, 2024, Aurora Digital B.V. entered into a service agreement with Veldhoven Software B.V., located at Technologiepark 92, 5652 AE Eindhoven. The contract stipulated that Veldhoven would deliver a fully integrated CRM module by November 30, 2024.

Veldhoven Software failed to meet the deadline, delivering the software only on February 15, 2025, with critical bugs that required further patching. During the period of non-performance (November 30, 2024 – January 31, 2025), Aurora Digital was unable to onboard three key enterprise clients.

The direct costs incurred due to the delay (overtime for internal IT staff to mitigate the issue) amount to € 27.500. We are now seeking to recover this amount plus additional consequential damages for lost profits.

### 3. Legal Analysis: Causation under Article 6:98 DCC

#### 3.1 The Requirement of Sufficient Causal Link

Under Dutch law, a breach of contract gives rise to liability only if the damage is sufficiently related to the breach. Article 6:98 DCC states that damage is attributable to the obligor only if it arises from an event for which the obligor is accountable according to law, a legal act, or prevailing opinion in social life.

In our case, Veldhoven Software’s failure to deliver on time is clearly attributable to them (breach of contract). However, the question is whether the *extent* of the damage, particularly the consequential loss, falls within the scope of this attribution.

#### 3.2 Application to Direct Losses (€ 27.500)

The direct costs of € 27.500 are clearly foreseeable and directly caused by the breach. The causal chain is unbroken:
1. Veldhoven failed to deliver the module on November 30, 2024.
2. Aurora Digital’s internal IT team had to work overtime to manually integrate workarounds.
3. This overtime resulted in costs of € 27.500.

There is no intervening cause that breaks this chain. Therefore, the attribution of these direct damages is straightforward under Article 6:98 DCC.

#### 3.3 The Sticking Point: Consequential Damages and Foreseeability

The more difficult issue is the claim for lost profits from the three delayed client contracts (estimated at € 150.000). Under prevailing Dutch legal doctrine, consequential damages are only recoverable if they were foreseeable at the time of contract conclusion.

We must demonstrate that Veldhoven Software B.V. could have foreseen that a delay in delivery would result in the loss of these specific enterprise clients. The contract itself did not explicitly state that the CRM module was critical for onboarding specific named clients. However, during negotiations in August 2024, Aurora Digital’s CEO sent an email to Veldhoven’s sales director (dated August 15, 2024) stating: *"This integration is a prerequisite for our Q4 enterprise rollout. Any delay will result in direct revenue loss."*

#### 3.4 Argument for Attribution

To secure the consequential damages, we must argue that the breach of duty was so significant that the resulting financial impact falls within the scope of protection of the legal rule violated (the service agreement). We will contend that:
1. The warning email created a specific expectation regarding timeliness.
2. The delay was not due to force majeure or Aurora Digital’s own fault.
3. The causal link between the late delivery and the lost contracts is strong, as the clients had explicitly stated they would withdraw if integration was not complete by November 30.

However, we must be cautious. Veldhoven Software may argue that the loss of profits was too remote or that Aurora Digital failed to mitigate its damages by seeking alternative solutions sooner. The lack of a mitigation effort in December 2024 could weaken our position on the consequential part, but it does not affect the direct € 27.500 claim.

### 4. Conclusion and Recommendation

The causal link for the direct costs of € 27.500 is solidly grounded in Article 6:98 DCC and established case law regarding foreseeable direct damages. We recommend proceeding with litigation or formal demand primarily focused on recovering this confirmed amount, as it carries minimal legal risk.

Regarding the consequential damages, while we have a plausible argument based on the August 15 email, the burden of proof is high. I advise against including the full consequential claim in the initial formal demand to avoid giving Veldhoven Software B.V. an immediate grounds for dismissal based on lack of foreseeability. Instead, we should reserve the right to claim these damages if further negotiation reveals that their breach was willful or grossly negligent, which would strengthen our position under Article 6:98 DCC.

### 5. Next Steps

1. Issue a formal demand letter to Veldhoven Software B.V. for the € 27.500 in direct costs.
2. Request an explanation for the delay and any mitigation efforts made by their side.
3. Prepare a supplementary claim for consequential damages if the initial response is unsatisfactory, leveraging the August 15 communication as evidence of foreseeability.
