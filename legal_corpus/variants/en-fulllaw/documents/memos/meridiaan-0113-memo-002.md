**PRIVILEGED LEGAL ADVICE**

**To:** The Board of Directors, Maaslicht Cloud B.V.
**From:** Meridiaan Advocaten B.V., specifically mr. M. Terhorst
**Date:** 12 April 2025
**Re:** Matter 2025.0113 – Potential liability for improper management regarding the transaction dated 1 March 2025

### 1. Introduction

This memorandum provides legal advice to Maaslicht Cloud B.V. (hereinafter: "Maaslicht Cloud") concerning the conduct of its sole director, Mr. J. van den Berg, in relation to the transfer of intellectual property rights and associated server infrastructure to Maaslicht Systems B.V. (hereinafter: "Maaslicht Systems") on 1 March 2025.

The primary legal standard applicable to this matter is Article 2:9 of the Dutch Civil Code (Burgerlijk Wetboek, hereinafter: "DCC"), which imposes duties on directors of private limited companies (besloten vennootschap). As a fellow director of Maaslicht Cloud, you are entitled to this privileged advice. This document constitutes work product and legal advice intended solely for your internal use.

### 2. Relevant Facts

On 1 March 2025, Maaslicht Cloud transferred the ownership of its proprietary "LichtNet" software codebase and two dedicated physical server units located at Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven, to Maaslicht Systems B.V. for a consideration of €45,000.

At the time of the transaction:
1. The fair market value of the software codebase alone was estimated by an independent valuation report dated 15 February 2025 at approximately €350,000.
2. The server units had a residual book value of €15,000 but a replacement cost exceeding €80,000.
3. Mr. van den Berg acted as the sole director and decision-maker for Maaslicht Cloud. No shareholder resolution was passed prior to the transaction, nor was it reported to the shareholders until 10 March 2025.
4. Maaslicht Systems is a company in which Mr. van den Berg holds a 60% personal shareholding.

### 3. Legal Analysis: Article 2:9 DCC

**3.1 The Statutory Duty**
Article 2:9 DCC stipulates that directors shall perform their tasks with due care and diligence, taking into account the interests of the company and its shareholders. Furthermore, directors must act in accordance with the law and the articles of association. A key component of this duty is the prohibition against placing personal interests above those of the company without proper authorization.

**3.2 Breach of Due Care (Verwaarlozing)**
The standard of care expected from a director is that of a "good director" acting in a comparable situation. The transfer of assets worth approximately €430,000 (combined value of IP and hardware) for only €45,000 constitutes a significant deviation from prudent management. A reasonably diligent director would have:
*   Obtained an independent valuation prior to negotiation;
*   Sought shareholder approval under Article 2:170 DCC for the disposal of substantial assets; or
*   Ensured that the transaction was on arm's length terms comparable to market rates.

The failure to secure shareholder consent and the gross undervaluation suggest a breach of the duty of care.

**3.3 Conflict of Interest and Self-Dealing**
Given Mr. van den Berg’s 60% stake in Maaslicht Systems, he had a personal financial interest in the transaction. Under Dutch corporate law principles derived from Article 2:9 DCC, a director must avoid conflicts of interest. When a conflict exists, the director must refrain from participating in the decision-making process or obtain explicit waiver/approval from the shareholders or the supervisory board (if applicable). Here, Mr. van den Berg both initiated and executed the transaction without disclosure to the other party, Maaslicht Cloud’s minority shareholder, TechVentures B.V.

**3.4 Causation and Damages**
The difference between the consideration paid (€45,000) and the fair market value (~€430,000) represents a direct loss to the corporate estate of Maaslicht Cloud. This loss is directly attributable to the improper exercise of managerial powers.

### 4. Conclusion

There are strong indications that Mr. J. van den Berg has breached his duties under Article 2:9 DCC. The transaction was not conducted in the interest of Maaslicht Cloud, failed to observe proper procedural safeguards regarding conflicts of interest, and resulted in substantial financial detriment to the company.

Maaslicht Cloud may have grounds to claim damages from Mr. van den Berg personally under Article 2:170 DCC read in conjunction with Article 6:162 DCC (tortious liability), as well as to seek restitution of the assets or compensation for their value.

### 5. Recommendations

We recommend the following immediate steps:
1. Convene an emergency shareholders' meeting to address the breach and potential removal of Mr. van den Berg from his directorship if not already done.
2. Issue a formal demand letter to Mr. van den Berg and Maaslicht Systems B.V. for restitution of €385,000 (the difference between market value and consideration) plus interest.
3. Preserve all internal communications regarding this transaction as potential evidence for future litigation.

Please contact mr. M. Terhorst at our Eindhoven office to discuss the drafting of the demand letter.
