**MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Management Board, Kempen Labs B.V.
**From:** mr. F. Lindeman, Meridiaan Advocaten
**Date:** 14 May 2025
**Re:** Tax analysis of corporate income tax correction and substantial-interest implications for Kempen Labs B.V.

### 1. Introduction

Following your request regarding the proposed adjustment of the taxable profit for Kempen Labs B.V. (hereinafter: "the Company") for the fiscal year ending 31 December 2024, we have prepared this memorandum. The core issue concerns the reclassification of a €450,000 expenditure from operating costs to a non-deductible distribution under Article 8 of the Corporate Income Tax Act 1969 (hereinafter: "CIT Act"), and how this correction affects the substantial interest held by Mr. J. de Vries under Article 3.8 of the Income Tax Act 2001 (hereinafter: "IT Act").

### 2. Analysis of Corporate Income Tax Correction

#### 2.1 Applicability of Article 8 CIT Act

The Dutch tax authority (Belastingdienst) has indicated that €450,000 paid to a related party, TechSolutions B.V., lacks sufficient economic substance and is therefore not deductible as a business expense. Instead, it constitutes a hidden distribution or a capital contribution without corresponding equity increase.

Under **Article 8 Corporate Income Tax Act 1969**, only expenditures that are reasonably connected with the company’s enterprise are deductible. If the expenditure is deemed to be made for reasons other than the further development of the enterprise (such as private benefit of the substantial interest holder), it is not deductible. In this case, the lack of arm's length pricing and the absence of a clear service agreement support the authority’s position that the deduction must be disallowed.

#### 2.2 Consequences for Taxable Profit

The disallowance of the €450,000 deduction results in an increase in the Company’s taxable profit by the same amount. Assuming a corporate income tax rate of 19% (for the portion up to €200,000) and 25.8% for the remainder, the additional tax liability is calculated as follows:

*   Taxable profit increase: €450,000
*   Additional CIT liability (estimated blended rate): €106,100

This amount will be due upon issuance of the amended assessment notice.

### 3. Impact on Substantial Interest (Article 3.8 IT Act)

#### 3.1 Definition of Substantial Interest

Mr. J. de Vries holds a 30% share in Kempen Labs B.V. Under **Article 3.8 Income Tax Act 2001**, a substantial interest exists if the shareholder, either alone or together with related parties, holds directly or indirectly at least 5% of the issued shares or voting rights. Mr. de Vries clearly meets this threshold.

#### 3.2 Valuation of Shares and Box 2 Taxation

The correction under Article 8 CIT Act increases the Company’s taxable profit, which may lead to a higher retained earnings position. However, for purposes of **Article 3.8 Income Tax Act 2001**, the value of the shares is determined based on their fair market value at the end of the tax year (31 December 2024), not solely on accounting profit.

While the disallowance of the expense increases the company’s equity, it does not automatically translate to a proportional increase in share value for all stakeholders, especially if the expenditure was viewed as a misallocation of capital. Nevertheless, the net asset value (NAV) of Kempen Labs B.V. will be lower by €450,000 than previously reported, which could negatively impact the valuation of Mr. de Vries’ shares if an independent appraisal is required for box 2 taxation.

#### 3.3 Benefit from Loans and Other Benefits

If the €450,000 payment was effectively a loan to Mr. de Vries or his related party without adequate interest, it could be reclassified as a benefit under **Article 3.8 Income Tax Act 2001**. In such a scenario, the full amount would be taxable in box 2 (wealth) or box 1 (income) for Mr. de Vries, depending on the structure. However, given that the payment was made to TechSolutions B.V., we assume no direct benefit flows to Mr. de Vries personally, and thus this risk is minimal unless a piercing of the corporate veil is argued by the tax authority.

### 4. Conclusion and Recommendations

1.  **Corporate Level:** The Company should prepare for an additional CIT liability of approximately €106,100 due to the disallowance under **Article 8 Corporate Income Tax Act 1969**. We recommend negotiating a settlement with the tax authority on the interest penalty if the assessment is confirmed.
2.  **Substantial Interest Level:** For Mr. de Vries, the primary impact is the potential reduction in share value due to the lower retained earnings. There is no direct taxable benefit under **Article 3.8 Income Tax Act 2001** unless a direct link to private benefit is established.
3.  **Next Steps:** We advise conducting an independent valuation of Kempen Labs B.V. shares as of 31 December 2024 to accurately determine the box 2 tax base for Mr. de Vries.

Please do not hesitate to contact us if you require further clarification or wish to discuss representation before the tax court.
