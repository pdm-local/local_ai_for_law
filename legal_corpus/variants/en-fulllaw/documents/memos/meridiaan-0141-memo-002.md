**CONFIDENTIAL LEGAL MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Management Board, Verdana Retail B.V.
**From:** mr. M. Terhorst, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**Date:** 14 May 2025
**Subject:** Legal assessment of alleged improper management under Article 2:9 DCC regarding the Novael Digital B.V. transaction

### 1. Introduction and Scope

Following your request regarding the significant financial loss incurred by Verdana Retail B.V. (hereinafter: "the Company") due to the failed performance by Novael Digital B.V. (hereinafter: "the Counterparty"), I have conducted a legal review of the decision-making process surrounding the procurement agreement dated 12 February 2025.

This memorandum addresses whether the sole director and shareholder, Mr. J. van den Berg, acted in accordance with the standard of care expected of a reasonably acting director during the negotiation and conclusion of this agreement. The analysis is grounded in Dutch corporate law, specifically Article 2:9 of the Dutch Civil Code (DCC).

### 2. Legal Framework: Article 2:9 DCC

Article 2:9 DCC establishes that directors must conduct themselves as a reasonably acting director would in similar circumstances. This duty encompasses both procedural and substantive obligations:
1.  **Procedural Duty:** Directors must ensure adequate information gathering, proper deliberation, and compliance with internal governance rules (e.g., obtaining shareholder or supervisory board approval if required by the Articles of Association).
2.  **Substantive Duty:** Directors must act in the best interest of the Company, avoiding conflicts of interest and ensuring that business decisions are rational and financially sound.

A breach of this duty occurs if a director fails to act as a prudent manager would, leading to damage for the Company. In cases where the director is also the sole shareholder, liability is typically limited to third parties unless the actions constitute gross negligence or intent (sjoemelpraktijk). However, the standard of care remains objective.

### 3. Factual Background

The facts established through our review of internal emails and meeting minutes are as follows:

*   **15 January 2025:** Mr. van den Berg requested an IT system upgrade to modernize inventory management.
*   **28 January 2025:** The Company received a quote from Novael Digital B.V. for €450,000. No competitive bids were solicited.
*   **01 February 2025:** Mr. van den Berg reviewed the proposal but did not consult with external legal counsel or financial advisors regarding the technical specifications or payment terms.
*   **10 February 2025:** The Company’s Articles of Association require any single contract exceeding €200,000 to be approved by a written resolution of the shareholder. Mr. van den Berg bypassed this requirement, citing time pressure.
*   **12 February 2025:** Mr. van den Berg signed the agreement with Novael Digital B.V. without conducting a due diligence check on Novael’s financial solvency or past performance.
*   **20 March 2025:** Novael Digital B.V. ceased operations, leaving the Company with no deliverables and an outstanding invoice of €450,000 already paid.

### 4. Analysis of Potential Breach

#### 4.1. Procedural Irregularities
Mr. van den Berg clearly violated the internal governance protocols mandated by the Company’s Articles of Association. By bypassing the shareholder resolution requirement for a contract worth €450,000, he failed to adhere to the procedural safeguards designed to protect the Company’s assets. Under Article 2:9 DCC, directors are bound by these internal rules unless they are explicitly amended or waived in accordance with law. Failure to do so constitutes prima facie evidence of improper management.

#### 4.2. Lack of Due Diligence
A reasonably acting director would have conducted basic due diligence before committing the Company to a significant financial obligation. This includes:
*   Verifying the counterparty’s creditworthiness (e.g., via a BKR extract or trade reference).
*   Ensuring competitive pricing by soliciting at least two other quotes.
*   Reviewing technical deliverables with expert advice.

Mr. van den Berg did none of these things. He relied solely on a single proposal without verification. This omission is not merely a business error but a failure to meet the professional standard expected of a director managing substantial funds.

#### 4.3. Causation and Damages
The damage suffered by the Company (€450,000) is directly linked to the lack of due diligence. Had a credit check been performed, it would likely have revealed Novael Digital B.V.’s financial instability. Had competitive bids been sought, the Company might have secured similar services from a solvent provider at a lower cost or with better payment terms (e.g., milestone-based payments).

### 5. Conclusion and Recommendations

Based on the facts above, there are strong grounds to conclude that Mr. van den Berg breached his duty under Article 2:9 DCC. The combination of bypassing internal approval procedures and failing to perform basic due diligence demonstrates a significant deviation from the standard of care required of a director.

#### Immediate Actions Recommended:
1.  **Preserve Evidence:** Secure all internal communications, including emails and draft resolutions, related to this transaction.
2.  **Assess Insolvency Implications:** Given Novael Digital B.V.’s cessation of operations, we should evaluate whether they are in formal bankruptcy proceedings. If so, the Company must file its claim with the curator immediately.
3.  **Director Liability Review:** While Mr. van den Berg is also the sole shareholder (which typically insulates him from liability to the Company itself), his actions may constitute gross negligence if third parties (e.g., employees or creditors) are affected, or if he misappropriated funds. We recommend a separate analysis of potential criminal or administrative implications if fraud is suspected.
4.  **Governance Reform:** Implement stricter internal controls requiring dual signatures for contracts over €50,000 and mandatory external due diligence for any transaction exceeding €200,000.

The director's actions in this matter were irresponsible and exposed the Company to unnecessary risk. Immediate steps must be taken to mitigate further losses and strengthen internal governance.
