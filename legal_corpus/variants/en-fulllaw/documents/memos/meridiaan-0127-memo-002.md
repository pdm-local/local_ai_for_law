**CONFIDENTIAL LEGAL ADVISORY**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Duinrand Networks B.V.
**FROM:** mr. M. Terhorst, Corporate / Company Law Specialist
**DATE:** 12 April 2025
**SUBJECT:** Preliminary Legal Assessment: Director’s Duty of Care and Management Standards under Article 2:9 DCC

### 1. Introduction and Scope

We refer to the urgent inquiry regarding the financial discrepancies identified during the internal audit of Duinrand Networks B.V. (“Duinrand”) concerning the service agreement concluded with Cobalt Labs B.V. (“Cobalt Labs”) on 15 January 2025. The audit revealed that payments totaling €450,000 were transferred to a third-party vendor without prior board approval or adequate due diligence.

This memo analyzes whether the actions (and omissions) of Mr. J. van der Meer, director of Duinrand Networks B.V., constitute a breach of the statutory duty of care and proper management imposed on directors under Dutch law, specifically Article 2:9 of the Dutch Civil Code (DCC).

### 2. Legal Framework: Article 2:9 DCC

Pursuant to **Article 2:9 DCC**, every director is obliged to manage the company with due care and in the interest of the enterprise. This duty encompasses both a general standard of conduct and specific obligations regarding financial administration and internal controls. The legal standard applied is that of a “properly acting director” acting under comparable circumstances.

Key elements of this duty include:
1.  **Knowledge and Skill:** Directors are expected to possess and apply the knowledge and skills reasonably required for their position. Ignorance of relevant facts or applicable regulations does not exempt a director from liability if they should have known better.
2.  **Information Gathering:** Before taking significant decisions, especially those involving substantial financial outlays or new business relationships, directors must ensure they are sufficiently informed.
3.  **Internal Controls:** The board is responsible for establishing and maintaining adequate internal control mechanisms, particularly regarding the authorization of payments and the verification of counterparties.

### 3. Analysis of Facts against Legal Standards

In this matter, we examine three specific aspects of Mr. van der Meer’s conduct:

#### 3.1 Lack of Due Diligence on Counterparty
The decision to engage Cobalt Labs B.V., registered in Rotterdam (KvK number 92847561), was made without the standard vendor risk assessment. Given that Cobalt Labs had only been incorporated for six months and lacked a verifiable credit history, a properly acting director would have required additional guarantees or escrow arrangements. Proceeding with a €450,000 advance payment without such safeguards demonstrates a failure to exercise reasonable care.

#### 3.2 Circumvention of Internal Authorization Procedures
Duinrand’s Articles of Association and internal financial regulations (dated 1 March 2024) explicitly state that any single transaction exceeding €100,000 requires dual signatory approval from the CEO and CFO. Mr. van der Meer authorized the payment unilaterally on 16 January 2025. This is a clear violation of established internal controls. Under Article 2:9 DCC, directors cannot rely on the existence of internal rules to shield themselves if they actively choose to bypass them without valid business justification. No such justification exists in the file; the transaction was not marked as urgent or critical infrastructure maintenance.

#### 3.3 Omission to Monitor Performance
Following the payment, no milestone reports or deliverables were requested from Cobalt Labs for three months. A director exercising due care would monitor the execution of contractual obligations, particularly when significant funds have been disbursed upfront. The failure to follow up until the funds were lost suggests negligence in the ongoing management of the enterprise’s assets.

### 4. Assessment of Liability and Risk

Based on the above, there is a strong prima facie case that Mr. van der Meer has breached his duty under **Article 2:9 DCC**. The breach is not merely technical but substantive, resulting in direct financial harm to Duinrand Networks B.V.

Potential consequences include:
*   **Personal Liability:** Duinrand may claim damages from Mr. van der Meer for the loss suffered (€450,000 plus interest).
*   **Derivative Action:** Shareholders holding at least 7% of the issued share capital could initiate inquiry proceedings under Article 2:345 DCC to investigate the management and potentially dismiss the board.
*   **Criminal Implications:** If it is found that Mr. van der Meer acted with intent to cause damage or for personal gain, this could escalate to criminal charges of fraud or embezzlement.

### 5. Recommendations

We recommend the following immediate steps:

1.  **Preservation of Evidence:** Secure all emails, board minutes, and bank records related to the Cobalt Labs transaction. Do not alter any digital files.
2.  **Formal Notice:** Issue a formal letter to Mr. van der Meer demanding an explanation for the unauthorized payment and the lack of due diligence, citing Article 2:9 DCC.
3.  **Interim Measures:** Suspend Mr. van der Meer’s signatory rights immediately pending further investigation.
4.  **Legal Action:** If the explanation is unsatisfactory, prepare a claim for damages based on breach of statutory duty. Simultaneously, instruct our debt collection agency to pursue Cobalt Labs B.V. for restitution, though their insolvency risk is high.

### 6. Conclusion

The management of the disputed transaction falls significantly below the standard of a properly acting director under **Article 2:9 DCC**. The combination of inadequate due diligence, bypassing internal controls, and failure to monitor performance establishes a clear breach of duty. We advise proceeding with immediate protective measures to mitigate further loss.
