**MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Aurora Logistics B.V. (The "Company")
**FROM:** mr. D. van Raay, Insolvency Law Specialist
**DATE:** 12 March 2025
**SUBJECT:** Legal Opinion on the Avoidance of Payment under Article 42 Bankruptcy Act

### 1. Introduction and Objective

This memorandum is prepared at the request of Aurora Logistics B.V. to assess the viability of avoiding a specific payment made by the Company to Meridiaan Solutions B.V. (the "Counterparty"). The central question is whether the Counterparty knew or should have known that the payment would prejudice the general body of creditors, thereby satisfying the subjective element for avoidance under Article 42 of the Bankruptcy Act (*Faillissementswet*).

### 2. Relevant Facts

On 15 January 2025, Aurora Logistics B.V. transferred a sum of € 95.000 to the bank account of Meridiaan Solutions B.V., located at Herengracht 450, Amsterdam. This payment was intended to settle an outstanding invoice for consulting services rendered in December 2024.

At the time of the transfer:
1. Aurora Logistics B.V. was already experiencing severe liquidity issues, having failed to pay two other major suppliers in November 2024.
2. The Company had received informal warnings from its primary bank regarding potential overdraft limits on 10 January 2025.
3. Meridiaan Solutions B.V. is a related entity within the same corporate group, sharing common managerial oversight with Aurora Logistics B.V.

### 3. Legal Framework: Article 42 Bankruptcy Act

Article 42 of the Bankruptcy Act provides that acts performed by the debtor after the opening of bankruptcy or in the period leading up to it can be avoided if the counterparty knew or should have known that the act would prejudice the general body of creditors.

To successfully invoke Article 42, three cumulative requirements must be met:
1. **Detriment:** The act must prejudice the general body of creditors (*schade aan het collectieve belang*).
2. **Timing:** The act was performed after the onset of insolvency or in a period where insolvency was imminent.
3. **Knowledge:** The counterparty knew or ought to have known of this detrimental effect (*wetens en willens*).

### 4. Analysis of Knowledge (Subjective Element)

The pivotal issue in this matter is whether Meridiaan Solutions B.V. possessed the requisite knowledge of prejudice at the time the € 95.000 was paid.

#### 4.1. Actual Knowledge
Given the corporate relationship between Aurora Logistics B.V. and Meridiaan Solutions B.V., it is highly probable that Meridiaan Solutions B.V. had access to internal financial reports. The board members of Meridiaan Solutions B.V. also sit on the supervisory board of Aurora Logistics B.V. Consequently, they were aware of the liquidity crisis reported in the internal management accounts dated 5 January 2025.

Furthermore, the timing of the payment—shortly after the bank warning on 10 January 2025—suggests that Meridiaan Solutions B.V. could not claim ignorance of the Company’s precarious financial state. A reasonable manager in the position of the directors of Meridiaan Solutions B.V. would have recognized that prioritizing a payment to a related entity over other creditors, while the debtor is on the brink of insolvency, constitutes prejudice to the collective body of creditors.

#### 4.2. Constructive Knowledge
Even if actual knowledge cannot be proven beyond doubt, Article 42 also covers cases where the counterparty *should have known* (*behoorde te weten*). The standard applied is that of a "reasonable and prudent person" in the same position.

In corporate law, related entities are held to a higher standard of scrutiny regarding the financial health of their affiliates. The fact that the payment was made shortly after the Company’s assets were effectively frozen by bank warnings implies that Meridiaan Solutions B.V. should have anticipated that paying € 95.000 would deplete the remaining available funds, thereby harming other creditors who had not yet been paid.

### 5. Prejudice to the General Body of Creditors

The payment of € 95.000 clearly prejudices the general body of creditors. At the time of payment, Aurora Logistics B.V. was already insolvent or nearing insolvency. By channeling these funds to Meridiaan Solutions B.V., the Company reduced the estate available for distribution among its other unsecured creditors. This satisfies the objective requirement of Article 42 Bankruptcy Act.

### 6. Conclusion and Recommendation

Based on the facts presented, it is our legal opinion that Meridiaan Solutions B.V. knew or should have known that the payment of € 95.000 would prejudice the general body of creditors. The close corporate relationship, access to internal financial data, and the timing of the payment relative to bank warnings strongly support a finding of constructive knowledge.

Therefore, the avoidance claim under Article 42 Bankruptcy Act has a strong chance of success. We recommend initiating formal proceedings to recover the € 95.000 from Meridiaan Solutions B.V. and adding it back to the bankruptcy estate.
