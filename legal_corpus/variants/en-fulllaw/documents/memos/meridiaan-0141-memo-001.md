**PRIVILEGED LEGAL ADVICE**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Verdana Retail B.V.
**FROM:** mr. M. Terhorst, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**DATE:** 12 March 2025
**SUBJECT:** Legal Compliance of Proposed Dividend Distribution – Article 2:216 DCC Assessment

### 1. Introduction and Mandate

On 10 March 2025, the management board of Verdana Retail B.V. (hereinafter: "the Company") requested urgent legal advice regarding the proposed distribution of a dividend amounting to **€750,000** to its sole shareholder, Novael Digital B.V. (hereinafter: "Shareholder").

The Board intends to adopt a resolution for this distribution at the upcoming Extraordinary General Meeting scheduled for 20 March 2025. This memorandum provides a critical legal assessment of the risks associated with this proposal, specifically focusing on the statutory requirements for dividend distributions under Dutch corporate law.

### 2. Legal Framework: Article 2:216 DCC

Under **Article 2:216 DCC**, a private company (Besloten Vennootschap) may only distribute profits to its shareholders if such distribution does not result in the Company’s net assets becoming insufficient to cover its paid-up and called-up share capital plus the statutory reserves. This requirement is commonly referred to as the "distribution test" or *verdeeltest*.

The core legal principle is that the balance sheet must reflect a true and fair view of the Company’s financial position. The distribution test is not merely a formal check but a substantive assessment of solvency at the moment the resolution is adopted. If the net assets fall below the sum of the share capital and statutory reserves after the proposed distribution, the distribution is legally prohibited.

### 3. Application to the Proposed Distribution

The Company’s most recent audited financial statements as of 31 December 2024 show a net asset value that is currently marginal relative to the required solvency threshold. The proposed dividend of **€750,000** represents a significant outflow of equity.

Based on our preliminary review of the interim management accounts for Q1 2025:

1.  **Net Asset Position:** The Company’s current net assets are estimated at €820,000.
2.  **Share Capital and Reserves:** The paid-up share capital is €22,500, and the statutory reserves amount to €45,000. The total threshold for distribution is therefore €67,500.
3.  **Post-Distribution Projection:** If the dividend of **€750,000** is paid, the remaining net assets would be approximately €70,000. While this technically exceeds the €67,500 threshold in a static view, it ignores interim liabilities and potential contingent obligations that have arisen since the last balance sheet date.

### 4. Risk Assessment and Director Liability

The primary risk lies in the timing of the distribution test. According to established case law (e.g., *Hoge Raad 13 March 2015, ECLI:NL:HR:2015:693*), the solvency must be maintained at the moment the resolution is passed and, crucially, when the payment is executed.

If the distribution of **€750,000** causes the Company’s net assets to drop below the threshold during the interval between resolution and payment due to ordinary business operations or unforeseen liabilities, the distribution becomes unlawful.

**Consequences for Directors:**
Pursuant to **Article 2:248 DCC**, directors are personally liable for damages suffered by creditors if they knowingly or negligently violate the distribution test. In cases where the Company becomes insolvent as a result of an unlawful distribution, directors may be held jointly and severally liable for the shortfall in the bankruptcy estate. Given that Novael Digital B.V. is the sole shareholder, there is a high risk that any claim arising from this distribution will target the directors directly.

### 5. Recommendations

To mitigate legal exposure, we advise the following:

1.  **Interim Balance Sheet:** Prepare a precise interim balance sheet as of the date of the General Meeting (20 March 2025) to confirm that net assets remain sufficient after deducting the **€750,000** dividend.
2.  **Contingency Buffer:** Ensure that the remaining net assets after distribution include a reasonable buffer for operational risks. A post-distribution equity position of only €70,000 is precarious and may be deemed insufficient by a court if liquidity issues arise shortly thereafter.
3.  **Alternative Structure:** Consider reducing the dividend amount to **€500,000** to provide a safer margin of solvency, or defer the distribution until after the audited 2024 financial statements are formally approved and any interim losses are accounted for.

### 6. Conclusion

The proposed distribution of **€750,000** carries substantial legal risk under **Article 2:216 DCC**. Without a robust, forward-looking solvency assessment confirming that net assets will remain strictly above the statutory threshold throughout the payment process, the directors face significant personal liability exposure. We recommend revising the proposed amount or delaying the resolution until comprehensive financial data supports the transaction.
