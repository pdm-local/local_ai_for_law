**Subject: Response to Cobalt Software B.V.’s claim regarding design rights and alleged infringement**

Dear mr. Reinders,

We act on behalf of Tinexus Digital B.V. (hereinafter: "Tinexus") in response to the letter received from Cobalt Software B.V. (hereinafter: "Cobalt") dated 28 February 2025, wherein Cobalt alleges that Tinexus’s proprietary user interface design for its cloud-based analytics platform ("NexusView") infringes upon Cobalt’s registered Community Design No. EU006789432-0001 (hereinafter: "the Registered Design").

After a thorough legal and technical assessment, Tinexus firmly denies any infringement of the Registered Design or any other intellectual property rights held by Cobalt. Our position is grounded in three primary arguments: independent creation, the lack of protected originality in the specific visual elements claimed, and the principle that general design styles cannot be monopolized under Dutch and EU law.

**1. Independent Creation**

Tinexus developed the NexusView interface through an internal team led by Lead Designer Ms. Elara Vance. The development process was documented extensively, with version control logs on GitHub showing iterative drafts starting from 15 October 2024. The final design was approved on 12 December 2024, prior to Cobalt’s public launch of its "Orbit" dashboard on 5 January 2025.

The visual similarities alleged by Cobalt are coincidental and stem from standard industry conventions in data visualization. Tinexus has never accessed Cobalt’s source code, design files, or unreleased prototypes. The independent creation is substantiated by the attached design notebooks (Exhibit A) and timestamped commit logs (Exhibit B).

**2. Absence of Original Characteristic**

Under Article 3(1) of Regulation (EC) No 6/2002 on Community Designs, a design must possess "individual character," meaning it produces a different overall impression on the informed user compared to any design made available to the public before the filing date.

Cobalt’s Registered Design primarily comprises:
*   A grid-based layout for data tables;
*   Standard color palettes (blue and grey) commonly used in enterprise software;
*   Generic icons (magnifying glass, download arrow) that are functional rather than artistic.

These elements are dictated by technical requirements or are common in the sector. They lack the creative freedom necessary to confer individual character. The informed user, being familiar with the existing body of knowledge on data analytics interfaces, would perceive the NexusView interface as a standard implementation of industry norms, not as a reproduction of Cobalt’s specific aesthetic choices.

**3. Non-Monopolizability of General Style**

Cobalt attempts to assert rights over a "dark mode" aesthetic with rounded corners and minimalist typography. It is a well-established principle in intellectual property law that general design styles or "look and feel" cannot be monopolized. Protection extends to specific, original expressions, not to functional layouts or common artistic trends. Allowing Cobalt to monopolize this style would stifle competition and innovation in the SaaS sector, contrary to the public interest protected by Article 2.20 of the Benelux Convention on Intellectual Property (BCIP) and relevant EU case law (e.g., *Cofemel*, C-683/17).

**Conclusion and Demand**

Tinexus Digital B.V. considers its NexusView interface to be a distinct, independently created work that does not infringe upon Cobalt’s intellectual property rights. We respectfully request that Cobalt withdraw its claims immediately to avoid unnecessary litigation costs.

We reserve all rights of Tinexus Digital B.V., including the right to seek a declaratory judgment of non-infringement and recovery of legal costs should Cobalt proceed with baseless action.
