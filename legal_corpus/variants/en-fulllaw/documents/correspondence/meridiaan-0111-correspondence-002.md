**Subject: Notice of objection against the additional assessment of corporate income tax for the fiscal year 2023 and the penalty under Article 67d of the State Taxes Act (AWR)**

Dear Sir or Madam,

We represent Saffier Software B.V. (hereinafter "the Client"), a company with its registered office in Amsterdam, regarding the additional assessment notice dated 1 March 2025 (reference number: AWB/2025/99876) and the accompanying penalty decision.

The Client submits this formal notice of objection against both the determination of the additional tax liability and the imposition of a penalty calculated at **50%** of the unpaid tax amount, pursuant to Article 67d of the State Taxes Act 1990 (hereinafter "AWR"). The Client contests the Inspector’s findings that the incorrect reporting was due to gross negligence or intent.

### I. Facts of the Case

Saffier Software B.V. is a technology firm specializing in cloud-based inventory management solutions. For the fiscal year ending 31 December 2023, the Client filed its corporate income tax return on 30 September 2024. The return was prepared by an external accounting firm, Van der Meer & Partners, based on financial statements audited by Audit Group Eindhoven.

On 15 February 2025, the Inspector issued a notification indicating that certain R&D expenditures had been classified incorrectly under the Innovation Box regime (Article 8 of the Corporate Income Tax Act 1969). The Inspector determined that these expenditures did not meet the qualifying criteria for patent income and therefore adjusted the taxable profit upward by EUR 425,000. Consequently, an additional assessment was issued for EUR 136,000 in corporate income tax. Alongside this amount, the Inspector applied a penalty of **50%** (EUR 68,000) under Article 67d AWR, citing gross negligence in the preparation and submission of the tax return.

### II. Legal Arguments

#### 1. Absence of Gross Negligence or Intent

The application of a penalty under Article 67d AWR requires proof that the taxpayer acted intentionally or with gross negligence. The Client firmly denies both allegations.

Firstly, the Client did not act with intent. There was no deliberate attempt to evade tax obligations. The classification of the R&D expenditures was based on the technical reports provided by the development team and the advice of the external accountant. When the Inspector challenged the qualification, the Client immediately cooperated and provided all necessary documentation, including developer logs and project descriptions.

Secondly, the standard for gross negligence is not met. Gross negligence implies a serious disregard for professional duty or a complete lack of care that goes beyond ordinary error. Saffier Software B.V. maintains a robust internal compliance framework. The company regularly reviews its tax positions with external advisors. The discrepancy arose from a complex interpretation of the definition of "technological advancement" under the Innovation Box regime, which is a matter of ongoing legal debate among practitioners. This constitutes a reasonable difference of opinion on the application of the law, not gross negligence.

#### III. Dispute Regarding the New Fact

Furthermore, the Client argues that the adjustment relies on facts that were not sufficiently disclosed in the original return due to ambiguity in the reporting guidelines, rather than an omission by the Client. The technical nature of the software development processes was fully documented and available upon request. The failure to explicitly label these costs as "Innovation Box eligible" in the tax return summary was a clerical oversight by the accounting firm, which does not rise to the level of gross negligence attributable to the legal entity's management.

### IV. Conclusion and Request

Given that the Client acted with due diligence and without intent or gross negligence, the imposition of any penalty is unfounded. The **50%** penalty rate is particularly disproportionate given the cooperative nature of the Client during the inquiry phase.

We therefore request:
1. The withdrawal of the penalty decision in its entirety;
2. In the alternative, a significant reduction of the penalty to EUR 0, acknowledging the absence of fault;
3. A stay of execution regarding the payment of the penalty pending the outcome of this objection procedure.

We trust that you will review this matter fairly and grant our request. We remain at your disposal for any further clarification or discussion.
