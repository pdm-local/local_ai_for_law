Dear Sir or Madam,

We represent Klaver Logistics B.V., hereinafter referred to as "the Client", with its registered office at Transportweg 12, 5611 ER Eindhoven. Pursuant to the relevant provisions of the General Administrative Law Act (Awb) and the State Taxes Act (AWR), we hereby submit this formal notice of objection against the additional assessment of corporate income tax for the fiscal year 2023, issued on 10 September 2025, as well as against the accompanying penalty decision dated 20 September 2025.

The Client contests both the factual basis for the additional assessment and the application of the penalty under Article 67d AWR. Specifically, we dispute the Tax Administration's characterization of the Client's conduct as involving gross negligence or intent, and we reject the assertion that a "new fact" within the meaning of Article 16 AWR exists in this matter.

**1. Factual Background**

The Client operates a logistics and transport company specializing in cold-chain distribution across the Benelux region. For the fiscal year ending 31 December 2023, the Client filed its corporate income tax return on 1 April 2024, declaring a taxable profit of EUR 1,250,000. The additional assessment issued by your office increases this profit to EUR 3,750,000, based on the allegation that certain cross-border service invoices totaling EUR 2,500,000 lacked sufficient substantive justification for the claimed business purpose.

The penalty decision imposes a sanction equal to **50%** of the additional tax due, calculated at EUR 487,500.

**2. Objection to the Additional Assessment and the Alleged "New Fact"**

The Tax Administration relies on Article 16 AWR to justify the additional assessment, claiming it is based on facts that were not known to the Inspector at the time of the initial decision. We firmly oppose this characterization.

At the time of filing the return, the Client had provided all relevant documentation regarding the cross-border services in question, including contracts, proof of payment, and detailed descriptions of the logistical support provided. The invoices were processed through standard due diligence protocols. The fact that the Inspector later re-evaluated the commercial rationale of these transactions does not constitute a "new fact" under Article 16 AWR. Rather, it represents a difference in interpretation regarding the existing facts and documentation already available to the Administration.

To qualify as a new fact, the information must be entirely unknown and unavailable to the Inspector previously. In this case, all source documents were in the Client's file. Therefore, the additional assessment is legally unfounded and should be annulled.

**3. Objection to the Penalty under Article 67d AWR**

Even if the Tribunal were to disagree with our position on the additional assessment (which we maintain is incorrect), the imposition of a penalty under Article 67d AWR is disproportionate and unjustified in this instance.

Article 67d AWR requires that the taxpayer's conduct amounts to gross negligence or intent for such a severe penalty to be applicable. The Client maintains that it acted in good faith. The internal accounting team relied on external advice from an independent tax consultant regarding the deductibility of these specific cross-border costs. This reliance negates any possibility of gross negligence, let alone intent.

Furthermore, the severity of the penalty is disproportionate. A penalty rate of **50%** is reserved for cases of deliberate evasion or willful blindness. Here, the discrepancy arises from a complex interpretation of international tax treaties and domestic deductibility rules, not from an attempt to defraud the Treasury. The Client promptly cooperated with the Inspector's inquiries upon request and provided supplementary explanations without delay.

Imposing a **50%** penalty in a case where the error is merely interpretive and not due to negligence contradicts the principle of proportionality under Article 3:4 Awb. We therefore request that the penalty be reduced to zero, or at the very least, waived entirely given the absence of culpable conduct.

**4. Conclusion and Requests**

In light of the foregoing, we request that the Dutch Tax Administration:

1. Annul the additional assessment of corporate income tax for the fiscal year 2023;
2. Annul the penalty decision under Article 67d AWR in its entirety;
3. Refund any amounts already paid under protest regarding these assessments.

We trust that you will review this objection carefully and revoke the contested decisions accordingly. Should you disagree with our objections, we reserve the right to appeal to the Court of Appeal in The Hague within the statutory period.
