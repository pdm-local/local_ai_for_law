**Subject: Request for clarification on prevailing liability cap in the Service Agreement dated 10 October 2024 between Maaslicht Online B.V. and Vesting Systems B.V.**

Dear mr. Bergsma,

We act on behalf of our client, **Vesting Systems B.V.** (“the Supplier”), regarding the ongoing interpretation of the Master Services Agreement concluded with **Maaslicht Online B.V.** (“the Client”) on 10 October 2024 (hereinafter: “the Agreement”).

A discrepancy has arisen between two provisions of the Agreement concerning the limitation of liability. Clause 8.2 explicitly states that the total aggregate liability of the Supplier shall not exceed €25.000. However, Annex B, which pertains to specific cloud infrastructure maintenance modules, contains a footnote referencing a standard industry cap of €100.000 for high-availability incidents. The Client has invoked the higher cap in its recent claim regarding service downtime on 12 December 2024, arguing that the specialized nature of the service overrides the general clause.

Our client maintains that Clause 8.2 constitutes the primary limitation of liability and that the footnote in Annex B was a drafting error or a non-binding reference to standard terms not explicitly incorporated by reference into the main body of the contract. Nevertheless, to resolve this ambiguity and avoid further dispute, we seek your legal assessment on which provision prevails under Dutch law.

Specifically, we request your opinion on whether the principle of reasonableness and fairness, as codified in **Article 6:248 DCC**, mandates that the higher liability cap be applied to protect the Client’s reasonable expectations, or if the explicit numerical limit in Clause 8.2 remains binding despite the conflicting footnote. The financial exposure for our client is directly linked to this interpretation, as the claimed damages approximate €25.000.

We look forward to your prompt response. Please consider this matter urgent due to the impending deadline for filing a defense against the Client’s claim.
