**Subject:** Re: Service Level Agreement Breach – Uptime Calculation and Damages Claim

Dear Mr. Van der Meer,

Thank you for your email of 12 May 2025 regarding the service interruption experienced on 3 May 2025 when Hanze Networks B.V. (hereinafter "Hanze") provided managed cloud infrastructure services to Lumen Digital B.V. (hereinafter "Lumen").

You have requested clarification on two specific points: first, whether the calculated availability of 99.5% constitutes a breach of the Service Level Agreement (SLA) and triggers a penalty; and second, whether Lumen is entitled to claim compensation for lost revenue resulting from the downtime. We address both matters below.

**1. Calculation of Availability and SLA Penalty**

According to Article 4.2 of the Master Service Agreement signed on 15 January 2024, Hanze guarantees a monthly uptime of 99.9%. The agreement defines availability as the ratio of operational hours to total calendar hours in a billing month, excluding scheduled maintenance windows agreed upon in writing by both parties.

For the month of April 2025, the total number of hours is 744. The outage lasted for 6 hours and 48 minutes (6.8 hours). Scheduled maintenance accounted for 2 hours. Therefore, the non-operational time attributable to Hanze's performance is 6.8 hours.

The calculation proceeds as follows:
*   Total hours: 744
*   Unavailable hours: 6.8
*   Available hours: 737.2
*   Availability percentage: (737.2 / 744) * 100 = 99.08%

You noted in your brief that the internal monitoring reported an availability of 99.5%. However, under the strict terms of the contract, any deviation below 99.9% is considered a breach. The figure of **99.5%** you referenced appears to be an interim metric from your own logs, but even if we were to accept a hypothetical uptime of exactly 99.5%, this remains significantly below the contractual threshold of 99.9%.

Consequently, Hanze is in clear breach of Article 4.2 of the Agreement. The SLA penalty clause (Article 4.3) stipulates that for every 0.1% below the guaranteed uptime, a credit equal to 5% of the monthly service fee is issued. Since the actual uptime was approximately 99.08%, Lumen is entitled to a credit of 82% of the monthly fee for April 2025. We have instructed our client to issue this credit note immediately.

**2. Recoverability of Lost Revenue**

Regarding your claim for lost revenue, we must look to the general principles of Dutch contract law, specifically Article 6:74 DCC (breach / damages) and Article 6:248(2) DCC (reasonableness and fairness).

Article 10.1 of the Agreement contains a limitation of liability clause. It states that Hanze's total liability for any claim arising out of or in connection with this agreement shall not exceed the fees paid by Lumen in the three months preceding the event. More critically, Article 10.2 explicitly excludes indirect damages, including loss of profit, loss of business, or loss of revenue.

Under Dutch law, exclusion of liability for indirect damages is generally valid unless the breach constitutes gross negligence or intent. The outage was caused by a routine software patching error, which we do not consider to rise to the level of gross negligence under Article 6:248 DCC. Furthermore, case law (e.g., HR 30 April 2004, ECLI:NL:HR:2004:AK1555) supports the enforceability of such exclusions in B2B contracts where the parties are deemed to have similar bargaining power and legal awareness.

Therefore, while Lumen is entitled to the SLA credit calculated above, the claim for lost revenue is not recoverable under the current contract terms. To pursue such damages would require proving that Hanze acted with intent or gross negligence, which is a high threshold not met by standard operational errors in IT infrastructure provision.

**Conclusion**

Hanze acknowledges the breach of the SLA regarding uptime and will process a credit of 82% for April 2025. We respectfully decline the claim for lost revenue based on the limitation of liability clauses in the agreement, which are enforceable under Article 6:248 DCC.

We hope this clarifies the legal position. Should you wish to discuss technical improvements to prevent recurrence, we are happy to arrange a meeting between our technical leads and your IT department next week.
