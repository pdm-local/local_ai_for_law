Dear Mr. Nolte,  

We acknowledge receipt of your letter dated 28 March 2025 in which you act on behalf of our insured, Veldhoven Software B.V. (“VSB”), concerning the liability claim brought by Aurora Digital B.V. (“Aurora”) for losses sustained during the service interruption of 15 March 2025.  

We have conducted a thorough investigation into the incident. Based on the technical logs provided and the independent forensic report commissioned by our office, we accept that VSB failed to adhere to the reasonable standard of care required under Article 6:248 DCC and general principles of tort law. Specifically, VSB’s monitoring alerts were disabled due to an internal configuration error, which delayed the detection of the database deadlock. Consequently, Global Shield Insurance Group N.V. hereby admits **partial liability** for the damages suffered by Aurora Digital B.V.  

However, we cannot accept your claim for a total amount of **€ 27.500**. Our assessment indicates that this figure is excessive and does not properly account for the significant contributory negligence of Aurora Digital B.V.  

**1. Dispute on Quantum of Damages**  

The sum of € 27.500 includes € 18.000 for alleged loss of profit and € 9.500 for administrative costs. Our analysis reveals that:  

a) **Loss of Profit:** Aurora failed to provide sufficient evidence linking the three-hour outage directly to a specific lost contract. The provided invoices for “consultancy hours” were generic and not substantiated by client correspondence or work reports. We value the verifiable loss of profit at € 4.500.  

b) **Administrative Costs:** The claimed administrative costs lack detailed time registration. We are only prepared to cover reasonable out-of-pocket expenses, estimated at € 1.200.  

Therefore, our initial calculation of total damages stands at **€ 5.700**.  

**2. Contributory Negligence**  

More critically, we contend that Aurora Digital B.V. bears significant responsibility for the extent of the damage due to its own failure to mitigate losses under Article 6:98 DCC.  

Aurora’s server architecture did not include a redundant failover system during the period in question, despite this being standard industry practice for cloud-based providers. Had Aurora implemented a basic hot-standby solution, the downtime would have been reduced from three hours to less than fifteen minutes. Furthermore, Aurora failed to activate its emergency support ticket until 45 minutes after the outage began, further exacerbating the technical delay.  

Given that Aurora’s negligence contributed substantially to the duration and severity of the business interruption, we argue for a reduction of liability by **60%** on the part of VSB.  

**3. Proposed Settlement**  

Taking into account our admission of partial liability (40%) and the calculated damages of € 5.700:  

*   Total Damages Assessed: € 5.700  
*   Reduction for Contributory Negligence (60%): -€ 3.420  
*   **Net Liability for VSB:** **€ 2.280**  

We hereby propose a final settlement of **€ 2.280**, inclusive of all costs related to this matter, in full and final settlement of Aurora Digital B.V.’s claim against Veldhoven Software B.V.  

This offer is valid until **12 May 2025**. Should we not receive a constructive response by this date, we reserve the right to defend the position that VSB’s liability is minimal or non-existent given the extent of Aurora’s contributory negligence.  

We look forward to resolving this matter amicably and efficiently.
