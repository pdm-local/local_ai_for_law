**Subject:** Advisory Response: Service Level Agreement Breach and Damages Claim against Novael Networks B.V.

Dear Mr. Van Dijk,

Thank you for your email dated 14 March 2025 regarding the recent service outage affecting Orion Interactive B.V.’s infrastructure hosted by Novael Networks B.V. I have reviewed the incident report provided and the relevant provisions of your Service Level Agreement (SLA), specifically clause 4.2.

**1. The 99.8% Availability Metric**

As you noted, the SLA mandates a monthly uptime guarantee of **99.8%**. During the period of 10 March 2025 to 13 March 2025, Novael Networks B.V. experienced a total downtime of 4 hours and 12 minutes. This results in a calculated availability for that month of approximately 99.65%, thereby constituting a clear breach of the contractual uptime guarantee.

Under Article 6:74 of the Dutch Civil Code (DCC), Novael Networks B.V. is liable for non-performance. The SLA explicitly states that in the event of a failure to meet the availability target, Orion Interactive B.V. is entitled to service credits equal to 10% of the monthly fee for every hour of downtime exceeding the threshold. Based on your current monthly contract value of €5,000, this amounts to a direct financial credit of €2,160 (4.2 hours * €120/hour).

**2. Recoverability of Lost Revenue**

You asked whether the lost revenue resulting from the outage is recoverable in addition to the service credits. This depends on the limitation of liability clause within your agreement.

Novael Networks B.V.’s standard terms typically limit liability to the amount paid in the preceding three months, excluding gross negligence or wilful misconduct. However, under Dutch law, specifically Article 6:248 DCC and Article 6:248(2) DCC regarding reasonableness and fairness, a blanket exclusion of consequential damages is often scrutinized heavily if it leaves the injured party with no effective remedy for foreseeable losses.

If we can establish that Novael Networks B.V. failed to adhere to its duty of care under Article 7:401 DCC by not maintaining adequate backup systems during the scheduled maintenance window, we may argue that the limitation of liability is void in this specific instance. Furthermore, Article 6:162 DCC (tort) may provide an alternative basis for claiming full damages if the breach is deemed severe enough to constitute a tortious act independent of the contract.

To pursue this, we need to quantify the "lost revenue" with precise financial records from your e-commerce platform logs for March 10–13, 2025, compared to the same days in February 2025. Without concrete data linking the outage directly to specific lost sales, a claim for lost profit will be difficult to sustain under Article 6:98 DCC regarding causation.

**3. Recommended Next Steps**

1.  **Formal Notice of Defect:** We will draft a formal letter to Novael Networks B.V. demanding the immediate application of the service credits and reserving the right to claim additional damages for lost revenue.
2.  **Evidence Preservation:** Please provide your server logs and financial transaction reports for the affected dates by 18 March 2025.
3.  **Negotiation Strategy:** If Novael Networks B.V. refuses to compensate for lost revenue, we will assess whether invoking Article 6:265 DCC (rescission) or terminating the contract due to fundamental non-performance is viable, potentially triggering a search for alternative providers under your duty to mitigate damages.

I await your financial data to proceed with the demand letter.
