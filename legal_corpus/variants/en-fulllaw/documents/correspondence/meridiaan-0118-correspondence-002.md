**Subject:** Legal assessment of Service Level Agreement breach and damages claim regarding incident on 10 May 2025

Dear Mr. Van der Meer,

Thank you for your email of 13 May 2025 concerning the technical outage experienced by your platform last Monday. As requested, I have reviewed the Service Level Agreement (SLA) concluded between Maaslicht Online B.V. and Vesting Systems B.V., as well as the relevant provisions of Dutch contract law, to address your questions regarding the availability penalty and the recoverability of lost revenue.

**1. The SLA Penalty for Availability**

Your query concerns the calculation of the service credit due to Vesting Systems B.V. following the downtime on 10 May 2025. You noted that the system was unavailable for four hours, resulting in a significant drop in user engagement.

According to Article 4.2 of the SLA, the guaranteed uptime is set at **99.9%** per calendar month. The agreement explicitly states that if the actual availability falls below this threshold, Vesting Systems B.V. is obligated to issue service credits proportional to the duration of the outage. For every hour of unplanned downtime exceeding the allowed tolerance, Maaslicht Online B.V. is entitled to a credit equal to 5% of the monthly recurring fee.

Based on the logs provided by Vesting Systems B.V., the incident lasted exactly four hours. Consequently, you are entitled to a total service credit of 20% of the monthly fee for that month. This amount will be deducted from your next invoice dated 1 June 2025. Please note that this credit constitutes the primary contractual remedy for this specific breach of performance standards.

**2. Recoverability of Lost Revenue**

You further inquired whether Maaslicht Online B.V. can claim compensation for the lost revenue generated during the outage period. I must advise against pursuing such a claim at this stage. Under Dutch law, specifically Article 6:74 DCC, damages are generally awarded to restore the injured party to the position they would have been in had the breach not occurred. However, this is subject to the principle of reasonableness and fairness as codified in Article 6:248 DCC and Article 6:248(2) DCC.

In standard IT service contracts, courts and arbitrators typically view SLA credits as *liquidated damages* (conventionele schadebeding). These clauses are designed to be the exclusive remedy for service failures, provided they are not grossly disproportionate to the actual damage suffered. The European Court of Justice and Dutch case law generally uphold these limitations to ensure legal certainty in commercial relationships.

Furthermore, to recover lost revenue under Article 6:162 DCC or general breach of contract principles, you would need to prove both causation (Article 6:98 DCC) and the directness of the damage. Lost profits are considered indirect damages unless explicitly agreed upon in writing as foreseeable at the time of contracting. Since the SLA does not contain a specific clause waiving the limitation on consequential or lost profit damages, Vesting Systems B.V. is likely protected by this exclusion. Attempting to claim additional revenue loss would likely be dismissed as contrary to the principle of reasonableness and fairness (Article 6:248 DCC), given that the SLA already provides a defined compensation mechanism.

**3. Conclusion and Next Steps**

In summary, your entitlement is limited to the service credit calculated above. I recommend accepting this credit to maintain the business relationship with Vesting Systems B.V., as litigation regarding lost revenue would carry a high risk of failure and additional legal costs under Article 6:233 DCC regarding general terms.

If you wish to negotiate a higher one-time credit or amend the SLA for future contracts to include broader damage coverage, please let me know. I can draft a letter of intent to Vesting Systems B.V. to discuss these terms amicably.
