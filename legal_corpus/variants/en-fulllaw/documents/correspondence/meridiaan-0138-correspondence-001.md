Dear Sir,

We act as legal counsel to Mr. Jan Willem van der Meer in the criminal proceedings concerning allegations of money laundering under Article 420bis Criminal Code, connected to transactions involving Orion Systems B.V. Our client was taken into pre-trial detention on 13 March 2025 pursuant to a warrant issued by the investigating judge at the Rotterdam District Court.

This letter serves as a formal objection to the continued deprivation of liberty of our client and constitutes a request for the immediate suspension of said detention, in accordance with **Article 67 Code of Criminal Procedure**.

### 1. Factual Background and Duration of Detention

The pre-trial detention of Mr. van der Meer has now lasted for exactly **30 days**. During this period, the investigating authorities have conducted several interrogations. While we acknowledge the seriousness of the allegations regarding the financial flows within Orion Systems B.V., the current justification for continued detention no longer meets the stringent requirements imposed by law after this initial phase.

### 2. Legal Objection under Article 67 Code of Criminal Procedure

Article 67 Code of Criminal Procedure provides strict conditions under which a suspect may be held in pre-trial detention beyond the initial period. Specifically, it mandates that continued detention must be necessary to prevent the risk of flight or the risk of tampering with evidence (prejudice to the investigation).

In our assessment, neither of these grounds justifies the extension of detention at this stage:

1.  **Risk of Flight:** Mr. van der Meer is a Dutch national with fixed residence in Eindhoven and has no passport available for travel abroad. He has fully complied with all reporting obligations imposed by the police since his arrest. Furthermore, Orion Systems B.V. has offered substantial collateral guarantees to ensure his availability for future proceedings.
2.  **Risk of Tampering:** The primary evidence in this case consists of digital transaction records from Orion Systems B.V., which are already secured on independent servers under the supervision of a court-appointed expert. Mr. van der Meer has no technical access to alter these records, nor does he have any incentive to tamper with physical evidence given his cooperative stance.

The continued deprivation of liberty beyond **30 days** without new, compelling facts indicating a heightened risk is disproportionate and violates the principle of proportionality inherent in Article 67 Code of Criminal Procedure.

### 3. Request for Suspension of Detention

Given that the initial justification for detention has diminished in weight and no new risks have emerged, we formally request the Public Prosecution Service to:

1.  Immediately recommend to the competent judge that the pre-trial detention of Mr. Jan Willem van der Meer be suspended;
2.  Release Mr. van der Meer from custody on **15 April 2025** at the earliest, subject to standard bail conditions if deemed necessary by the court.

We trust that the Public Prosecution Service will act in accordance with the legal standards set forth in Article 67 Code of Criminal Procedure and prioritize the rights of the defendant.

Please confirm receipt of this letter and inform us of your decision regarding this request no later than **14 April 2025**.
