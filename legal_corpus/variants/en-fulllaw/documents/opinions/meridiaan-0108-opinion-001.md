**CONFIDENTIAL LEGAL OPINION**

**To:** Mr. J. Havelaar, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**From:** Helix Data B.V. (Client)
**Date:** 14 March 2025
**Subject:** Assessment of prospects for dissolution under Article 7:671b DCC versus settlement certainty

### 1. Introduction and Background

Helix Data B.V. (“Helix”) instructs Meridiaan Advocaten to evaluate the legal position regarding the termination of the service agreement dated 10 January 2024 with Forta Logistics B.V. (“Forta”). The core dispute concerns Forta’s failure to deliver integrated logistics software modules by the agreed deadline of 30 November 2024, resulting in significant operational delays for Helix.

This opinion addresses two primary strategic options:
1. Petitioning the subdistrict court (Kantonrechter) to dissolve the contract pursuant to Article 7:671b DCC;
2. Pursuing a negotiated settlement.

Additionally, we assess the risk of Forta claiming additional fair compensation (“voldoende vergoeding”) if dissolution is granted.

### 2. Legal Framework: Dissolution under Article 7:671b DCC

Article 7:671b DCC provides that if a party fails to perform an obligation arising from an employment or service contract, the other party may demand performance within a reasonable period. If performance is not rendered within this period, the aggrieved party may dissolve the contract without judicial intervention by means of a written notice. However, in cases of dispute regarding the existence of grounds for dissolution or the validity of the notice, parties often seek confirmation from the subdistrict court.

In our matter, Helix issued a formal notice to perform on 15 December 2024, granting Forta until 15 January 2025 to deliver the missing modules. Forta failed to comply. Consequently, Helix sent a notice of dissolution via registered mail on 16 January 2025.

Forta has contested this dissolution, arguing that the delay was due to force majeure (unforeseeable server outages) and that partial performance is sufficient. Therefore, judicial confirmation under Article 7:671b DCC is necessary to ensure legal certainty.

### 3. Prospects of Success in Subdistrict Court Proceedings

The subdistrict court will examine whether Forta’s non-performance constitutes a “attributable failure” (toerekenbare tekortkoming) such that dissolution is justified.

#### 3.1 Attributability
Under Dutch law, the burden of proof for force majeure lies with Forta. To succeed, Forta must demonstrate that:
- The event was unforeseeable and irresistible;
- There is a direct causal link between the event and the non-performance.

The server outages cited by Forta occurred during a period of known maintenance schedules in their cloud provider’s infrastructure. Case law suggests that reliance on third-party providers does not automatically exempt one from liability unless the contract explicitly allocates such risks to Helix. The service agreement (Section 4.2) places the risk of technical failures on Forta as the service provider.

**Assessment:** High probability that the court will find Forta’s failure attributable to them. The force majeure defense is weak given the foreseeability of cloud infrastructure issues in modern logistics contracts.

#### 3.2 Reasonableness and Fairness (Redelijkheid en Billijkheid)
Even if a breach exists, Article 6:248 DCC requires that dissolution be reasonable and fair. The court may refuse dissolution if the breach is minor relative to the contract’s value. Here, the missing modules are core to Helix’s operational workflow. The delay has caused concrete financial loss exceeding €150,000. Dissolution is therefore proportionate.

**Conclusion on Prospects:** We estimate a **70-80% chance of success** in obtaining a court declaration that the dissolution by Helix was valid and effective as of 16 January 2025.

### 4. Risk of Additional Fair Compensation (Voldoende Vergoeding)

A critical risk in dissolution proceedings under Article 7:671b DCC is the counterclaim for additional fair compensation. If the court finds that Helix’s own actions contributed to the loss, or if Forta can prove that they would have performed had they received earlier notice, they may claim damages.

#### 4.1 Mitigation and Notice
Helix provided ample notice (15 December 2024). Forta did not respond with a credible alternative plan until after the dissolution notice. Thus, Helix has fulfilled its duty to mitigate loss.

#### 4.2 Quantum of Claim
Forta may claim:
- Unpaid invoices for partially delivered work: €45,000;
- Loss of profit on the full contract value (€200,000): Argued as lost revenue from future phases.

However, under Article 6:98 DCC, causation must be established. Since Forta failed to deliver core components, their right to payment for incomplete work is subject to set-off against Helix’s damages. The court will likely offset the €45,000 claim against Helix’s proven losses (estimated at €120,000).

**Risk Assessment:** Low risk of a net payout to Forta. Highest exposure is a potential order to pay €30,000–€50,000 if the court finds partial performance was substantially beneficial and Helix’s termination was slightly premature in timing. However, given the clear breach, this is unlikely.

### 5. Comparison with Settlement

#### 5.1 Certainty of Settlement
A settlement offers immediate certainty:
- Avoids litigation costs (estimated €20,000–€30,000 in legal fees);
- Prevents reputational risk in the logistics sector;
- Allows for a structured wind-down of data migration.

Proposed settlement terms under negotiation:
- Helix pays €60,000 (waiving unpaid invoices and claiming half damages);
- Mutual release of claims;
- Forta assists in data export for 3 months post-termination.

#### 5.2 Strategic Recommendation
While the legal prospects for dissolution are strong, the **certainty** of a settlement outweighs the marginal benefit of litigation. Litigation takes 6–9 months. The additional recovery from court (if any) is unlikely to exceed €10,000 after costs.

### 6. Conclusion and Advice

1. **Legal Position:** Helix has strong grounds for dissolution under Article 7:671b DCC. The subdistrict court will likely confirm the validity of the termination.
2. **Risk:** Minimal risk of adverse compensation orders, but not zero.
3. **Recommendation:** Accept the proposed settlement terms (€60,000 buyout) to close the matter efficiently. If Forta refuses, proceed with litigation, as the prospects are favorable.

We await your instructions on whether to finalize the settlement agreement or prepare the petition for the subdistrict court in Eindhoven.
