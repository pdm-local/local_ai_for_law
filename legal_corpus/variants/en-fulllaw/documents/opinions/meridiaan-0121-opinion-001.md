**CONFIDENTIAL LEGAL OPINION**

**To:** Board of Directors, Aurora Systems B.V.
**From:** mr. N. Vrugt, Partner at Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**Date:** 12 April 2025
**Re:** Compliance with Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (DSA) regarding Notice-and-Action Systems and Transparency Reporting

### 1. Introduction and Scope

This legal opinion is issued pursuant to your request dated 28 March 2025, concerning the compliance obligations of Aurora Systems B.V. ("Aurora") under European Union law. Specifically, this opinion addresses the applicability and requirements of **Regulation (EU) 2022/2065** (hereinafter "the DSA") to Aurora’s digital services, which include a user-generated content platform and associated hosting services.

Aurora has clarified that it operates as an intermediary service provider, primarily functioning as a hosting service for third-party content. While Aurora does not originate the content hosted on its platform, it exercises control over the infrastructure and the terms of service governing user interactions. Consequently, Aurora falls within the scope of the DSA, particularly regarding the obligations imposed on hosting services and online platforms.

### 2. Classification of Services under the DSA

Under Article 3 of **Regulation (EU) 2022/2065**, intermediary services are categorized based on their function. Aurora’s primary activity involves storing information provided by users. This classifies Aurora as a "hosting service" pursuant to Article 3(c). Furthermore, because Aurora interacts with the public and allows users to engage in transactions or content sharing, it may also be classified as an "online platform."

As an online platform, Aurora is subject to a heightened set of obligations compared to mere hosting services. These obligations are designed to ensure a safe and predictable online environment for all users within the Union. It is crucial to distinguish between the general duties applicable to all intermediary services and the specific transparency and reporting duties applicable to online platforms.

### 3. Notice-and-Action Mechanisms

A core component of the DSA's framework for hosting services is the establishment of effective "notice-and-action" mechanisms. Under Article 16 of **Regulation (EU) 2022/2065**, Aurora is required to put in place and maintain a mechanism enabling third parties to notify it easily and effectively of their alleged illegal content or activities.

#### 3.1. Accessibility and Ease of Use
The notice-and-action mechanism must be explicitly and prominently accessible, either directly from the homepage or through a clearly visible link in the terms of service. The process must be user-friendly and allow for the submission of notices without requiring excessive technical expertise. Aurora’s current system, which relies solely on a generic contact form located in the footer, is insufficient. It must be upgraded to a dedicated legal compliance portal that accepts structured notifications.

#### 3.2. Content of Notices
To be considered valid under the DSA, notices submitted by third parties must contain:
*   A statement of facts indicating why the content is alleged to be illegal;
*   The precise location (URL) of the content in question; and
*   The identity of the notifier, unless anonymity is justified.

Aurora must acknowledge receipt of such notices without undue delay. While the DSA does not prescribe a specific timeframe for acknowledgment, best practice and regulatory expectations suggest acknowledgment within 24 hours.

#### 3.3. Assessment and Decision-Making
Upon receiving a valid notice, Aurora must assess the alleged illegality diligently and in an objective manner. This assessment must consider the fundamental rights of all persons concerned, including freedom of expression and information. If Aurora determines that the content is indeed illegal, it must decide whether to disable access to or remove the content.

Crucially, under Article 16(4) of **Regulation (EU) 2022/2065**, if Aurora decides not to remove or disable access to the content, it must inform the notifier of its reasons for doing so. This transparency is vital to maintain trust and demonstrate compliance with the duty of care.

### 4. Transparency Reporting Obligations

As an online platform, Aurora is subject to strict transparency reporting requirements under Article 26 of **Regulation (EU) 2022/2065**. These reports are public documents designed to allow researchers, civil society organizations, and authorities to assess how Aurora manages risks associated with its services.

#### 4.1. Frequency and Content
Aurora must publish an annual transparency report. The first such report, covering the calendar year 2024, must be published by 15 February 2025. Subsequent reports must cover the preceding calendar year.

The report must include:
*   **Content Moderation:** Detailed statistics on the actions taken regarding illegal content, including the number of notices received, the outcomes of those notices, and the methods used for detection (e.g., automated vs. manual).
*   **Flagging Mechanisms:** Information on how users can flag content and the response times achieved.
*   **Risk Management:** A description of the systemic risks identified in Aurora’s risk assessment pursuant to Article 27, including measures taken to mitigate these risks. This includes risks related to the dissemination of illegal content and any adverse effects on the exercise of fundamental rights.
*   **Cooperation with Authorities:** Information on how Aurora cooperates with trusted flaggers and competent public authorities, as required under Articles 21 and 22 of the DSA.

#### 4.2. Language and Accessibility
The transparency report must be published in a clearly accessible location on Aurora’s platform, typically within the legal or compliance section of the website. It should be available in English and Dutch to ensure accessibility for users in the Netherlands and international stakeholders. The format should be machine-readable where possible to facilitate external analysis.

### 5. Cooperation with Authorities and Trusted Flaggers

Aurora must establish contact points for rapid communication with competent authorities and designated "trusted flaggers." Trusted flaggers are entities that have been verified by national digital services coordinators as having specialized expertise in identifying and reporting illegal content.

Under Article 21 of **Regulation (EU) 2022/2065**, notices from trusted flaggers must be prioritized. Aurora must give these notices preferential treatment, ensuring that they are processed more quickly than standard user notifications. Failure to prioritize these notices can result in significant reputational damage and regulatory scrutiny.

### 6. Conclusion and Recommendations

Based on the current operational structure of Aurora Systems B.V., the firm identifies several areas of non-compliance with **Regulation (EU) 2022/2065**. The most critical gaps lie in the accessibility and functionality of the notice-and-action system and the completeness of the transparency reporting framework.

We recommend the following immediate actions:
1.  **Develop a Dedicated Notice Portal:** Replace the generic contact form with a specialized legal compliance portal that accepts structured notices and provides automated acknowledgments.
2.  **Review Assessment Protocols:** Establish clear internal guidelines for the assessment of illegal content, ensuring respect for fundamental rights and documentation of decision-making processes.
3.  **Prepare the First Transparency Report:** Compile data for the 2024 calendar year and draft the first annual transparency report in accordance with Article 26 of the DSA.
4.  **Designate Contact Points:** Formalize contact points for authorities and trusted flaggers, ensuring they are operational and monitored during business hours.

Failure to implement these measures may expose Aurora to administrative fines imposed by national digital services coordinators, which can reach up to 6% of the total worldwide annual turnover of the undertaking.
