**MEMORANDUM OF ADVICE**

**To:** Maaslicht Interactive B.V.
**From:** mr. P. Slootweg, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**Date:** 15 March 2025
**Subject:** Prospects of appeal against the administrative penalty decision (Reference: 2025-ADM-EIN-892)

### 1. Introduction and Scope

You have instructed us to provide a legal opinion regarding the administrative penalty imposed on your company, Maaslicht Interactive B.V., by the Municipality of Eindhoven (hereinafter: "the Authority"). The penalty amounts to € 50.000 and was imposed due to alleged violations of municipal ordinances concerning public space usage during your recent promotional event at Kennedyplein.

This opinion focuses specifically on the legal prospects of filing an appeal before the Administrative Law Section of the Rotterdam Court (hereinafter: "the Court") under Article 8:1 GALA, assuming that your objection against the penalty decision will be rejected by the Authority.

### 2. Relevant Facts and Procedural History

On 10 November 2024, the Municipality of Eindhoven issued a penalty notice for the unauthorized occupation of public land without a valid permit. The total amount assessed is € 50.000. On 5 December 2024, we submitted an objection on your behalf, arguing that a verbal agreement had been reached with a senior civil servant and that the Authority failed to exercise due care in verifying this arrangement prior to issuing the penalty.

On 1 March 2025, the Authority rejected the objection. The rejection letter states that no valid permit was issued, that no binding verbal commitment could override statutory permitting requirements, and that the penalty is proportionate given the scale of the event and the lack of prior authorization. You have requested our assessment of your chances of overturning this decision at the judicial stage.

### 3. Legal Framework: The Appeal under Article 8:1 GALA (Awb)

The primary legal basis for appealing administrative decisions in the Netherlands is Article 8:1 GALA (Awb). This article grants any person with an interest the right to appeal a decision, rule, or act of a public law body to the court.

In proceedings under Article 8:1 GALA, the Court conducts a full review (*volle beoordeling*). This means the Court is not bound by the Authority’s assessment of facts or the merits of the case. The Court will examine:
1.  Whether the decision was properly prepared and supported by adequate reasoning;
2.  Whether the decision conforms to the law;
3.  Whether the decision is reasonable and fair in light of the relevant circumstances (reasonableness and fairness test under Article 6:248 DCC).

### 4. Substantive Assessment of Prospects

#### 4.1. Legality of the Penalty Imposition

The Authority must demonstrate that a violation of municipal law occurred. In administrative penalty cases, the burden of proof regarding the violation lies with the Authority. However, once a violation is established, the assessment of whether a penalty should be imposed and its amount falls within the Authority’s margin of appreciation (*beleidsvrijheid*).

The Municipality has cited specific articles of the Eindhoven Municipal Ordinance on Public Space. Our review indicates that these provisions are valid and applicable to your activities. The argument regarding a "verbal agreement" is legally weak in administrative law, as municipal permits generally require formal written application and approval to ensure legal certainty for all parties.

#### 4.2. Proportionality and the Amount of € 50.000

The most critical aspect of this appeal will be the proportionality of the penalty. Under the General Administrative Law Act (Awb) and general principles of administrative law, penalties must be proportional to the severity of the violation, the culpability of the offender, and the need for deterrence.

The amount of € 50.000 is significant. To challenge this successfully, we must argue that this amount violates the principle of proportionality. We will likely rely on the following points:
*   **Lack of Prior Intent:** You can demonstrate that the violation was not intentional but resulted from a misunderstanding with Authority staff. While this does not negate the violation, it reduces culpability.
*   **No Actual Harm:** The event did not result in physical damage to public infrastructure or safety risks. The violation was purely procedural (lack of permit).
*   **Disparity in Enforcement:** If we can identify cases where similar violations by other entities resulted in significantly lower penalties, this would support an argument that the penalty is disproportionately high relative to comparable cases.

#### 4.3. Procedural Defects

We will also scrutinize whether the Authority complied with procedural requirements, such as the right to be heard prior to imposing the final penalty. If the Authority failed to give you a reasonable opportunity to respond to their preliminary findings before issuing the € 50.000 penalty, this constitutes a substantial procedural defect that could lead to the annulment of the decision under Article 3:44 GALA.

### 5. Strategic Recommendations

Given the full review power of the Court under Article 8:1 GALA, we have moderate prospects of success in reducing the penalty, though complete annulment is less likely given the clear procedural violation (lack of permit).

We recommend the following strategy for the appeal:
1.  **Focus on Proportionality:** The primary argument should be that € 50.000 is excessive for a first-time, non-intentional procedural violation with no negative externalities.
2.  **Evidence of Mitigation:** Submit all correspondence with municipal staff to substantiate the claim of misunderstanding and good faith.
3.  **Request for Reduction:** Explicitly request that the Court exercise its power under Article 8:71 GALA (Awb) to reduce the penalty to a symbolic amount, such as € 500 or € 1.000, reflecting the procedural nature of the breach.

### 6. Conclusion

While the violation itself is difficult to contest due to the absence of a written permit, the severity of the € 50.000 penalty is vulnerable to judicial review under Article 8:1 GALA (Awb). The Court has broad discretion to adjust penalties it deems disproportionate. We estimate a reasonable chance of securing a significant reduction in the penalty amount through the appeal process.

We await your instructions on whether to proceed with drafting the appeal petition.
