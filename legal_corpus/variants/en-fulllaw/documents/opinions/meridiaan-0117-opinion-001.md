**CONFIDENTIAL LEGAL OPINION**

**To:** mr. H. Yildiz, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**From:** Meridiaan Advocaten B.V., Migration / Asylum Department
**Date:** 12 April 2025
**Re:** Matter 2025.0117 — Aurora Software B.V. and Mr. Alexei Volkov
**Subject:** Prospects of Appeal Against IND Decision No. IND-2025-0315

### 1. Introduction and Scope

Meridiaan Advocaten has been instructed by Aurora Software B.V. to provide a legal opinion regarding the adverse decision issued by the Immigration and Naturalisation Service (IND) on 15 March 2025. The IND rejected the asylum application of Mr. Alexei Volkov, employed as Chief Technology Officer at Aurora Software B.V., and declared him undesirable for the Netherlands under Article 67 of the Aliens Act 2000.

The client seeks to challenge this decision through an administrative appeal. This opinion evaluates the legal viability of the proposed strategy: combining a claim for asylum protection with a strong reliance on private and family life under Article 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR).

### 2. Factual Background

Mr. Alexei Volkov arrived in the Netherlands on 10 January 2023. He holds Russian nationality but claims fear of persecution in his home country due to his political activism and previous employment with a state-owned enterprise from which he defected. He registered as an asylum seeker on 12 January 2023.

In parallel, Mr. Volkov entered into an indefinite employment contract with Aurora Software B.V., located at Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven, effective 1 June 2023. His annual gross salary is €95,000. He has been central to the development of Aurora’s proprietary cloud infrastructure, a project critical to the company’s operations.

On 15 March 2025, the IND issued its decision (ref: IND-2025-0315) stating that Mr. Volkov does not qualify for refugee status under Article 29 of the Aliens Act 2000, as the risk of persecution was deemed insufficiently substantiated. Furthermore, the IND determined that his residence permit should be withdrawn due to "public order" concerns related to a minor administrative infraction during his initial entry process in 2023, without conducting a thorough proportionality assessment regarding his family ties.

Mr. Volkov is married to Ms. Elena Volkova (Dutch national) and has two children aged 4 and 7, all residing at the shared family home in Eindhoven. The family has lived continuously in the Netherlands for over two years.

### 3. Legal Analysis

#### 3.1 Asylum Claim under Article 29 Aliens Act 2000

The primary ground for rejection was the refusal of refugee status. Under **Article 29 Aliens Act 2000**, a person is recognized as a refugee if they have a well-founded fear of persecution on account of race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group, or political opinion.

While the IND found the evidence regarding political activism in Russia to be credible, they concluded that internal flight alternatives existed within Russian territory. However, recent jurisprudence from the Council of State indicates that for individuals involved in high-profile dissent against state-controlled entities, the risk of persecution is often considered widespread and severe, rendering internal relocation ineffective.

**Prospect:** Moderate to Strong. The appeal court may find that the IND failed to adequately assess the specific threat posed to Mr. Volkov given his role and public profile. However, relying solely on this ground is risky due to the strict evidentiary standards applied to "internal flight alternatives."

#### 3.2 Family Life under Article 8 ECHR

The stronger legal track lies in the claim for residence based on family life. Mr. Volkov has established a family unit with his Dutch wife and children in the Netherlands. The right to respect for private and family life is protected under **Article 8 ECHR**.

According to established case law of the European Court of Human Rights, expulsion of a lawful resident can only be justified if it is "in accordance with the law," pursues a legitimate aim (such as national security or public order), and is "necessary in a democratic society." The latter requires a proportionality assessment balancing the individual’s family life against the state’s interest in immigration control.

**Key Factors Favoring Mr. Volkov:**
1.  **Duration of Residence:** He has resided in the Netherlands for over two years, establishing deep social and family ties.
2.  **Family Unity:** His wife and children are Dutch nationals. Expulsion would force the entire family to leave the Netherlands, causing a severe disruption to their lives. The children’s integration into the Dutch school system is significant.
3.  **Dependency:** Mr. Volkov is the primary breadwinner (€95,000 annual salary) and active caregiver. His removal would severely impact the family’s financial stability and emotional well-being.
4.  **Public Order Grounds:** The IND cited a minor administrative infraction. In light of his professional importance to Aurora Software B.V. and the lack of any criminal record or serious security threat, the severity of expulsion is disproportionate to the administrative offense.

**Prospect:** Strong. The combination of long-term residence, Dutch family members, and the minor nature of the alleged violation creates a high threshold for the state to justify expulsion. The appeal court is likely to find that removing Mr. Volkov violates Article 8 ECHR unless the IND can demonstrate a pressing social need that outweighs these ties, which is unlikely given the facts.

### 4. Strategic Recommendation

We advise filing an appeal with the Regional Court of ‘s-Hertogenbosch within six weeks of the decision date (i.e., by 29 April 2025). The appeal should focus primarily on the violation of **Article 8 ECHR**, arguing that the IND failed to conduct a proper proportionality test.

The asylum claim under **Article 29 Aliens Act 2000** should be included as a subsidiary argument, highlighting the inconsistencies in the IND’s assessment of internal flight alternatives. The professional indispensability of Mr. Volkov to Aurora Software B.V. can serve as contextual evidence of his integration and lack of threat to public order.

We recommend submitting supporting documents from Aurora Software B.V. confirming Mr. Volkov’s role, salary, and the critical nature of his projects, alongside school records and medical certificates for his children to substantiate the family life claim.

### 5. Conclusion

The prospects of success in the appeal are favorable if the legal strategy prioritizes the **Article 8 ECHR** family life claim. The IND’s decision appears disproportionate given the deep-rooted family ties and the minor nature of the grounds for expulsion. We recommend proceeding with the appeal immediately to preserve Mr. Volkov’s right to remain in the Netherlands pending the court’s ruling.
