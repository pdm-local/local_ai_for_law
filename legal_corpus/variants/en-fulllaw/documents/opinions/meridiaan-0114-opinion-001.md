### 1. Introduction and Background

Meridiaan Advocaten has been instructed by Hanze Data B.V. ("the Client") to provide a legal opinion regarding the administrative penalty imposed by the Municipality of Eindhoven ("the Authority") on 10 February 2025. The penalty was levied due to alleged violations of environmental regulations concerning the disposal of industrial electronic waste at the Client’s facility located at Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven.

The Authority has determined that the Client failed to properly document the chain of custody for hazardous materials, resulting in a fine of € 25.000. As requested, we have reviewed the case file, including the decision letter and the supporting evidence provided by the Inspectorate. We also anticipate the outcome of the objection procedure filed on 28 February 2025. Based on our assessment of the Authority’s reasoning and current administrative law jurisprudence, it is highly probable that the objection will be dismissed. Consequently, this opinion focuses on the prospects of success in an appeal to the administrative court pursuant to Article 8:1 GALA (Awb).

### 2. Legal Framework for Appeal

The right to appeal an administrative decision to the court is governed by Article 8:1 GALA (Awb). This provision grants any interested party the standing to appeal a decision that affects their legal interests adversely. In cases involving administrative penalties, the administrative court conducts a full review (*volledige toetsing*). This means the court examines both the factual circumstances and the legal justification of the penalty de novo. The court is not bound by the Authority’s assessment of facts or law, although it will typically give significant weight to the Authority’s technical expertise regarding regulatory compliance.

The grounds for appeal must challenge either:
1.  The factual basis of the violation;
2.  The legal qualification of those facts; or
3.  The proportionality and correctness of the penalty amount.

### 3. Analysis of Prospects on Appeal

#### 3.1 Factual Basis and Liability

The Authority’s decision rests on inspection reports dated 5 January 2025, which clearly indicated that waste streams were mixed in a manner prohibited under the Environmental Management Decree. The Client’s objection argued that the mixing was accidental and due to staff turnover during the holiday season. However, internal emails obtained by the Inspectorate show that the responsible site manager, Mr. J. van den Berg, instructed junior staff to bypass standard segregation protocols to meet production targets in December 2024.

In an appeal, it is difficult to dispute these objective facts. The administrative court will likely uphold the finding that Hanze Data B.V. failed to meet its duty of care. Strict liability applies to corporate entities regarding environmental compliance. Therefore, appealing on the grounds that no violation occurred has negligible prospects of success.

#### 3.2 Legal Qualification

The Authority qualified the actions as a "serious breach" under the Municipal Sanctions Ordinance. The Client argues it should be classified as "minor." However, given the volume of hazardous waste involved and the lack of immediate corrective measures post-inspection, the classification as serious is legally defensible. Courts generally respect the Authority’s discretion in interpreting regulatory severity unless the interpretation is manifestly unreasonable. This ground for appeal is weak.

#### 3.3 Proportionality and Amount of Penalty (€ 25.000)

The most viable avenue for appeal lies in challenging the proportionality of the penalty amount. The Authority applied the standard sanction table, resulting in a fine of € 25.000. Under Article 8:1 GALA (Awb), the court can reduce the penalty if it is disproportionate to the gravity of the violation, the degree of fault, or the Client’s financial situation.

**Arguments for Reduction:**
1.  **First-Time Offense:** Hanze Data B.V. has no prior administrative penalties in the last five years. The Authority acknowledged this but gave limited weight. In previous case law (e.g., Council of State judgment 2023-112), first-time offenses warrant a significant discount, often reducing penalties by 50%.
2.  **Cooperation:** After the initial inspection, the Client fully cooperated with subsequent audits and implemented new compliance software by January 15, 2025. This remediation should mitigate the penalty.
3.  **Financial Impact:** The fine represents a substantial portion of the Company’s quarterly profit margin. While not insurmountable, it strains liquidity.

**Counter-Arguments from the Authority:**
The Authority may argue that the intentional nature of the instruction (to bypass protocols) aggravates the offense. If the court accepts this aggravating factor, it may uphold the € 25.000 amount or reduce it only marginally to € 17.500.

### 4. Conclusion and Recommendations

Based on the above analysis, the prospects for overturning the decision entirely are low due to clear factual evidence of non-compliance. However, there are **moderate prospects** for a significant reduction of the penalty amount through appeal under Article 8:1 GALA (Awb).

We recommend proceeding with the appeal if the objection is dismissed, specifically focusing on:
1.  Arguing that the "serious" classification was too harsh given the immediate remediation; and
2.  Demanding a reduction of the penalty from € 25.000 to approximately € 12.500 based on first-time offense status and cooperation.

We advise initiating the appeal within six weeks of the rejection of the objection, currently expected by 1 April 2025. Please confirm instructions to draft the appeal petition upon receipt of the objection decision.
