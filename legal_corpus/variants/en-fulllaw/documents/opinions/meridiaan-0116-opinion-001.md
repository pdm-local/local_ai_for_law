**To:** Orion Media B.V.
**From:** Meridiaan Advocaten B.V., represented by mr. E. Nolte (Personal Injury / Tort)
**Date:** 14 March 2025
**Subject:** Legal Opinion regarding the claim against Tinexus Media B.V. for damages related to service interruption and data corruption

### 1. Introduction and Mandate

You have instructed Meridiaan Advocaten B.V. to provide a legal opinion on the viability of a civil claim against Tinexus Media B.V. ("Tinexus") following the severe operational failure that occurred between 10 February 2025 and 17 February 2025. The incident resulted in the unavailability of your media streaming platform, "StreamLine," and the partial corruption of archived content libraries.

This opinion addresses two primary components of your claim:
1. The recoverability of material damages totaling € 310.000;
2. The recoverability of non-material damages amounting to € 12.500;
3. The risk of a reduction in compensation due to contributory negligence on the part of Orion Media B.V. ("Orion").

### 2. Legal Framework: Tortious Liability

The basis for your claim is found in Article 6:162 of the Dutch Civil Code (DCC), which establishes liability for unlawful acts. For liability to attach, four cumulative requirements must be met: an unlawful act, attributability, damage, and a causal link between the act and the damage.

In this matter, the unlawfulness is established through Tinexus’s breach of its contractual and statutory duties of care regarding the maintenance and security of the cloud hosting infrastructure it provided to Orion. As a professional service provider, Tinexus is held to a high standard of care under Article 6:248(2) DCC (reasonableness and fairness). The failure to maintain redundant backup systems during a scheduled maintenance window constitutes a breach of this duty.

Crucially, regarding the quantification of damages, we must apply Article 6:107 DCC, which governs the assessment of the extent of damage and the manner in which it is compensated. This article requires that compensation be sufficient to fully cover the loss suffered, provided such loss is attributable to the unlawful act.

### 3. Assessment of Material Damages (€ 310.000)

You claim material damages of € 310.000, comprising lost advertising revenue, emergency IT remediation costs, and client compensation payouts.

**3.1. Causation**
Under Article 6:98 DCC, a causal link exists if the unlawful act can be regarded as an *adequate cause* of the damage. It is highly probable that a court would find that the seven-day outage directly caused the loss of advertising impressions and subscription cancellations. The chain of causation is not broken by any intervening events, provided that Orion took reasonable steps to mitigate losses (e.g., issuing public statements, activating contingency protocols).

**3.2. Certainty and Quantification**
The amount of € 310.000 must be supported by concrete evidence. We have reviewed your preliminary invoices from the external forensic IT firm "CyberSafe BV" (dated 20 February 2025) totaling € 45.000 for data recovery, and your internal financial audit showing a revenue dip of € 265.000 compared to the same period in 2024. Given the specificity of these figures, the court will likely accept the material damage claim in full, assuming the forensic report confirms Tinexus’s infrastructure error as the sole root cause.

### 4. Assessment of Non-Material Damages (€ 12.500)

You seek € 12.500 in non-material damages for reputational harm and loss of confidence among your business partners.

**4.1. Legal Admissibility**
Under Article 6:106 DCC, a claim for non-material damages (immateriële schade) is admissible when the unlawful act has caused significant distress, reputational injury, or loss of confidence that cannot be adequately compensated through material damages alone. Dutch case law recognizes that severe reputational damage to a commercial entity can give rise to such compensation if it affects the company’s standing in the market.

**4.2. Viability of the Claim**
The amount of € 12.500 is modest and proportionate to the scale of Orion Media B.V. Given the specific mention of lost partnerships with two major distributors (GlobalStream Ltd. and EuroMedia AG), we advise that this claim is viable. The court will consider the extent to which Tinexus’s failure directly impacted your brand equity. We expect a judge to award a symbolic or moderate amount in this range, acknowledging the stress and business uncertainty caused by the outage.

### 5. Risk of Contributory Negligence

The most significant risk to your claim is a reduction based on contributory negligence (mede-toerekenbare oorzaak). Tinexus will likely argue that Orion failed to implement sufficient local backups or disaster recovery protocols as required by industry standards.

**5.1. Analysis of Orion’s Conduct**
Internal emails dated 05 February 2025 indicate that Orion’s CTO, Ms. J. Van Der Meer, delayed the approval of a secondary backup subscription until after the outage occurred. Tinexus may use this to argue that Orion assumed an unnecessary risk by relying solely on their single provider.

**5.2. Predicted Reduction**
If the court accepts Tinexus’s argument that Orion failed to mitigate its own risk adequately, it may apply a proportional reduction under Article 6:98 DCC regarding the extent of causation. However, given that Tinexus is the professional party with superior technical knowledge and control over the infrastructure, the primary liability remains with them. We estimate that a court would reduce Orion’s compensation by no more than 20%.

**5.3. Impact on Total Claim**
*   **Material Damages:** € 310.000 minus 20% = € 248.000.
*   **Non-Material Damages:** € 12.500 (less likely to be reduced as reputation is directly tied to the provider's failure).

Even with a 20% reduction, the net recoverable amount remains substantial at approximately € 260.500.

### 6. Conclusion and Advice

The claim against Tinexus Media B.V. is legally viable and has a high probability of success in litigation or settlement negotiations. The causal link between their negligence and your damages is strong under Article 6:98 DCC, and the quantification of material losses at € 310.000 is well-supported by documentation.

Regarding the non-material damages, the request for € 12.500 is reasonable and supported by Article 6:106 DCC principles regarding the broader impact of their breach. The risk of a contributory negligence reduction exists but is manageable; it will not defeat the claim entirely.

**Recommendation:**
We advise you to send a formal letter of demand to Tinexus Media B.V. claiming the full amount of € 322.500 (€ 310.000 + € 12.500). In this letter, we will explicitly reference their breach of duty and the adequacy of the causal link under Article 6:98 DCC. We anticipate that Tinexus, fearing legal costs and reputational damage, will agree to settle for approximately 85-90% of the claimed amount.

Should they refuse, we are prepared to initiate proceedings in the District Court of Eindhoven.
