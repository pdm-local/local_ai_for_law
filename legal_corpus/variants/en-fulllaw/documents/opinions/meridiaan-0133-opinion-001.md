**CONFIDENTIAL LEGAL OPINION**

**To:** Board of Directors, Maaslicht Software B.V.
**From:** mr. L. Okafor, Privacy / GDPR Practice Group, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**Date:** 14 May 2025
**Subject:** Necessity of a Data Protection Impact Assessment for the 'NeuroMatch' AI Recruitment Module

### 1. Introduction and Instructions

Maaslicht Software B.V. ("the Client") has requested our legal opinion regarding the compliance obligations associated with the upcoming launch of its proprietary AI-driven recruitment module, "NeuroMatch." The Client intends to process applicants' facial micro-expressions and voice tonality to generate suitability scores for job placements.

This opinion addresses a single question: Is a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) required under Article 35 GDPR for this specific processing activity?

### 2. Facts of the Matter

The Client’s technical architecture involves the following processing activities:
1.  **Data Types:** The system captures video and audio recordings of job applicants during remote interviews. It extracts biometric data points (facial geometry, pupil dilation, voice stress patterns) to infer emotional states and cognitive load.
2.  **Automated Decision-Making:** The processed biometric data is fed into a proprietary machine learning model that generates a "Hire/Reject" recommendation score without human intervention in the initial phase.
3.  **Risk Profile:** Biometric data is classified as special category data under Article 9 GDPR. The processing involves systematic monitoring of individuals on a large scale to profile their psychological traits for employment purposes.

### 3. Legal Framework: Article 35 GDPR

Article 35 GDPR imposes a mandatory obligation to conduct a DPIA prior to processing when the processing is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons.

The regulation explicitly lists criteria that indicate the need for a DPIA. According to the guidance issued by the European Data Protection Board (EDPB), a DPIA is required when processing involves:
*   Systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects based on automated processing, including profiling, which produces legal effects or similarly significantly affects the data subject.
*   Processing on a large scale of special categories of data (such as biometric data for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person).

### 4. Assessment

We must evaluate whether the 'NeuroMatch' processing meets the threshold of "high risk."

**A. Nature of the Data**
The processing involves biometric data derived from video and audio recordings. Under Dutch and EU law, biometric data used for identification or psychological profiling is highly sensitive. The unauthorized disclosure or misuse of such data carries significant risks of discrimination and identity theft.

**B. Purpose and Context**
The purpose is recruitment. This creates a clear imbalance of power between the employer (or platform) and the applicant. Applicants are often in a vulnerable position, compelled to submit to invasive monitoring to secure employment. The inference of psychological traits from facial expressions lacks robust scientific validity and poses a high risk of biased or erroneous outcomes.

**C. Likelihood and Severity of Risk**
The risk is not merely hypothetical. Errors in biometric analysis can lead to wrongful exclusion from the labor market (severity). Given that Maaslicht Software B.V. plans to deploy this tool across the EU market, the scale is substantial. The combination of sensitive data, automated profiling, and significant societal impact confirms a high likelihood of harm.

**D. Conclusion on Necessity**
Based on the above, the processing operations described are explicitly covered by the criteria requiring a DPIA. Specifically, the systematic and extensive profiling using special category data triggers the mandatory requirement under Article 35 GDPR.

It is therefore legally insufficient to rely on general privacy notices or standard terms of service. The Client must document the risks and the measures envisaged to address them before commencing any such processing.

### 5. Recommendations

1.  **Immediate Suspension:** Do not process biometric data for 'NeuroMatch' until the DPIA is completed and reviewed by the Data Protection Officer (DPO).
2.  **Conducting the DPIA:** The Client must draft a DPIA that details:
    *   The processing operations and purposes.
    *   The necessity and proportionality of the processing.
    *   The risks to data subjects.
    *   The safeguards, security measures, and mechanisms in place to mitigate these risks.
3.  **Consultation:** If the DPIA indicates that the remaining risk is high despite proposed mitigation measures, the Client must consult the supervisory authority (the Dutch Authority for Personal Data, Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens) prior to processing, pursuant to Article 36 GDPR.
4.  **Alternative Approach:** We strongly advise reconsidering the use of biometric data. Less intrusive methods (e.g., standard CV parsing or structured interview scores) may achieve the Client’s business goals with significantly lower legal risk and no requirement for a DPIA.

### 6. Final Opinion

**Yes, a Data Protection Impact Assessment is strictly required under Article 35 GDPR for the intended 'NeuroMatch' processing.** Failure to conduct this assessment constitutes a violation of the GDPR and exposes Maaslicht Software B.V. to administrative fines and civil liability.
