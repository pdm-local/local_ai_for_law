**Re:** Matter no. 2025.0132 — Advisory Opinion on Liability Cap Enforcement and Statutory Exceptions

### 1. Introduction and Scope

This opinion addresses the legal risks surrounding the enforcement of Clause 14.2 (“Limitation of Liability”) in the Master Services Agreement (MSA) dated 12 January 2024 between Lumen Digital B.V. (“Lumen”) and Hanze Networks B.V. (“Hanze”). Specifically, we evaluate the prospects of renegotiating the agreed maximum liability cap of €50,000 given the recent data corruption incident on 10 April 2025, which resulted in significant operational downtime for Lumen. Furthermore, we assess the fallback position under Dutch statutory law, specifically Article 6:248(2) DCC, should renegotiation fail.

### 2. Current Contractual Position: The Liability Cap

Under Clause 14.2 of the MSA, Hanze’s aggregate liability for any and all claims arising from the contract is strictly capped at the total fees paid by Lumen in the preceding six months. As of the date of this opinion, this amount stands at €50,000. The actual direct damages incurred by Lumen due to the service failure are estimated at €320,000.

Contractually, Hanze is entitled to rely on this cap. However, Dutch contract law imposes strict limits on the enforceability of limitation clauses. The core legal question is whether the conduct giving rise to the damage falls outside the scope of protection intended by the cap.

### 3. Renegotiation Prospects

Given the severity of the incident and the potential reputational damage to both parties, negotiation remains the preferred first step. Lumen should approach Hanze with a formal request to waive the liability cap for this specific incident.

The leverage for Lumen lies in:
1. The breach of SLA uptime guarantees (99.9% availability was not met for 72 hours).
2. The potential argument that the limitation clause is unreasonably onerous given the nature of the failure.

It is likely that Hanze will resist a full waiver but may agree to a partial settlement, perhaps capping liability at €150,000 as a goodwill gesture to preserve the commercial relationship. This outcome is probable if Lumen can demonstrate that Hanze’s internal compliance protocols were ignored during the incident.

### 4. Fallback Position: Article 6:248(2) DCC

If negotiations fail and Hanze insists on the €50,000 cap, Lumen must consider litigation. The primary legal instrument to bypass the contractual cap is **Article 6:248(2) DCC** (Reasonableness and Fairness).

#### 4.1 Legal Standard
Article 6:248(2) DCC provides that a rule of law binding on the parties shall not apply if its strict adherence would be unacceptable according to standards of reasonableness and fairness. The Supreme Court has established that limitation clauses may be deemed unenforceable if they contradict the core purpose of the obligation or if the breach is attributable to intent (*opzet*) or willful recklessness (*kennelijk onverschilligheid*).

#### 4.2 Application to Facts
To successfully invoke Article 6:248(2) DCC, Lumen must prove that Hanze’s employees acted with intent or willful recklessness. Evidence suggests the following:
- The system warning was ignored by two senior engineers on duty.
- Standard redundancy protocols were manually disabled for “maintenance ease” without documentation.

If it can be proven that the damage resulted from *kennelijk onverschilligheid* (conscious disregard of a serious risk), the liability cap is likely to be struck down as unreasonable. The courts generally hold that parties cannot contract out of liability for damages caused by their own willful recklessness, as this would violate fundamental principles of fair dealing.

#### 4.3 Burden of Proof
Lumen bears the burden of proof regarding Hanze’s internal decision-making process. We recommend securing internal emails and system logs from 9-10 April 2025 that show the deliberate disabling of safeguards. Without this evidence, a court may view the incident as ordinary negligence, in which case the cap would likely remain enforceable under Article 6:74 DCC regarding the scope of liability.

### 5. Strategic Recommendations

1. **Preserve Evidence:** Immediately secure all server logs and internal communications from Hanze regarding the April 2025 incident.
2. **Formal Demand:** Send a formal notice to Hanze’s legal department on 15 May 2025, demanding a meeting to discuss liability waiver based on the unreasonableness of the cap in light of willful recklessness.
3. **Litigation Readiness:** Prepare a claim for full damages under Article 6:162 DCC (tort) and breach of contract, explicitly citing Article 6:248(2) DCC as the basis for disregarding Clause 14.2.

### 6. Conclusion

The liability cap is not absolute. While Hanze will initially rely on it, the statutory framework of Article 6:248(2) DCC offers a robust mechanism to challenge its enforcement if we can demonstrate that their actions amounted to willful recklessness. The prospects of success in litigation are moderate to strong, provided the evidence of intentional disregard for safety protocols is admissible and convincing.

We advise proceeding with negotiations from a position of strength, clearly indicating our readiness to litigate based on these statutory grounds.
