**CONFIDENTIAL LEGAL OPINION**

**To:** The Board of Directors, Duinrand Networks B.V.
**From:** mr. M. Terhorst, Partner at Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**Date:** 14 May 2025
**Re:** Matter No. 2025.0127 – Director’s Liability and Prospects for Inquiry Proceedings

### 1. Introduction

You have instructed Meridiaan Advocaten B.V. to provide a legal opinion concerning the liability exposure of the directors of Duinrand Networks B.V. (hereinafter: "Duinrand") regarding a specific dividend distribution made to its shareholder, Cobalt Labs B.V. (hereinafter: "Cobalt"). The central issue is whether this distribution complied with the solvency test required under Dutch corporate law and whether the conduct of the directors constitutes a serious mismanagement justifying the opening of inquiry proceedings.

The facts relevant to our analysis are as follows: On 15 January 2025, the General Meeting of Duinrand resolved to distribute a dividend of € 480.000 to Cobalt Labs B.V. At the time of this resolution, Duinrand had accumulated losses exceeding € 300.000 in its previous financial year (2024). The distribution was made without an updated solvency test being drawn up or reviewed by the statutory auditor, relying instead on the audited accounts of 2023 which showed a surplus.

### 2. Director’s Duty and the Solvency Test

Under Dutch law, directors of a private limited company (Besloten Vennootschap) are bound by strict fiduciary duties. **Article 2:9 DCC** establishes that directors must act in the best interests of the company and its associated enterprises, taking into account the interests of shareholders and creditors. This duty requires directors to exercise the care and diligence expected of a reasonably acting director in similar circumstances.

A critical component of this duty is ensuring that any dividend distribution does not threaten the continuity of the company. **Article 2:216 DCC** mandates that a resolution approving the appropriation of profits may only be adopted if, after taking into account the company’s assets and liabilities (including reserves), the balance sheet shows a sufficient equity position to cover the distribution. The directors are personally liable for damages resulting from their failure to comply with this provision.

In the present case, the reliance on the 2023 financial statements ignores the material adverse change in Duinrand’s financial position during 2024. By approving the payment of € 480.000 without conducting a current solvency test that accounted for the € 300.000 loss, the directors failed to verify whether Duinrand retained sufficient equity to cover this outflow while maintaining adequate working capital. This omission likely constitutes a breach of **Article 2:9 DCC** because it disregarded the legitimate interests of creditors who might have been prejudiced by the depletion of company assets during a period of financial instability.

### 3. Assessment of Liability Exposure

The breach of **Article 2:216 DCC** creates a strong presumption of liability. If the distribution is deemed unlawful because it endangered the solvency of Duinrand, the directors are jointly and severally liable to compensate any damage suffered by third parties (such as creditors) or, in certain interpretations, by the company itself if the loss is restored to the balance sheet improperly.

Furthermore, under **Article 2:9 DCC**, the directors’ failure to perform a basic solvency check before distributing € 480.000 can be characterized as gross negligence. The amount is significant relative to the company’s apparent financial distress. Courts generally do not accept "lack of knowledge" or "reliance on outdated reports" as a valid defense when the risk to creditors was obvious.

### 4. Prospects of Inquiry Proceedings (WFO Procedure)

You have also inquired about the prospects of initiating inquiry proceedings under Book 2 of the Dutch Civil Code against Duinrand. To succeed, the petitioner must demonstrate that there is grounds to fear serious mismanagement that threatens the company’s interests.

Given the facts:
1.  **Serious Mismanagement:** The failure to apply the solvency test required by **Article 2:216 DCC** constitutes a clear violation of statutory duties. Combined with the breach of the duty of care under **Article 2:9 DCC**, this amounts to serious mismanagement.
2.  **Threat to Interests:** The outflow of € 480.000 from a company in deficit creates a tangible threat to creditor interests and the continuity of the business.

The High Court of the Netherlands (Hoge Raad) has established that improper dividend distributions are a classic example of grounds for inquiry proceedings. The court would likely appoint an interim administrator or provisional administrator to stabilize the company’s governance immediately. While the procedure is costly and time-consuming, the legal merits appear strong due to the clear statutory violation.

### 5. Recommendations

1.  **Immediate Remediation:** Duinrand should urgently demand the return of the unlawful dividend from Cobalt Labs B.V. under the grounds of unjustified enrichment or breach of corporate law. If Cobalt refuses, immediate legal action for restitution is advised.
2.  **Director Indemnification:** The directors should review their D&O insurance policies immediately to assess coverage for potential claims arising from this breach.
3.  **Governance Review:** Implement a strict protocol for future dividend distributions requiring an independent solvency test signed off by the statutory auditor or an external accountant before any General Meeting resolution.

### 6. Conclusion

The distribution of € 480.000 likely violated **Article 2:216 DCC** and constituted a breach of **Article 2:9 DCC**. The directors face significant personal liability exposure. The prospects for successful inquiry proceedings are favorable, given the clear statutory non-compliance and the resulting risk to creditors.
