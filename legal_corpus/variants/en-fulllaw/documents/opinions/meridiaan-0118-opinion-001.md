**CONFIDENTIAL LEGAL OPINION**

**To:** The Board of Directors, Maaslicht Online B.V.
**From:** mr. T. Bergsma, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**Date:** 14 May 2025
**Re:** Matter 2025.0118 — Enforceability of Liability Cap in MSA dated 12 November 2023

### 1. Introduction

You have instructed Meridiaan Advocaten B.V., specifically mr. T. Bergsma, to provide a legal opinion regarding the enforceability of Section 14.2 (Limitation of Liability) in the Master Services Agreement (the "MSA") concluded between Maaslicht Online B.V. ("Maaslicht") and Vesting Systems B.V. ("Vesting"). The dispute arises from a series of critical data corruption incidents in Q1 2025, resulting in alleged damages exceeding €450,000.

The central question is whether the contractual liability cap of €50,000 remains enforceable if Vesting’s conduct involves intent or wilful recklessness, and what legal avenues exist to renegotiate or bypass this cap.

### 2. Factual Background

On 12 November 2023, Maaslicht and Vesting entered into the MSA for the provision of cloud infrastructure services. Section 14.2 states:
> "In no event shall either party’s aggregate liability exceed the total fees paid by Maaslicht to Vesting in the twelve (12) months preceding the claim. Under no circumstances shall Vesting be liable for indirect, consequential, or punitive damages."

On 3 February 2025, a backup failure occurred due to a configuration error in Vesting’s automated maintenance script. Internal emails from Vesting’s CTO, dated 28 January 2025, reveal that the team was aware of the impending database schema update but proceeded without performing the required integrity checks, citing "tight deadlines." This direct negligence led to data loss affecting Maaslicht’s core operational logs.

### 3. Legal Analysis: The Liability Cap

Under Dutch law, parties are generally free to limit liability, provided such limitations do not violate mandatory provisions or the standards of reasonableness and fairness (*redelijkheid en billijkheid*). Section 14.2 is a valid contractual clause under Article 6:233 DCC, provided it was incorporated correctly.

However, Dutch case law consistently holds that liability cannot be excluded for **intent** (*opzet*) or **wilful recklessness* (*zettelijk verwijtbaar onachtbaarheid*). If Maaslicht can prove that Vesting acted with such disregard for the consequences that it amounts to wilful recklessness, the liability cap becomes unenforceable *ab initio*.

### 4. Application of Article 6:248(2) DCC

The pivotal legal instrument in this matter is **Article 6:248(2) DCC**. This article stipulates that a rule binding upon the parties does not apply if its strict application would be unacceptable according to standards of reasonableness and fairness.

In the context of liability caps, Article 6:248(2) DCC serves as the primary tool for challenging the cap’s enforceability. The Hoge Raad has established that a liability cap may be disregarded if the breach is so severe that holding the client to the cap would be unjust.

To invoke **Article 6:248(2) DCC**, Maaslicht must demonstrate that:
1. Vesting’s conduct transcends mere negligence and enters the realm of wilful recklessness;
2. The imbalance between the fee paid (€4,200 annually) and the potential liability (capped at €50,000) is grossly disproportionate given the severity of the breach.

The internal communications from 28 January 2025 are critical evidence here. Proceeding with a known high-risk operation without safeguards suggests a conscious disregard for Maaslicht’s interests. This strengthens the argument that the cap should be set aside under **Article 6:248(2) DCC**.

### 5. Prospects of Renegotiation and Fallback Position

#### A. Renegotiation
A direct renegotiation of the lowest cap is unlikely to succeed through amicable means alone, as Vesting will rely on the written contract. However, Maaslicht can leverage the threat of litigation based on **Article 6:248(2) DCC** to force a settlement. The prospect of having the cap invalidated and facing full liability for €450,000+ creates significant pressure on Vesting to agree to a higher settlement figure, potentially in the range of €150,000–€200,000.

#### B. Litigation Fallback
If renegotiation fails, Maaslicht should file for court dissolution or damages. The fallback position is a claim that **Article 6:248(2) DCC** renders the €50,000 cap unenforceable due to Vesting’s wilful recklessness. Success depends on the court’s assessment of whether "tight deadlines" constitute sufficient justification for bypassing safety protocols. Given the evidence of known risks, there is a strong argument that the cap should be disregarded.

### 6. Conclusion and Recommendation

1.  **Enforceability:** The liability cap of €50,000 is vulnerable to being set aside under **Article 6:248(2) DCC** if intent or wilful recklessness is proven.
2.  **Evidence:** Preserve all internal communications from Vesting dated between 1 January 2025 and 10 February 2025. These are crucial for establishing the requisite state of mind.
3.  **Strategy:** Initiate formal negotiations with Vesting Systems B.V. immediately. State clearly that Maaslicht intends to invoke **Article 6:248(2) DCC** in any subsequent litigation, arguing that the cap is unreasonable given the wilful nature of the breach.
4.  **Next Steps:** If no agreement is reached by 30 June 2025, prepare a summons seeking full damages and a declaration that the liability cap is void under **Article 6:248(2) DCC**.
