**CONFIDENTIAL LEGAL OPINION**

**To:** Board of Directors, Maaslicht Retail B.V.
**From:** mr. L. Okafor, Privacy Counsel at Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**Date:** March 14, 2025
**Re:** Assessment of Mandatory Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) for the "Maaslicht Connect" Biometric Loyalty System

### 1. Introduction and Scope

At the request of Maaslicht Retail B.V. ("the Client"), I have prepared this legal opinion to determine whether a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) is required under Article 35 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) for the intended processing activities associated with the new "Maaslicht Connect" customer loyalty program.

The Client intends to launch a unified digital identity system that combines standard transaction history with voluntary biometric identification (facial recognition at point-of-sale terminals in Eindhoven and Amsterdam stores) and cross-border data transfers to a cloud provider located in Singapore for analytics purposes. This opinion evaluates the risk profile of this processing against the criteria set forth in Article 35 GDPR and relevant guidance from the European Data Protection Board (EDPB).

### 2. Legal Framework: Article 35 GDPR

Article 35 GDPR mandates that a DPIA must be carried out where a type of processing is likely, by reason of its nature, scope, context, and purposes, to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. The regulation explicitly lists three cumulative criteria that trigger this obligation if two or more are met:

1.  Systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects relating to natural persons which is based on automated processing, including profiling, and on which decisions are based that produce legal effects similarly affect the data subject;
2.  Systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area on a large scale;
3.  Processing on a large scale of special categories of data (Article 9 GDPR) or data relating to criminal convictions and offenses (Article 10 GDPR).

Furthermore, the Dutch Data Protection Authority (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens) guidelines specify that DPIAs are also required for processing operations involving innovative technologies or large-scale profiling.

### 3. Assessment of the Intended Processing

We analyze the Client's proposed processing against the statutory criteria and risk indicators.

#### 3.1. Nature of the Data and Biometric Identification

The "Maaslicht Connect" system proposes to capture facial geometry data at checkout counters. Under Article 9(1) GDPR, biometric data used for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person is classified as a special category of personal data. The processing of such data is prohibited unless a specific exemption under Article 9(2) applies. While explicit consent may serve as a legal basis, the mere inclusion of special category data significantly elevates the risk profile. Although criterion 3 (large-scale processing of special categories) requires "large scale," the sensitivity of biometric data alone necessitates a careful risk assessment.

#### 3.2. Profiling and Automated Decision-Making

The Client intends to use the biometric identifier to link in-store behavior with online purchase history to generate personalized discount offers. This constitutes profiling under Article 4(4) GDPR. The processing involves automated evaluation of personal aspects (purchasing habits, location data) to produce effects that influence the economic behavior of the data subject (pricing/offer discrimination). If this profiling leads to automated decisions without human intervention that have legal or similarly significant effects on the individual, criterion 1 is met. Even if human review exists, the systematic and extensive nature of the evaluation across the Client's 45 retail locations suggests a high risk of intrusion into private life.

#### 3.3. Systematic Monitoring

The deployment of cameras capable of facial recognition at all entry points and checkout lanes in publicly accessible areas (store interiors open to customers) constitutes systematic monitoring. The EDPB guidelines indicate that using technical means for surveillance on a large scale is highly likely to result in high risk. The Client's operation covers a national footprint with over 2 million monthly visitors, satisfying the "large scale" threshold.

#### 3.4. Data Transfers and Storage

The data will be transferred to Singapore. Given the current adequacy status of the EU-US Privacy Shield being invalidated and the varying adequacy decisions for third countries, this transfer introduces additional risks regarding the enforceability of data subject rights and the level of protection afforded to the data.

### 4. Risk Analysis Conclusion

Based on the assessment above, two or more of the criteria in Article 35(3) GDPR are met:
1.  **Systematic and extensive profiling** involving automated evaluation of personal aspects;
2.  **Systematic monitoring** of a publicly accessible area on a large scale via biometric surveillance;
3.  **Processing of special category data** (biometrics).

The combination of these factors creates a high risk to the rights and freedoms of data subjects, specifically regarding:
*   Discrimination or unfair treatment based on inferred sensitive characteristics;
*   Loss of anonymity in public spaces;
*   Potential function creep where data is used for purposes other than loyalty rewards;
*   Security risks associated with the storage of irreversible biometric templates.

### 5. Conclusion and Recommendation

**Conclusion:** A Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) is **strictly required** under Article 35 GDPR prior to the commencement of any processing activities related to the "Maaslicht Connect" biometric loyalty system. Failure to conduct this assessment would constitute a violation of the GDPR, exposing Maaslicht Retail B.V. to administrative fines and civil liability.

**Recommendations:**
1.  **Initiate DPIA Immediately:** The Client must commission a full DPIA documenting the processing operations, purposes, necessity, proportionality, and risks.
2.  **Consultation with Supervisory Authority:** Given the high residual risk even after mitigation measures (such as on-device processing or strict access controls), Article 36 GDPR may require prior consultation with the Dutch Data Protection Authority if the Client determines that the processing would still result in a high risk.
3.  **Implement Privacy by Design:** Ensure that facial recognition data is processed locally on terminals and deleted immediately after verification, rather than stored centrally, to minimize the scope of processing.
4.  **Transparency and Consent:** Draft clear, granular consent forms that distinguish between standard loyalty services and biometric processing, ensuring consent is freely given and revocable.
