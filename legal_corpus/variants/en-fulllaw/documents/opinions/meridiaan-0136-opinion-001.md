# LEGAL OPINION

**Matter:** 2025.0136
**Date:** 12 April 2025
**To:** The Board of Directors, Nordlicht Networks B.V., Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven
**From:** mr. J. Havelaar, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.

## 1. Introduction and Background

Meridiaan Advocaten B.V. has been instructed by Nordlicht Networks B.V. ("Nordlicht") to provide legal advice regarding the termination of the service agreement dated 15 January 2023 with Novael Technologies B.V. ("Novael"). The dispute concerns Novael's failure to deliver the agreed-upon cloud infrastructure maintenance services, resulting in significant operational downtime for Nordlicht during Q4 2024.

This opinion evaluates two strategic paths:
1. Initiating proceedings before the Subdistrict Court of Eindhoven to dissolve the contract pursuant to Article 7:671b of the Dutch Civil Code (DCC); and
2. Pursuing a negotiated settlement to secure immediate compensation without litigation risk.

## 2. Legal Framework: Dissolution under Article 7:671b DCC

Article 7:671b DCC provides that if one party is in default of performance, the other party may dissolve the contract by notice given to the other party, provided that the default is such that it justifies dissolution. The Subdistrict Court must confirm this dissolution upon request if the court finds that the grounds for dissolution are sufficient.

### 2.1 Assessment of Novael’s Default
The evidence gathered indicates that Novael failed to meet the Service Level Agreement (SLA) requirements on twelve occasions between November 2024 and January 2025. Specifically, the server availability dropped below 99.5% for three consecutive months. Under Dutch employment and service contract law, a persistent failure to perform essential obligations constitutes a "sufficiently serious default" (*voldoende tekortkoming*).

### 2.2 Prospects of Success in Litigation
The Subdistrict Court of Eindhoven has established case law requiring that the debtor be given a reasonable period to cure the defect after formal notice. Nordlicht issued a final notice on 10 March 2025, granting Novael seven days to remedy the defects. Novael failed to respond substantively.

Based on current jurisprudence, the prospects of obtaining a court order confirming the dissolution of the contract are **high (approx. 80-85%)**. The court will likely rule that the prolonged downtime constitutes a fundamental breach justifying immediate termination.

## 3. Financial Risks: Fair Compensation

While dissolution is legally viable, it does not automatically extinguish Novael’s liability for damages. A critical risk in dissolution proceedings is the claim for "fair compensation" (*billijke vergoeding*).

### 3.1 The Risk of Counterclaims
Novael may argue that Nordlicht also failed to provide adequate access logs or timely payments for invoices issued in November 2024 (totaling €15,000). If the court finds mutual fault, it may reduce any awarded damages or even order Nordlicht to pay compensation to Novael under Article 6:74 DCC principles applied by analogy.

### 3.2 Estimated Exposure
If the matter proceeds to litigation, the total duration is estimated at 18–24 months. During this period:
- Legal costs for both parties may be partially shifted if Nordlicht is deemed to have acted unreasonably in any procedural aspect.
- The court may order a "fair compensation" payment from Nordlicht to Novael if it finds that the immediate dissolution was too harsh given Novael's partial performance efforts in early 2025.
- Estimated additional exposure: €5,000 to €12,000 in potential counter-damages or cost shifts.

## 4. Comparison: Litigation vs. Settlement

| Factor | Dissolution via Article 7:671b DCC | Negotiated Settlement |
|--------|-----------------------------------|-----------------------|
| **Certainty of Termination** | High (if procedural steps followed) | Immediate upon signing |
| **Financial Outcome** | Uncertain; risk of counter-claims | Fixed: €45,000 lump sum |
| **Timeframe** | 18–24 months | Immediate |
| **Legal Costs** | Approx. €15,000–€20,000 (Nordlicht’s share) | Approx. €2,500 (administrative) |
| **Reputation Risk** | Public court record | Confidential |

## 5. Recommendation

While the Subdistrict Court of Eindhoven is likely to confirm the dissolution of the contract under Article 7:671b DCC due to Novael’s substantial breach, the litigation route carries significant financial uncertainty and time costs. The risk of a counter-claim for fair compensation cannot be entirely excluded.

We strongly recommend accepting the settlement offer currently on the table from Novael Technologies B.V., which includes:
1. Immediate dissolution of the service agreement;
2. Payment of €45,000 as full and final compensation;
3. Waiver of all future claims between the parties.

This approach provides financial certainty, avoids the risk of an additional fair compensation order against Nordlicht, and allows immediate reallocation of resources to alternative service providers.

## 6. Conclusion

The legal grounds for dissolution under Article 7:671b DCC are strong, but the associated risks outweigh the benefits given the availability of a comprehensive settlement. We advise signing the settlement agreement no later than 20 April 2025 to preserve the current offer terms.

Should you wish to proceed with litigation instead, please instruct Meridiaan Advocaten B.V. by return email so that we may draft the formal summons and prepare the evidence file for the Subdistrict Court of Eindhoven.
