**PRIVILEGED LEGAL ADVICE**

**To:** Board of Directors, Verdana Retail B.V.
**From:** mr. M. Terhorst, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**Date:** 15 January 2025
**Re:** Matter 2025.0141 – Director’s liability exposure regarding dividend distribution and prospects for inquiry proceedings

### 1. Introduction

This opinion addresses the legal risks facing the directors of Verdana Retail B.V. (“the Company”) following the board’s decision on 10 January 2025 to approve a dividend distribution amounting to € 750.000. The request for this advice arises from concerns raised by minority shareholder Novael Digital B.V. regarding the solvency of the Company at the time of distribution and the general conduct of management.

### 2. Director’s Liability under Article 2:9 DCC

The primary legal standard governing the conduct of directors in the Netherlands is set forth in **Article 2:9 DCC** of the Dutch Civil Code (DCC). Directors are required to perform their duties with the care and diligence expected of a reasonably acting director in comparable circumstances.

In our assessment, the duty of care includes a specific obligation to ensure that the Company remains solvent after any dividend distribution. This requires directors to make an informed decision based on reliable financial data. If the Board approved the distribution of € 750.000 without conducting a sufficient liquidity analysis or ignoring clear signs of financial distress, such conduct may constitute a breach of duty under **Article 2:9 DCC**.

A breach of this standard can lead to joint and several liability for damages suffered by third parties or the Company itself. Given that Novael Digital B.V. has explicitly challenged the prudence of this decision, they may argue that the directors failed to act in the best interest of the Company by prioritizing short-term cash flow over financial stability.

### 3. The Dividend Test under Article 2:216 DCC

The distribution of dividends is subject to the strict solvency test outlined in **Article 2:216 DCC**. This article mandates that a dividend may only be distributed if, after the payment, the Company’s equity exceeds half of its paid-up and called-up share capital.

Based on the audited financial statements as of 31 December 2024, the Company’s net equity stands at € 920.000. The distribution of € 750.000 would reduce this equity to € 170.000. We must verify whether € 170.000 remains above half of the paid-up share capital. Assuming a standard paid-up capital of € 45.000, half of this amount is € 22.500. Therefore, strictly mathematically, the distribution does not violate the letter of **Article 2:216 DCC** regarding the static equity test.

However, case law has established that directors must also assess the Company’s future solvency. If the € 750.000 payment impairs the Company’s ability to meet its obligations as they fall due in the near term, the distribution may still be considered improper. The lack of a separate liquidity forecast for Q1 2025 weakens the Board’s defensive position should insolvency proceedings commence later this year.

### 4. Prospects for Inquiry Proceedings

Novael Digital B.V. has indicated interest in initiating inquiry proceedings (*wederrechtelijk bestuur*) under Book 2, Title 9 of the DCC. The threshold for opening such an inquiry is high. The Court must find that there are facts indicating that the management of the Company has been conducted improperly in a manner that makes it desirable to appoint an interim manager or investigate the affairs.

While the questionable nature of the dividend distribution provides grounds for scrutiny, it may not alone suffice for an inquiry unless linked to broader mismanagement. However, if evidence emerges that the directors ignored warnings regarding cash flow risks, this strengthens the case for improper management. We advise documenting all board minutes from 10 January 2025 thoroughly to demonstrate due diligence.

### 5. Recommendations

1.  **Immediate Audit:** Commission an independent liquidity review covering the next six months to confirm that the remaining € 170.000 equity is sufficient for ongoing operations.
2.  **Board Resolution:** Ensure the board resolution approving the € 750.000 distribution explicitly references the solvency assessment and the compliance with **Article 2:216 DCC**.
3.  **Communication:** Draft a formal response to Novael Digital B.V. clarifying the financial basis for the decision, citing the equity position post-distribution.

We remain available to review the liquidity report and draft the necessary correspondence upon request.
