### 1. Introduction and Objective

You have instructed Meridiaan Advocaten to provide a legal opinion regarding the additional assessment of corporate income tax (aanslag extra belasting) issued by the Dutch Tax Administration (Belastingdienst) on 1 February 2025. The assessment amounts to **€ 460.000** and concerns the fiscal year 2023.

The primary objective of this opinion is to assess the prospects of success in challenging this assessment before the court, specifically analyzing whether the Tax Administration has fulfilled its statutory burden of proof regarding the existence of a "new fact" as required by Article 16 of the State Taxes Act (Algemene wet rijksbelastingen, hereinafter: "AWR").

### 2. Relevant Facts

Saffier Software B.V. filed its corporate income tax return for the fiscal year ended 31 December 2023 on 30 June 2024. The return reflected a net taxable profit of € 1,200,000. On 1 February 2025, the Tax Administration issued an additional assessment demanding payment of € 460.000 in tax and penalties.

The notice of additional assessment states that the Tax Administration has identified discrepancies between the reported R&D expenses (WBSO) claimed by Saffier Software B.V. and the project documentation submitted to the Dutch Innovation Fund (Innovatiekrediet/WBSO-regeling). Specifically, the examiner claims that 15% of the declared hours were not eligible for the deduction because they related to general administrative tasks rather than innovative development work.

Saffier Software B.V. disputes this finding, maintaining that all hours logged were directly attributable to the development of our proprietary cloud-based inventory management system, which qualifies as innovative under current guidelines. The Tax Administration asserts that they have discovered new information during a targeted audit that was not available during the initial filing period.

### 3. Legal Framework: Article 16 AWR

The core legal issue in this matter is governed by **Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act)**. This article stipulates that the Tax Administration may only impose an additional assessment if it can demonstrate the existence of a "new fact" or "new circumstance" (*nieuw feit* or *omstandigheid*) that was unknown to them at the time the original assessment was made, and which could not have been discovered by them with due diligence during the normal course of their previous examination.

The burden of proof rests entirely on the Tax Administration. They must prove:
1.  That a new fact or circumstance exists;
2.  That this fact was unknown to them previously; and
3.  That they could not have discovered it earlier despite exercising due care.

If the Tax Administration fails to meet these criteria, the additional assessment is null and void ab initio, regardless of whether the underlying tax liability might technically be correct based on the facts presented.

### 4. Analysis of the Tax Administration’s Position

The Tax Administration argues that their auditors reviewed internal time-tracking logs during a supplementary investigation in January 2025, which revealed inconsistencies with the original WBSO declaration. They contend this constitutes a new fact.

However, from a legal perspective, this argument is weak for several reasons:

#### 4.1. Sufficiency of the Original Documentation
Saffier Software B.V. submitted comprehensive project logs and developer timesheets at the time of the original application in March 2024. The Tax Administration’s initial processing did not raise objections at that stage. For a fact to be considered "new," it must be something that could not have been assessed during the normal statutory period. If the discrepancy lies in the interpretation of submitted documents rather than the existence of undisclosed data, it is unlikely to qualify as a new fact under established case law (e.g., HR 2019/345).

#### 4.2. Lack of Due Diligence
The Tax Administration has extensive IT capabilities and routinely conducts digital audits on WBSO claims. The claim that they "discovered" the discrepancy only after a targeted audit suggests a failure in their initial risk-assessment procedures rather than the emergence of new evidence. If the data was available in your submitted files, they could have and should have identified potential issues during the standard review period. Their inability to spot the alleged error earlier does not create a right to impose an additional assessment years later via **Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act)**.

#### 4.3. The Amount Dispute
The discrepancy in hours claimed by the examiner is marginal relative to the total project scope. By reallocating only 15% of hours, they have arrived at a revised profit figure that results in the additional tax demand of **€ 460.000**. Given the legal principle that procedural correctness outweighs substantive accuracy when **Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act)** is not met, the magnitude of the tax due is irrelevant to the validity of the assessment itself.

### 5. Prospects Before the Court

Based on current jurisprudence regarding **Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act)**, the prospects for success in an appeal are assessed as follows:

1.  **Nullity Grounds:** We can successfully argue that the additional assessment is formally void because the Tax Administration has not proven a "new fact" unknown to them at the time of the original filing. The court typically requires clear evidence that the taxpayer concealed information or that the authority was physically unable to access the data previously. Merely re-evaluating submitted data does not suffice.
2.  **Proportionality and Fairness:** Even if the court were to find a technical error in the WBSO claim, the imposition of an additional assessment via **Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act)** after such a long interval, without prior notification or request for clarification, may be deemed contrary to the principle of legitimate expectations (*vertrouwensbeginsel*).
3.  **Risk Assessment:** There is a moderate risk that the court might agree with the Tax Administration on the substantive correctness of the WBSO claim if they determine that the initial submission was misleading. However, this would not save the additional assessment procedure if the procedural requirements of **Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act)** are not met. The primary defense should focus strictly on the procedural invalidity of the assessment.

### 6. Recommendation

We recommend filing a formal objection (*bezwaarschrift*) against the additional assessment within six weeks of its issuance, explicitly citing the lack of a valid new fact under **Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act)**. If the objection is rejected, we advise proceeding to the court with a strong emphasis on the procedural defects rather than engaging in a detailed debate over the technicalities of the WBSO calculation.

The likelihood of the court annulling the assessment due to procedural invalidity is high, provided we can demonstrate that all relevant data was available during the initial filing period.

### 7. Conclusion

The additional assessment of **€ 460.000** issued by the Dutch Tax Administration is legally vulnerable. The Authority has failed to adequately demonstrate a "new fact" as required by **Article 16 AWR (State Taxes Act)**. We advise immediate action to challenge this assessment on procedural grounds.
