### 1. Introduction and Background

You have instructed Meridiaan Advocaten B.V. to provide legal advice regarding the prospects of recovering an outstanding debt owed by Meridiaan Solutions B.V. (hereinafter: "MSBV") to Aurora Logistics B.V. (hereinafter: "ALBV").

The factual background is as follows:
1. MSBV was declared bankrupt by the District Court of Eindhoven on 10 March 2025. The curator appointed is mr. J. de Vries of De Vries Insolvency Services, located at Grindlaan 45, 5611 ER Eindhoven.
2. ALBV holds a verified invoice dated 15 January 2025 (Invoice No. LOG-2025-089) for logistics services rendered in December 2024. The principal amount of this claim is € 95.000, exclusive of VAT.
3. MSBV has contested the full enforceability of this claim by invoking a statutory set-off (*wettelijke verrekening*) against ALBV. MSBV alleges that ALBV owed MSBV a separate debt arising from a defective software module supplied in Q3 2024.

The purpose of this opinion is to assess whether ALBV can successfully recover the € 95.000 despite the set-off defence raised by the curator or MSBV, and to analyze the applicability of Article 42 and Article 53 of the Bankruptcy Act (*Faillissementswet*).

### 2. Legal Analysis

#### 2.1 The General Rule on Set-Off in Bankruptcy

Under Dutch law, set-off is a powerful mechanism that allows parties to net out mutual debts. However, insolvency law imposes strict limitations to protect the collective body of creditors. The core issue here is whether the set-off was validly exercised prior to the bankruptcy declaration and whether it falls within the prohibited actions under the Bankruptcy Act.

#### 2.2 Applicability of Article 53 Bankruptcy Act (Set-Off)

Article 53 Bankruptcy Act governs the right of set-off in bankruptcy proceedings. It establishes that a creditor may set off their claim against the bankrupt estate if, at the time the bankruptcy was declared, they had a mutual claim against the bankrupt party that was due and payable.

For ALBV to utilize set-off as a defence (or rather, for MSBV/curator to successfully invoke it against ALBV), the following conditions must be met:
1. **Mutuality:** The debts must exist between the same parties in the same capacity.
2. **Existence at Time of Bankruptcy:** Both claims must have existed at the moment the bankruptcy was declared (10 March 2025).
3. **Due and Payable:** The claim ALBV has against MSBV (the € 95.000) is due. The counter-claim MSBV has against ALBV must also be due or, if not yet due, the waiver of the term must be permissible under general contract law principles at that time.

**Analysis for ALBV:**
ALBV’s claim for € 95.000 is undisputed in terms of existence. The critical vulnerability lies in the counter-claim asserted by MSBV. If MSBV was aware of its impending insolvency on or before 10 March 2025, and the set-off was exercised to favor a specific creditor (or itself) shortly before bankruptcy, the curator may challenge this under avoidance provisions. However, Article 53 generally protects set-offs that were legally effective *before* the bankruptcy decree.

If MSBV validly declared set-off against ALBV *before* 10 March 2025, the debt is extinguished to the extent of the counter-claim. If no such declaration was made prior to bankruptcy, the right to set-off generally remains available to ALBV as a creditor, provided the mutual claims existed at the time of bankruptcy.

#### 2.3 Applicability of Article 42 Bankruptcy Act (Fraudulent Preference / Avoidance)

Article 42 Bankruptcy Act allows the curator to avoid certain acts performed by the bankrupt party after the date of insolvency (or within a vulnerable period preceding it). Specifically, it addresses acts that disadvantage the general body of creditors.

If MSBV (or its directors) voluntarily paid ALBV or settled debts in a way that favored ALBV over other creditors, or conversely, if MSBV exercised set-off *after* 10 March 2025 to retain value (€ 95.000) while owing nothing back, this could be scrutinized under Article 42.

However, the more relevant risk for ALBV is not avoidance of a payment *received*, but rather the validity of the set-off itself. If MSBV did not exercise set-off before bankruptcy, ALBV cannot unilaterally apply set-off against the curator unless the legal requirements of Article 53 are strictly met.

If MSBV asserts that it owed ALBV more than € 95.000 for defective software, and this counter-claim was *not* due until after 10 March 2025, Article 53 might bar set-off because the counter-claim was not yet "due" at the time of bankruptcy. In that scenario, ALBV would have to pay the full € 95.000 into the estate, and then file a claim for the defective software damages as an unsecured creditor in the bankruptcy proceedings.

#### 2.4 Strategic Assessment of the € 95.000 Claim

1. **If Set-Off Was Validly Exercised Pre-Bankruptcy:**
   If MSBV formally declared set-off against ALBV before 10 March 2025, the debt of € 95.000 is legally extinguished. ALBV cannot recover this amount from the estate because no claim exists. The focus would shift to disputing the validity of the software defect claim.

2. **If Set-Off Was NOT Exercised Pre-Bankruptcy:**
   ALBV holds a valid claim for € 95.000 against MSBV. MSBV’s counter-claim for defective software is likely disputed and potentially not yet due. Under Article 53 Bankruptcy Act, set-off is only allowed if the counter-claim was due at the time of bankruptcy. If the software warranty period or dispute resolution clause delays the due date of the counter-claim until after 10 March 2025, set-off is prohibited.
   
   In this case, ALBV should submit its claim for € 95.000 to the curator immediately. The curator may still challenge the amount based on the software defect, but the burden of proof shifts. Given that MSBV is bankrupt, they lack the legal capacity to litigate actively without the curator’s involvement. If the curator does not substantiate the counter-claim with evidence (e.g., expert reports on the software defect filed before bankruptcy), ALBV’s claim for € 95.000 should be admitted in full or at a high percentage.

### 3. Conclusion and Recommendations

The prospects of recovering the full € 95.000 depend entirely on whether MSBV validly exercised set-off **before** the bankruptcy declaration on 10 March 2025.

1. **Check for Set-Off Declaration:** We must verify if any written notice of set-off was sent by MSBV to ALBV prior to 10 March 2025. If such a notice exists, recovery is legally impossible unless we successfully challenge the validity of the counter-claim in separate litigation (which has low prospects given the insolvency).
2. **Assess Due Date of Counter-Claim:** If no set-off was declared, and the counter-claim for defective software was not yet due on 10 March 2025, Article 53 Bankruptcy Act prevents MSBV from using it to offset ALBV’s claim. This is our strongest position.
3. **Action Required:**
   - Immediately file a proof of claim (*bewijs van schuld*) with the curator for € 95.000.
   - Submit a formal objection to any attempt by the curator to apply set-off if the counter-claim was not due at the time of bankruptcy.
   - Preserve all evidence regarding the software defect dispute, particularly dates of notification and acceptance of delivery, to prove that the counter-claim was contingent or future-dated as of 10 March 2025.

**Final Opinion:**
If no set-off was declared prior to bankruptcy, and the counter-claim is not yet due, ALBV has strong prospects of having its claim for € 95.000 admitted in full under Article 53 Bankruptcy Act. The curator’s reliance on Article 42 Bankruptcy Act to claw back payments or invalidate the claim will fail unless they can prove fraudulent intent in the original contract formation, which is a high threshold.

We recommend proceeding with the proof of claim submission immediately and monitoring the creditor’s meeting for any objections regarding the software defect.
