# ADVISORY OPINION

**To:** Orion Interactive B.V., Eindhoven
**From:** mr. T. Bergsma, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V., Eindhoven
**Date:** 12 April 2025
**Matter No.:** 2025.0104
**Subject:** Prospects of Renegotiating the Liability Cap; Fallback on Article 6:248(2) DCC

## 1. Introduction and Facts

On 15 January 2023, Orion Interactive B.V. ("Orion") and Novael Networks B.V. ("Novael") entered into a Master Services Agreement (the "Agreement"), governed by Dutch law. The Agreement concerns the development and maintenance of Orion’s proprietary cloud infrastructure. Clause 14.2 of the Agreement limits Novael's aggregate liability to a maximum amount of €250,000 (two hundred fifty thousand euros).

On 10 March 2025, significant data corruption occurred within Orion’s production environment due to a software update deployed by Novael. Preliminary forensic analysis indicates that this damage was not the result of mere negligence, but rather stemmed from a critical failure in Novael’s quality assurance protocols, which were allegedly bypassed to meet an accelerated deadline. The resulting financial loss to Orion is estimated at €1,850,000 (one million eight hundred fifty thousand euros).

Orion has requested our advice on two points:
1. Whether we can successfully renegotiate the liability cap in light of the severity of the breach; and
2. What legal grounds exist to bypass the liability cap entirely, specifically focusing on the principle of reasonableness and fairness (*redelijkheid en billijkheid*).

## 2. Legal Analysis: Renegotiation and Good Faith

Under Dutch law, parties are free to limit their liability, provided such limitation does not violate mandatory provisions or public order. However, the relationship between Orion and Novael is governed by the principle of good faith. Article 6:248(2) DCC mandates that in the exercise of rights and performance of obligations, a party must act in accordance with what reasonableness and fairness require.

### 2.1 The Inadequacy of the Cap for Gross Misconduct

While liability caps are generally enforceable in B2B contracts, their application becomes questionable when the breach results from intent (*opzet*) or willful recklessness (*kwade wil*). Dutch jurisprudence suggests that a party cannot rely on a limitation of liability if it has acted in gross disregard of the other party's interests.

Novael’s conduct—bypassing safety protocols to meet an artificial deadline—constitutes a severe deviation from standard professional care. To insist on the €250,000 cap in this context would likely be deemed unreasonable by a Dutch court. The disparity between the capped amount (€250,000) and the actual damage (€1,850,000) is substantial. A court would likely find that enforcing the cap here violates the standards of reasonable professional conduct expected in technology contracts.

### 2.2 Prospects of Renegotiation

We advise initiating immediate negotiations with Novael’s legal department. The leverage lies in the threat of litigation where we will argue that the liability clause is void *ab initio* due to its incompatibility with Article 6:248(2) DCC in cases of gross negligence or intent.

We propose a settlement amount of €1,500,000, representing approximately 81% of the proven damages. This figure is defensible given that litigation costs and the risk of a full judgment against Novael outweigh their current cash flow constraints. It is critical to frame the negotiation not merely as a dispute over a contract term, but as an acknowledgment of fundamental breach of trust.

## 3. Fallback: Application of Article 6:248(2) DCC

Should negotiations fail, we will pursue litigation. Our primary legal argument will be that Novael cannot rely on Clause 14.2 due to the application of Article 6:248(2) DCC.

### 3.1 Reasonableness and Fairness as a Limiting Principle

Article 6:248(2) DCC serves as a corrective mechanism in Dutch civil law. It allows courts to disregard contractual provisions that lead to an unacceptable result given the circumstances of the case. In technology contracts, where the service provider assumes significant control over the client’s operational integrity, the duty of care is heightened.

Novael failed to provide adequate warnings regarding the risks of the update. This omission, combined with the deliberate bypassing of QA checks, demonstrates a lack of regard for Orion’s contractual interests. Consequently, relying on the liability cap would be contrary to reasonableness and fairness.

### 3.2 Burden of Proof and Intent

To successfully invoke this article, we must establish that Novael’s actions amounted to willful recklessness. The internal emails from Novael’s project manager dated 08 March 2025, instructing developers to "skip the final integration test," provide strong evidence of intent. This shifts the burden onto Novael to prove that their conduct was within acceptable professional bounds, which they cannot do given the evidence of bypassed protocols.

## 4. Conclusion and Advice

1. **Negotiation Strategy:** Proceed with negotiations immediately. Highlight the evidentiary strength of the "skipped test" emails. Demand a settlement of €1,500,000. Make it clear that we reserve the right to declare the liability cap void under Article 6:248(2) DCC.
2. **Litigation Fallback:** If Novael refuses to exceed the €250,000 cap, file a claim for full damages. The core argument will be that enforcing the cap in light of their intentional misconduct violates reasonableness and fairness under Article 6:248(2) DCC.
3. **Preservation of Evidence:** Ensure all technical logs and email correspondence regarding the March 2025 update are preserved in their original format. Any alteration may undermine our claim of intent.

We await your instructions to draft a formal letter of demand to Novael Networks B.V., based at Keizersgracht 123, Amsterdam, prior to commencing any legal proceedings.
