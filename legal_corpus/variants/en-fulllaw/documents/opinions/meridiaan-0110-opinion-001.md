**CONFIDENTIAL LEGAL OPINION**

**To:** The Board of Directors, Novael Cloud B.V.
**From:** mr. R. Bahadoer, Meridiaan Advocaten
**Date:** 12 April 2025
**Re:** Matter 2025.0110 – Criminal Proceedings regarding Alleged Money Laundering

### 1. Introduction and Scope

This opinion is drafted in response to the urgent request from Novael Cloud B.V. (“the Client”) concerning the ongoing criminal investigation by the Financial Intelligence Unit-Netherlands (FIU-NL) and the Public Prosecution Service. The Client faces potential charges under Article 420bis of the Criminal Code (Wetboek van Strafrecht, hereinafter “CC”).

The purpose of this document is to:
1. Assess the viability of an acquittal based on the exclusion of evidence obtained in violation of fundamental rights;
2. Outline a robust sentencing defence strategy in the event that the court proceeds to conviction.

This advice is privileged and confidential. It constitutes legal work product intended solely for the internal use of Novael Cloud B.V.

### 2. Legal Framework: Article 420bis Criminal Code

The charge against the Client relies on Article 420bis CC, which criminalizes the concealment or transformation of proceeds of crime. To secure a conviction, the Public Prosecution Service must prove beyond reasonable doubt that:
1. The funds in question were derived from criminal conduct; and
2. The Client performed an act of concealment or transformation with knowledge of this illicit origin.

The threshold for "knowledge" (opzet) is high. Mere negligence or willful blindness may suffice for civil liability, but criminal conviction requires proof of conscious acceptance of the risk that the funds were criminal proceeds.

### 3. Strategy I: Acquittal via Exclusion of Evidence (Article 6 ECHR)

The most viable path to an acquittal lies in challenging the admissibility of the key evidence: the bank records of the Client’s primary operating account at ING Bank, Amsterdam, obtained via a warrant issued on 15 March 2025.

#### 3.1. Violation of Fair Trial Rights (Article 6 ECHR)

The Client asserts that the execution of the search warrant violated Article 6 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR), which guarantees the right to a fair trial and includes the right to effective legal assistance during the initial stages of criminal proceedings.

**Facts:**
During the raid on Novael Cloud B.V.’s offices at Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven, on 16 March 2025, the investigating officers failed to inform the Client’s designated legal counsel that a criminal investigation had been opened. Furthermore, the seizure of digital servers was conducted without a specific inventory list provided to the Client, violating the principle of proportionality and the right to prepare a defence.

**Legal Argument:**
Under Article 6 ECHR, the rights of the defence must be respected from the first interrogation or act of investigation. The failure to provide immediate access to counsel during the seizure phase constitutes a systemic irregularity that taints the subsequent use of evidence derived from that seizure. We will file a motion to exclude all digital evidence obtained on 16 March 2025, citing the European Court of Human Rights judgment in *Bykov v. Russia* and domestic precedents such as *HR 24 November 2021, ECLI:NL:HR:2021:1890*.

If the court grants this motion, the prosecution will lack the necessary proof to establish the "illicit origin" of the funds under Article 420bis CC. Without this evidence, the charge must collapse.

### 4. Strategy II: Sentencing Defence (Should Conviction Occur)

If the court rejects the motion to exclude evidence and proceeds to conviction, we shift focus to mitigating the sentence. The maximum penalty for Article 420bis CC is four years imprisonment or a fifth-category fine. We aim for a conditional dismissal or a minimal custodial sentence.

#### 4.1. Lack of Specific Intent

We will argue that while the Client managed transactions involving high-risk jurisdictions, there was no specific intent (opzet) to launder money. The Client’s compliance officer, mr. J. van der Meer, implemented standard due diligence procedures in accordance with the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Act (Wwft). The error was one of oversight regarding a single vendor, not a systemic scheme to conceal criminal proceeds.

#### 4.2. Cooperation and Restitution

Novael Cloud B.V. has voluntarily:
1. Reported the suspicious transaction to the FIU-NL on 10 March 2025;
2. Frozen the relevant assets pending investigation;
3. Provided full access to all internal communications.

Under Dutch sentencing guidelines, voluntary cooperation and restitution are significant mitigating factors. We will request that the court apply Article 9a CC (conditional dismissal) or impose a nominal fine under Article 10 CC, citing the Client’s clean record and immediate remedial actions.

### 5. Conclusion and Next Steps

The prospects for acquittal are **moderate to strong** if we successfully exclude the evidence obtained during the March 2025 raid on grounds of violating Article 6 ECHR. The procedural errors in the warrant execution are material.

If exclusion fails, the sentencing defence is robust due to the Client’s proactive cooperation and lack of malicious intent. We anticipate a fine rather than imprisonment, provided the court accepts the narrative of negligence rather than fraud.

**Immediate Actions Required:**
1. File a motion to exclude evidence with the Rechtbank Eindhoven no later than 20 April 2025.
2. Prepare witness statements from mr. J. van der Meer and the CEO, ms. A. de Vries.
3. Do not communicate with any third parties regarding the case without prior approval from Meridiaan Advocaten.

We await your instructions to proceed with the filing of the motion.
