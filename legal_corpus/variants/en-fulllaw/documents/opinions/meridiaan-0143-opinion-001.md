**CONFIDENTIAL LEGAL OPINION**

**To:** Board of Directors, Duinrand Data B.V.
**From:** mr. D. van Raay, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**Date:** 12 May 2025
**Subject:** Prospects of recovering the € 180.000 against the set-off defence

### 1. Introduction and Background

We have been instructed by Duinrand Data B.V. (hereinafter: "our client") to provide a legal opinion regarding the prospects of successfully recovering a claim in the amount of **€ 180.000** against Duinrand Retail B.V. (hereinafter: "the Debtor"). The Debtor has entered into provisional trusteeship proceedings on 24 April 2025, and subsequently, a declaration of bankruptcy was pronounced by the sub-district court of Amsterdam on 10 May 2025.

The claim arises from an IT infrastructure maintenance agreement concluded in January 2023, whereby our client provided server hosting services to the Debtor. Despite repeated invoices due since February 2024, the Debtor has failed to pay the outstanding balance of **€ 180.000**. The Debtor’s insolvency administrator (curator) has informed us that the Debtor intends to invoke a right of set-off (*set-off*) against our client’s claim, citing a prior debt owed by our client to the Debtor for defective software modules delivered in 2022.

### 2. Legal Framework: Set-Off in Bankruptcy Proceedings

The central legal issue is whether the right of set-off remains available to the Debtor after the declaration of bankruptcy. Under Dutch law, the general rule regarding set-off in insolvency is strictly regulated by the Bankruptcy Act (*Faillissementswet*).

#### 2.1 General Principle of Set-Off (Article 53 Bankruptcy Act)

Article 53 of the Bankruptcy Act establishes that a creditor may generally exercise their right of set-off against the estate, provided that the claims are mutual and due. However, this right is not absolute. The critical limitation lies in the timing of when the conditions for set-off were met. Specifically, Article 53 stipulates that set-off is only permitted if the claim against the bankrupt party was already due and enforceable at the moment of the declaration of bankruptcy.

Furthermore, jurisprudence has developed strict criteria regarding the *knowledge* of the insolvency proceedings. If the creditor (in this case, effectively the Debtor acting through its management or a related party) knew or should have known that the debtor was on the verge of insolvency at the time they acquired their claim against our client, the right to set-off may be denied. This is to prevent creditors from artificially creating claims to reduce their exposure to the estate unfairly.

#### 2.2 Avoidance of Fraudulent Preferences (Article 42 Bankruptcy Act)

Our client must also consider whether the underlying debt claimed by the Debtor for set-off could be challenged under Article 42 of the Bankruptcy Act. Article 42 allows the curator to avoid any payment or legal act performed by the bankrupt party that is detrimental to the creditors and which was done with the intent to harm them, or in circumstances where the other party acted in bad faith.

If the claim the Debtor wishes to set off against our client’s **€ 180.000** was created or acknowledged during the "suspect period" (typically the three months prior to the bankruptcy application, or one year if involving related parties), the curator may seek to annul that debt. If the underlying debt is avoided, it ceases to exist legally, and consequently, the basis for set-off vanishes.

### 3. Application to the Current Case

#### 3.1 Analysis of the Set-Off Defence

The Debtor claims a counterclaim of **€ 200.000** related to software defects. Our client disputes this amount entirely, arguing that the software was delivered in compliance with the contract specifications as confirmed by the acceptance protocol dated 15 March 2023.

For the set-off to be valid under Article 53 Bankruptcy Act, two conditions must be met:
1.  **Mutuality:** The claims must be between the same parties in the same capacity. This condition is satisfied as both Duinrand Data B.V. and Duinrand Retail B.V. are legal entities involved in the transaction.
2.  **Timing and Knowledge:** The claim against our client must have been due before or at the time of bankruptcy, and crucially, the Debtor must not have acquired this claim with knowledge of the insolvency proceedings.

Given that the software delivery occurred in 2023, well before the insolvency crisis which became apparent in late 2024, it is highly unlikely that the Debtor "acquired" this claim during the suspect period through a new transaction. However, if the validity of the debt was contested *after* the onset of financial difficulties, the curator may argue bad faith under Article 42 Bankruptcy Act to avoid acknowledging the debt at all.

#### 3.2 Risk Assessment

1.  **Risk of Invalid Set-Off:** There is a moderate risk that the curator will challenge the set-off if they can demonstrate that our client knew of the Debtor’s insolvency when performing services in early 2024. However, since our client provided services *before* the bankruptcy declaration and the claim is for unpaid invoices, our position is strong. The set-off right belongs to the bankrupt estate, not the debtor personally, but the estate can only use it if the legal conditions are met.
2.  **Risk of Avoidance:** If the curator determines that the software defect claim is baseless or fabricated to reduce the net payable amount, they may invoke Article 42 Bankruptcy Act to avoid the acknowledgment of this debt. This would leave our client with a full unsecured claim for **€ 180.000** against the estate, without deduction.

### 4. Conclusion and Recommendations

Based on the current facts, the prospects of recovering the **€ 180.000** are favorable, provided we act decisively:

1.  **Formal Objection:** We must formally object to any attempt by the curator to set off the software debt unless they provide irrefutable proof of the defect and the quantification of damages. Given that the acceptance protocol from March 2023 exists, the burden of proof lies heavily on the Debtor/curator.
2.  **Filing Proof of Claim:** We must file our proof of claim (*conclusie van vordering*) immediately with the court-appointed curator, explicitly stating that the set-off is disputed and that the debt is due and payable.
3.  **Legal Action:** If the curator refuses to accept our claim in full, we should prepare for summary proceedings (*kort geding*) or ordinary proceedings to establish the existence of the debt and the invalidity of the set-off defense, citing the lack of bad faith under Article 42 Bankruptcy Act.

In summary, while the Debtor will likely attempt to use Article 53 Bankruptcy Act to reduce their liability, the historical nature of the counterclaim (originating in 2023) and our client’s clean performance record provide a strong basis to reject the set-off entirely. We advise pursuing full recovery of **€ 180.000** as an unsecured claim against the estate.
