**Subject:** Compliance obligations under Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (DSA) regarding notice systems and transparency duties

### 1. Introduction and Scope of Advice

Meridiaan Advocaten B.V. has been instructed by Lumen Cloud B.V. (“the Client”) to provide a legal opinion regarding the application of Union law to its current operations as an online platform hosting services. Specifically, we address the obligations imposed by **Regulation (EU) 2022/2065** (hereinafter: “the DSA”) concerning the establishment of robust notice-and-action mechanisms and the duty to publish transparency reports.

This opinion is based on the facts provided in our file notes dated 15 March 2025, wherein you confirmed that Lumen Cloud B.V. hosts user-generated content for third-party e-commerce sellers via its SaaS infrastructure, constituting it as a “hosting service” and potentially an “online platform” under the DSA depending on the level of interaction with the goods sold.

### 2. Legal Framework: Regulation (EU) 2022/2065

The DSA establishes a harmonized framework for digital services across the European Union. For entities like Lumen Cloud B.V., which act as intermediaries, the DSA imposes specific due diligence obligations that supersede previous national implementations of the E-Commerce Directive. The core regulation is cited precisely as **Regulation (EU) 2022/2065**.

It is critical to distinguish between “hosting services” and “online platforms.” If Lumen Cloud B.V. merely provides technical infrastructure without organizing or promoting content, it may qualify for the liability exemption under Article 6 of the DSA. However, if the service involves any form of ordering, ranking, or filtering of goods or services offered by users, it likely qualifies as an “online platform,” triggering enhanced obligations under Articles 14 through 27 of the DSA.

### 3. Notice-and-Action Mechanisms

Under Article 16 of **Regulation (EU) 2022/2065**, Lumen Cloud B.V. must establish and operate a mechanism to enable recipients of its service to notify the company of their opinion that any content hosted on its platform constitutes illegal goods, services, or content.

#### 3.1 Requirements for the Notice System
To comply with Article 16(2), your notice-and-action system must:
1.  Be easily accessible, explicitly and prominently named, and understandable to the average person.
2.  Allow users to indicate clearly and unequivocally the reason why the content or good is considered illegal (e.g., intellectual property infringement, hate speech, safety risks).
3.  Provide a confirmation of receipt of the notice without undue delay.
4.  Enable Lumen Cloud B.V. to assess the notice expeditiously.

#### 3.2 Operational Implementation
We advise that Lumen Cloud B.V. implement a digital portal accessible at `compliance.lumencloud.nl/reports`. This portal must allow users to upload evidence supporting their claim of illegality. Upon submission, the system must automatically generate a unique reference number and send an email confirmation to the notifier within 24 hours of receipt.

If Lumen Cloud B.V. decides to remove or disable access to the content, it must inform the user who uploaded the content (the defendant) of this decision without undue delay, providing reasons for the removal based on the assessment performed. Conversely, if the notice is deemed manifestly unfounded, Lumen Cloud B.V. must inform the notifier of its decision and the reasons thereof.

### 4. Transparency Reporting Obligations

As an online platform, Lumen Cloud B.V. is subject to the transparency reporting obligations set forth in Article 26 of **Regulation (EU) 2022/2065**.

#### 4.1 Frequency and Content
Lumen Cloud B.V. must publish a transparency report at least once every year. The report for the calendar year 2024 was due by 17 February 2025; should this not have been published, immediate action is required to align with the 2025 reporting cycle (due by 17 February 2026).

The transparency report must include:
1.  Information on the content moderation policies applied by Lumen Cloud B.V., including how human review is conducted.
2.  The number of notices received, the type of notices, and the number of actions taken (e.g., removals, disabling access).
3.  The average time taken to process notices.
4.  Information on any orders received from public authorities or judicial decisions mandating the removal or disabling of content.

#### 4.2 Accessibility
The report must be made available in a machine-readable format and published on a dedicated section of Lumen Cloud B.V.’s website. The URL must be easily accessible from the homepage.

### 5. Risk Assessment and Mitigation

Failure to comply with **Regulation (EU) 2022/2065** may result in significant penalties. Article 72 allows competent authorities to impose fines of up to 6% of the total worldwide annual turnover of the undertaking concerned for non-compliance with information obligations or transparency reporting duties.

To mitigate this risk, Lumen Cloud B.V. should:
1.  Conduct an internal audit by 30 April 2025 to verify that the current notice-and-action system meets the technical and procedural standards of Article 16.
2.  Draft the 2025 Transparency Report starting in January 2026, ensuring data collection systems are logging all relevant metrics from 1 January 2025 onwards.
3.  Designate a point of contact for legal inquiries regarding DSA compliance, as required by Article 14.

### 6. Conclusion

Lumen Cloud B.V. must strictly adhere to the provisions of **Regulation (EU) 2022/2065**. The establishment of a compliant notice-and-action system and the timely publication of transparency reports are not optional but mandatory legal duties. We recommend that Lumen Cloud B.V. implements the technical upgrades described in Section 3.1 immediately to avoid any potential findings of non-compliance by the Digital Services Coordinator in the Netherlands.
