### 1. Introduction

We have been instructed by Nimbus Retail B.V. to provide a legal opinion regarding the termination of the lease agreement for the commercial retail premises located at Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven. The Tenant, Stroomdal Media B.V., has ceased paying rent for the months of March, April, and May 2025.

The central issue is whether Nimbus Retail B.V. can successfully obtain a court order for dissolution of the lease and eviction under **Article 7:274 DCC**, despite the Tenant's argument that the premises suffer from significant defects (specifically, HVAC failure) which allegedly justify withholding payment.

### 2. Factual Background

The parties entered into a commercial lease agreement on 1 January 2024 for a period of five years. The monthly rent is set at € 9.500, excluding VAT and additional charges.

Stroomdal Media B.V. has failed to pay the rent for March 2025 (due 1 April), April 2025 (due 1 May), and May 2025 (due 1 June). The total outstanding arrears amount to € 28.500 for the base rent alone, excluding statutory interest and costs.

On 5 May 2025, Stroomdal Media B.V. sent a written notice to Nimbus Retail B.V. stating that they are withholding payment due to "substantial defects" in the heating, ventilation, and air conditioning system, rendering half of the premises unusable for their storage needs. They have demanded immediate repairs before resuming payments.

Nimbus Retail B.V. has attempted to schedule repairs but alleges that the Tenant blocked access on 6 May 2025, preventing necessary maintenance.

### 3. Legal Analysis

#### 3.1 Grounds for Dissolution under Article 7:274 DCC

The primary legal basis for our claim is **Article 7:274 DCC**. This article provides that the lessor may seek dissolution of the lease if the lessee fails to pay rent or other sums due, provided that the failure is attributable to the lessee and constitutes a breach of contract.

To succeed, Nimbus Retail B.V. must demonstrate:
1.  That the Tenant is in arrears;
2.  That the arrears are not justified by a legal defense (such as a valid *exceptio non adimpleti contractus* or formal notice to repair);
3.  That the breach is sufficiently serious to warrant dissolution.

#### 3.2 Assessment of the Tenant’s Defects Defense

Stroomdal Media B.V. relies on the doctrine of reciprocal performance (*tegenprestatie*). However, Dutch case law establishes that a tenant cannot unilaterally withhold rent without prior formal steps unless the defects are so severe that the premises are entirely unusable (*onbruikbaarheid*) or significantly diminish the value such that payment would be unconscionable.

Key legal principles apply here:
1.  **Duty to Notify:** The Tenant is obligated to notify the Landlord of defects in a timely manner. While they notified us on 5 May, this notice came after months of non-payment. More importantly, under general civil law principles, a tenant must usually grant the landlord a reasonable period to cure the defect before withholding rent entirely, unless the situation is emergency-level critical.
2.  **Proportionality:** The defects are described as HVAC failure affecting "half" the premises. This does not render the entire lease object unusable. Withholding 100% of the rent for a partial defect is generally considered disproportionate by Dutch courts.
3.  **Obstruction of Repairs:** By denying access to repair technicians on 6 May, Stroomdal Media B.V. has actively prevented the cure of the alleged defect. This undermines their defense significantly. A tenant cannot invoke their own obstruction (eigen daad) to justify non-payment.

Therefore, the defects defense is unlikely to hold up in court as a justification for complete rent withholding. The Tenant’s obligation to pay remains primary.

#### 3.3 Valuation of the Claim

The claim for dissolution will include:
1.  **Outstanding Rent:** € 28.500 (€ 9.500 x 3 months).
2.  **Statutory Interest:** Calculated from due date until payment or judgment.
3.  **Legal Costs:** Standard procedural costs for the dissolution action.

The monthly value of € 9.500 is a critical factor in assessing the "seriousness" of the breach. Given that the arrears accumulate rapidly and the Tenant’s business (media storage) does not appear to be fundamentally destroyed by HVAC issues (unlike, for example, a cold-storage facility), the court may view the non-payment as an attempt to force renegotiation rather than a genuine inability to use the premises.

### 4. Prospects of Success

Our prospects for obtaining an order for dissolution under **Article 7:274 DCC** are strong, provided we act swiftly.

1.  **Likelihood of Eviction:** High. The clear breach of payment obligations, combined with the Tenant’s refusal to allow repairs, makes it difficult for them to claim they are acting in good faith.
2.  **Risk of Counterclaims:** The Tenant may file a counterclaim for damages related to the defects or seek a reduction of rent (*huurvermindering*). However, this would be handled separately and does not typically stop eviction proceedings if the rent arrears are undisputedly due. We should prepare for a potential partial rent reduction claim regarding the specific unusable area, but this is unlikely to exceed 20-30% of the total rent.
3.  **Procedural Strategy:** We recommend issuing a formal *ingebrekestelling* (notice of default) with a final deadline of 5 days for payment and access for repairs. If they fail to comply, we will file the dissolution action immediately at the Rechtbank Eindhoven.

### 5. Recommendations

1.  **Serve Formal Notice:** Immediately serve a formal notice of default on Stroomdal Media B.V., citing **Article 7:274 DCC**, demanding payment of € 28.500 plus interest within 5 days, and granting access for repairs.
2.  **Document Obstruction:** Ensure the incident on 6 May is documented in writing with dates, times, and witnesses.
3.  **File Action:** If the deadline passes without payment, file a request for dissolution of the lease and eviction at the court. We can apply for a provisional measure (*kort geding*) to expedite eviction if there is a risk of further damage or insolvency of the Tenant.

### 6. Conclusion

Nimbus Retail B.V. has solid grounds to dissolve the lease and evict Stroomdal Media B.V. The tenant’s defense regarding defects is weakened by their obstruction of repairs and the disproportionate nature of withholding all rent. The monthly rent of € 9.500 highlights the severity of the financial breach.

We await your instruction to proceed with the formal notice of default.
