**Re:** Matter 2025.0130 – Liability Assessment and Damage Calculation

### 1. Introduction and Facts

This opinion addresses the legal viability of a claim by Aurora Digital B.V. ("Aurora") against Veldhoven Software B.V. ("Veldhoven") arising from the security incident of 14 March 2025. On that date, unauthorized third parties gained access to Aurora’s customer database hosted on servers maintained by Veldhoven. The investigation commissioned by Meridiaan Advocaten confirms that the breach resulted directly from Veldhoven’s failure to apply critical security patches within the agreed Service Level Agreement (SLA) timeframe of 48 hours.

As a result, Aurora incurred significant costs in forensic analysis, customer notification, and credit monitoring services. Furthermore, Aurora suffered substantial reputational harm leading to a loss of goodwill among key enterprise clients.

### 2. Assessment of Liability and Causation

Under Dutch tort law, liability is established when an unlawful act causes damage. The failure to maintain agreed-upon security standards constitutes an unlawful act attributable to Veldhoven.

The critical legal hurdle is the requirement of causation (*causaliteit*). According to established case law, we must establish that the damage would not have occurred but for the breach. In this matter, the forensic report dated 20 April 2025 explicitly states that the intrusion vector was a known vulnerability for which patches had been available since February 2025. Had Veldhoven adhered to its contractual and statutory duty of care, the intrusion would have been prevented.

Therefore, we cite **Article 6:98 DCC** as the basis for attributing causality to Veldhoven’s omission. The causal link between the failure to patch and the data leakage is direct and unbroken. There are no intervening causes that break this chain of liability.

### 3. Quantification of Damages

Aurora claims two distinct categories of loss: material damages for remediation efforts and non-material damages for reputational injury.

#### 3.1 Material Damages
Aurora has documented the following expenses related to the incident response:
- Forensic IT investigation: € 15,000
- Legal counsel fees (external): € 7,500
- Customer notification and credit monitoring services: € 5,000

Total material damages amount to **€ 27.500**. These costs are fully compensable as they were reasonably incurred to mitigate the consequences of Veldhoven’s default.

#### 3.2 Non-Material Damages
Aurora also claims compensation for non-material damage (*immateriële schade*) due to reputational harm and loss of trust among its client base. While Dutch law traditionally requires a high threshold for such claims, recent jurisprudence allows for compensation where the breach is significant and results in measurable loss of goodwill or emotional distress to the legal entity’s reputation.

We are claiming **€ 25.000** as a fair valuation of this reputational erosion. This figure is supported by independent market analysis showing a 15% drop in new client inquiries during the quarter following the incident.

### 4. Risk Assessment: Contributory Negligence

Veldhoven has argued that Aurora’s internal IT team failed to monitor their own firewall logs for anomalies, contributing to the delay in detection by two days. Under **Article 6:107 DCC**, if the damage is partly due to a cause for which both parties are responsible, the compensation may be reduced proportionally.

We must weigh the risk of a contributory-negligence reduction (*eigen schuld van het slachtoffer*). While Aurora’s monitoring was imperfect, it was not the *cause* of the breach itself. The root cause remains Veldhoven’s failure to patch. Courts are generally reluctant to reduce damages significantly when the primary fault lies with the service provider’s core contractual obligation (security maintenance).

However, there is a modest risk that a court might apply a 10% reduction on the non-material damages portion, arguing that earlier detection could have limited the scope of data exfiltration. This would affect the € 25.000 claim but not the € 27.500 material claim, as the remediation costs were necessary regardless of detection time.

### 5. Conclusion and Recommendation

The claim against Veldhoven Software B.V. is legally viable. The causation under **Article 6:98 DCC** is strong due to the clear violation of security standards. The total claim stands at **€ 27.500** in material damages plus **€ 25.000** in non-material damages.

We recommend proceeding with a formal demand letter citing these statutes and figures. We anticipate that Veldhoven’s insurance carrier will settle the material damages immediately, while negotiations on the non-material portion may require mediation. The risk of a successful defense on contributory negligence is low to moderate, primarily affecting the quantum of the reputational damage award rather than the liability itself.
