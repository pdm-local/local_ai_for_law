**CONFIDENTIAL LEGAL OPINION**

**To:** The Board of Directors, Orion Systems B.V.
**From:** mr. R. Bahadoer, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V. (Criminal Law Department)
**Date:** 15 June 2025
**Matter No.:** 2025.0138
**Re:** Criminal Proceedings against Orion Systems B.V. – Preliminary Assessment of Defence Strategy

### 1. Introduction and Scope

This opinion addresses the criminal liability of Orion Systems B.V. ("the Company") arising from transactions conducted through its subsidiary, Orion Digital Solutions, between January 2024 and March 2025. The Public Prosecution Service (OM) has initiated proceedings alleging that the Company facilitated the laundering of proceeds of crime in violation of Article 420bis of the Dutch Criminal Code (Wetboek van Strafrecht).

As legal counsel, I have reviewed the case file provided by the investigating judge on 10 June 2025. This document outlines the prospects for acquittal, the possibility of excluding key evidence under Article 6 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR), and sentencing strategies should a conviction be unavoidable.

### 2. Analysis of the Substantive Charge: Article 420bis Criminal Code

The core allegation is that Orion Systems B.V. knowingly or recklessly facilitated the conversion or transfer of property, knowing that such property was derived from criminal activity. To secure a conviction under Article 420bis Criminal Code, the prosecution must prove two elements:
1. The objective element: The act of facilitating money laundering.
2. The subjective element: Knowledge (opzet) regarding the illicit origin of the funds.

#### 2.1. Lack of Subjective Intent (*Opzet*)

The strongest defence lies in challenging the *mens rea*. Our internal audits and KYC (Know Your Customer) records indicate that Orion Systems B.V. conducted standard due diligence checks on all counterparties involved in the disputed transactions. The client accounts were verified against the FIMIS database, and no red flags were initially raised.

While the funds were eventually linked to a shell company in Cyprus, which was later identified as illicit, our records show that at the time of transaction execution (12 February 2025), there was no concrete evidence suggesting criminal origin. The prosecution’s reliance on "constructive knowledge" is weak because the sophistication of the money laundering scheme employed by the third-party intermediary exceeded standard detection capabilities. Without direct proof that Orion Systems B.V. *knew* the funds were illicit, the threshold for intentional liability under Article 420bis Criminal Code is not met.

#### 2.2. Reasonable Doubt and Precautionary Measures

We have documented evidence of immediate freezing of assets upon receipt of a police inquiry on 15 March 2025. This demonstrates good faith and lack of intent to conceal proceeds. The gap between the transaction date and the discovery of the illicit nature of the funds undermines the allegation of prior knowledge.

### 3. Procedural Defence: Exclusion of Evidence under Article 6 ECHR

Even if substantive liability is argued, we must challenge the admissibility of the electronic communications seized from Orion Systems B.V.’s servers on 1 March 2025.

#### 3.1. Violation of Fair Trial Rights

The search warrant issued by the investigating judge on 28 February 2025 was overly broad, authorizing the seizure of all employee emails regardless of relevance to the specific transactions in question. This violates the principle of proportionality and the right to a fair trial under Article 6 ECHR. The European Court of Human Rights has established that excessive searches infringe upon the presumption of innocence and the right to privacy.

We will file a motion to exclude all evidence derived from this warrant, arguing that the seizure was not strictly necessary for the investigation and thus rendered the subsequent interrogation of CEO Mr. J. van der Meer unreliable. If the court accepts this argument, the prosecution’s case collapses due to lack of corroborating digital evidence.

### 4. Sentencing Defence Strategy

Should the court reject the exclusion of evidence and proceed to conviction, we must mitigate the penalty. Orion Systems B.V. is facing a maximum penalty of six years imprisonment or a fine of up to €72 million in the fourth category.

#### 4.1. Mitigating Factors

1. **Self-Reporting:** Orion Systems B.V. voluntarily reported suspicious activity to the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU-NL) on 16 March 2025, one day after the initial police inquiry. This cooperation is a significant mitigating factor under Dutch sentencing guidelines.
2. **Corporate Compliance Program:** The Company implemented a robust Anti-Money Laundering (AML) program in 2023, exceeding regulatory requirements. The failure was an isolated incident involving a sophisticated external fraudster, not a systemic compliance failure.
3. **No Criminal History:** Orion Systems B.V. has no prior criminal record.

#### 4.2. Proposed Penalty Range

We aim for a conditional sentence (*voorwaardelijke straf*) with a probationary period of five years, coupled with an obligation to pay a fine equivalent to the illicit gains (approximately €150,000) plus legal costs. We argue that imprisonment would be disproportionate given the Company’s remedial actions and lack of direct intent.

### 5. Conclusion and Recommendations

#### 5.1. Prospects of Acquittal

The prospects for acquittal are **moderate to strong**. The absence of direct evidence of *knowledge* regarding the illicit origin of funds, combined with the procedural irregularities in the search warrant, provides a solid foundation for defence. The argument under Article 6 ECHR regarding the overbreadth of the search is particularly potent and may lead to the dismissal of key evidence.

#### 5.2. Immediate Next Steps

1. **File Objection to Search Warrant:** Submit a formal request to the court to exclude all evidence obtained from the 1 March 2025 search, citing Article 6 ECHR violations regarding proportionality and privacy.
2. **Submit Due Diligence Records:** Provide certified copies of all KYC checks performed prior to the transactions to demonstrate lack of intent.
3. **Prepare Sentencing Submission:** Draft a detailed sentencing memorandum highlighting the voluntary reporting to FIU-NL and the implementation of corrective AML measures.

We recommend maintaining a firm stance on the lack of *opzet* while keeping the Article 6 ECHR argument ready as a procedural trump card. The goal is to achieve a dismissal of the case or, at minimum, a minimal fine with no imprisonment.
