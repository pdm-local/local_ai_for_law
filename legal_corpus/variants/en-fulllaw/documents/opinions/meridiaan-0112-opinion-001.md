**LEGAL OPINION**

**Matter:** 2025.0112
**Date:** 14 March 2025
**To:** Brightlane Technologies B.V.
**From:** mr. A. Koster, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**Subject:** Prospects of dissolution and eviction given the arrears versus the defects defence

### 1. Introduction and Background

You have instructed Meridiaan Advocaten to evaluate your prospects for dissolving the business tenancy agreement with Polderzicht Digital B.V. (hereinafter: "the Tenant") located at Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven, and subsequently obtaining an eviction order. The core dispute arises from the Tenant's failure to pay rent for the months of January and February 2025, amounting to a total claim of € 6.200.

The Tenant has refused payment on the grounds that the premises suffer from significant defects, specifically regarding the HVAC system and water drainage, which they argue constitute a breach of your obligations under Article 7:206 DCC. They assert a right of retention or set-off against the rent due.

You have valued the claim at € 3.100 per month for the relevant periods in dispute. This opinion addresses whether these arrears provide sufficient grounds for immediate dissolution and eviction under Dutch law, particularly in light of the Tenant's defence.

### 2. Legal Framework: Article 7:274 DCC

The primary statutory basis for the dissolution of a business tenancy due to non-payment of rent is Article 7:274 DCC. This article grants the landlord the right to dissolve the lease if the tenant fails to pay the rent or other amounts due within fourteen days after being summoned to do so.

For this provision to apply effectively, two cumulative conditions must be met:
1. There must be an outstanding debt that is due and payable.
2. The tenant must have failed to pay within the statutory 14-day period following a formal summons (ingebrekestelling).

In your case, the rent for January and February 2025 was due on the first day of each respective month. Assuming you issued a formal summons on 15 March 2025, the 14-day period would expire on 29 March 2025. If the Tenant has not paid by that date, the statutory condition for dissolution under Article 7:274 DCC is technically fulfilled.

### 3. The Defects Defence and Proportionality

The central legal hurdle in this matter is the Tenant's assertion of defects. Under Dutch law, a tenant may invoke a *exceptio non adimpleti contractus* (defense of non-performance) if the landlord has failed to fulfill their maintenance obligations under Article 7:206 DCC. However, case law establishes strict limits on this defence in business tenancies.

#### 3.1 Severity of Defects
The court will assess whether the defects are so severe that they render the premises unfit for the intended use or significantly diminish their value. The Tenant claims issues with HVAC and drainage. To succeed in a full set-off or retention of rent, the Tenant must prove that the defects substantially impair the usability of the office space. If the defects are minor or easily remediable, a total refusal to pay rent is considered disproportionate.

#### 3.2 Proportionality Assessment
Courts often apply the principle of reasonableness and fairness (redelijkheid en billijkheid). Even if defects exist, withholding 100% of the rent is rarely justified unless the premises are completely uninhabitable. If the Tenant can demonstrate that the defects reduce the value of the lease by, for instance, 20%, they might be entitled to retain only that percentage, not the full amount.

Your valuation of € 3.100 per month represents the full contractual rent. If the court finds that the defects justify a partial reduction in rent (e.g., € 600 per month), the Tenant would still be in arrears for the remaining € 2.400 per month. This residual arrears figure is sufficient to trigger Article 7:274 DCC, provided the formal summons requirements are met.

### 4. Valuation of the Claim and Financial Position

You have stated the claim value as € 3.100 per month. For January and February 2025, this totals € 6.200. It is critical to note that any partial set-off approved by the court will reduce the principal amount owed but will not necessarily negate the breach of contract unless the arrears are fully covered.

Given that Polderzicht Digital B.V. has not paid any rent for two months, the debt is substantial enough to warrant legal action. The Tenant's failure to engage in a good-faith negotiation regarding repair timelines further weakens their position. If they believed the defects were severe, they should have formally requested repairs under Article 7:206 DCC rather than simply withholding payment without judicial authorization or prior agreement.

### 5. Procedural Steps and Prospects

To proceed with dissolution and eviction, you must follow these steps precisely:

1. **Formal Summons:** Issue a formal written summons (ingebrekestelling) to Polderzicht Digital B.V., demanding payment of the € 6.200 within 14 days. This document must explicitly state that failure to pay will lead to dissolution of the lease and an application for eviction.
2. **Waiting Period:** Allow the 14-day period to expire without full payment.
3. **Court Application:** File a request with the sub-district court (kantonrechter) in Eindhoven for dissolution of the lease under Article 7:274 DCC and an order for eviction.

#### Prospects of Success
Our assessment indicates a **high probability of success** for dissolution, but with caveats:
- The court is likely to dismiss the Tenant's claim that they can withhold *all* rent if the defects are not catastrophic.
- The court may rule that only a portion of the rent was legitimately withheld due to the defects. However, as long as any arrears remain after this adjustment, Article 7:274 DCC allows for dissolution.
- Eviction is discretionary. The court will consider the Tenant's financial situation and the availability of alternative premises. If Polderzicht Digital B.V. can demonstrate immediate financial hardship or lack of viable alternatives, the court might grant a grace period (termijn) for eviction, but this rarely exceeds three months.

### 6. Recommendations

1. **Immediate Summons:** Send the formal summons via registered mail and bailiff service to Polderzicht Digital B.V. at their registered address on Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven, immediately.
2. **Documentation of Defects:** Prepare evidence countering the Tenant's defect claims. Commission an independent expert report if necessary to demonstrate that the premises remain fit for use and that any defects are minor or have been addressed in part. This will prevent the court from accepting a large set-off.
3. **Litigation Strategy:** Be prepared for the court to reduce the rent due to legitimate defects. However, maintain your position that the remaining balance is owed. The focus should be on the procedural breach (non-payment after summons) rather than the quantum of the defect-based reduction.

### 7. Conclusion

Based on Article 7:274 DCC, you have strong grounds for dissolving the tenancy due to non-payment of rent. The Tenant's defence regarding defects is unlikely to result in a total discharge of their payment obligation unless they can prove the premises are entirely unusable, which is factually improbable given your description of the business operations. We recommend proceeding with the formal summons immediately to preserve your right to dissolution and eviction.
