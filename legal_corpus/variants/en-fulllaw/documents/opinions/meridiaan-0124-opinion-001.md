**CONFIDENTIAL LEGAL OPINION**

**To:** The Board of Directors, Saffier Logistics B.V.
**From:** mr. R. Bahadoer, Advocate, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**Date:** 12 May 2025
**Matter:** 2025.0124
**Subject:** Criminal Investigation into Money Laundering Allegations Against Mr. J. van der Meer

### 1. Introduction and Scope of Advice

This opinion is issued pursuant to your request regarding the ongoing criminal investigation by the National Prosecution Service (Openbaar Ministerie, hereinafter "the OM") against Mr. J. van der Meer, former Compliance Officer at Saffier Logistics B.V. The investigation concerns allegations that Mr. van der Meer facilitated the laundering of illicit funds through complex shipping transactions in violation of Article 420bis of the Dutch Criminal Code (Wetboek van Strafrecht, hereinafter "Criminal Code").

This document constitutes privileged legal advice. It is prepared for the exclusive use of the Board of Saffier Logistics B.V. and does not constitute legal advice for any third party. The analysis below addresses three distinct areas: (1) the prospects of acquittal based on substantive law; (2) the admissibility of evidence obtained during the search warrant executed on 15 April 2025; and (3) sentencing strategies should a conviction be unavoidable.

### 2. Substantive Law Analysis: Article 420bis Criminal Code

Article 420bis Criminal Code criminalizes money laundering, defined as performing an act with the intention of concealing or disguising the illicit origin of proceeds derived from any offense, or assisting another person in actions where such concealment is possible.

#### 2.1 Elements of the Offense
To secure a conviction, the OM must prove beyond reasonable doubt that:
1. Proceeds were derived from a predicate offense; and
2. Mr. van der Meer acted with the specific intent (*opzet*) to conceal or disguise the origin of these proceeds.

Our review of the transaction logs indicates that while the shipping routes utilized by Saffier Logistics B.V. in Q3 2024 were structurally complex, they corresponded to standard industry practices for intermodal freight between the Port of Rotterdam and the inland terminals in Duisburg. The "layering" effect observed in the financial records was a result of currency hedging mechanisms agreed upon with our banking partner, Rabobank N.V., rather than an attempt to obscure the source of funds.

#### 2.2 Lack of Specific Intent
The cornerstone of our defense is the absence of *opzet* (intent). Mr. van der Meer acted in good faith reliance on the audit reports provided by our external accountant, Deloitte Accountants B.V., dated 10 September 2024, which confirmed compliance with anti-money laundering regulations. Without proof that Mr. van der Meer knew or should have known that the funds were illicit, the subjective element of Article 420bis Criminal Code is not met. Mere negligence (*schuld*) is insufficient for a conviction under this specific provision.

### 3. Procedural Defense: Admissibility of Evidence and Article 6 ECHR

The investigation relied heavily on electronic data seized during a search warrant executed at Saffier Logistics B.V.’s office at Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven, on 15 April 2025. We argue that this evidence should be excluded from proceedings.

#### 3.1 Violation of the Right to a Fair Trial
Article 6 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR) guarantees the right to a fair trial. This includes the right to an effective defense and equality of arms. The search warrant issued by the investigating judge (*onderzoeksrechter*) on 10 April 2025 was overly broad, authorizing the seizure of all email servers and physical files without specific suspicion linking Saffier Logistics B.V. to Mr. van der Meer’s alleged individual conduct.

The execution of this warrant resulted in the bulk seizure of client data belonging to unrelated third parties, including contracts with Maersk A/S and DB Schenker N.V. This overreach violates the principle of proportionality inherent in Article 6 ECHR. The prosecution cannot rely on evidence obtained through a manifestly disproportionate intrusion into the rights of Saffier Logistics B.V. and its clients. We will file a motion to exclude all data retrieved from the servers under Section 359a of the Code of Criminal Procedure, citing this violation.

#### 3.2 Chain of Custody Issues
Furthermore, the chain of custody for the primary hard drives seized on 15 April 2025 is broken. The forensic report submitted by the National Forensic Institute does not contain a continuous hash verification log between 15 April 2025 and the analysis date of 20 May 2025. This gap creates reasonable doubt regarding the integrity of the evidence, rendering it unreliable for conviction.

### 4. Sentencing Strategy: Mitigation in Case of Conviction

Should the court disregard the procedural defenses and proceed to a verdict on the merits, we must prepare a robust sentencing defense. While acquittal remains our primary objective, we anticipate the risk of a partial conviction based on "recklessness" rather than intent.

#### 4.1 Mitigating Factors
In such a scenario, we will emphasize the following mitigating circumstances:
1. **Cooperation:** Mr. van der Meer has voluntarily provided all personal documents and waived professional secrecy to assist the investigation since 20 April 2025. This cooperation should be rewarded with a significant reduction in sentence under the OM’s sentencing guidelines.
2. **No Prior Record:** Mr. van der Meer has no prior criminal record and is a person of good character, with references from his previous employment at KPMG N.V.
3. **Lack of Personal Gain:** The investigation confirms that Mr. van der Meer received no illicit profit. The transactions were processed in the name of Saffier Logistics B.V., and any fees earned were standard professional compensation.

#### 4.2 Proposed Sentence Range
We argue for a suspended sentence or a conditional discharge (*voorwaardelijke invrijheidstelling*), given that the conduct was not driven by personal enrichment but by professional error in judgment. We will submit a pre-sentencing report (*voorlopig rapport*) from a psychologist specializing in occupational crime, concluding that Mr. van der Meer poses no risk to society and is suitable for community service rather than imprisonment.

### 5. Conclusion and Next Steps

Saffier Logistics B.V. maintains that the charges under Article 420bis Criminal Code are unfounded due to the lack of criminal intent. The reliance on external audits and standard industry practices negates the requisite *opzet*. Furthermore, the evidence obtained in violation of Article 6 ECHR rights must be excluded.

We recommend the following immediate actions:
1. File a formal objection to the admissibility of the seized digital evidence within five days of this opinion.
2. Prepare a detailed affidavit from Mr. van der Meer clarifying the business rationale for the Q3 2024 transactions.
3. Initiate settlement negotiations with the OM, offering a conditional discharge in exchange for the withdrawal of charges against Saffier Logistics B.V. as a legal entity.

We remain at your disposal to discuss this strategy in person.
