**CONFIDENTIAL LEGAL OPINION**

**To:** Mr. M. Terhorst, Partner at Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
**From:** Maaslicht Cloud B.V., represented by its Board of Directors
**Date:** 15 April 2025
**Subject:** Liability exposure regarding the dividend distribution and prospects for inquiry proceedings (Matter 2025.0113)

### 1. Introduction and Background

We refer to our consultations held on 10 April 2025 regarding the financial position of Maaslicht Cloud B.V. ("Maaslicht") and its subsidiary, Maaslicht Systems B.V. ("Systems"). Specifically, we seek your advice concerning the legality of the dividend distribution approved by the General Meeting on 28 February 2025, the potential personal liability of the directors for this distribution, and the procedural prospects for initiating inquiry proceedings under Book 2 of the Dutch Civil Code (DCC).

The facts are as follows: On 15 March 2025, Maaslicht Cloud B.V., registered at the Chamber of Commerce under number 87654321 with its statutory seat in Eindhoven, declared a dividend of € 480.000 to be paid to its sole shareholder, Mr. J. van der Meer. This distribution was based on the annual accounts for the fiscal year ending 31 December 2024. At the time of the declaration, Maaslicht reported retained earnings sufficient to cover this amount in nominal terms. However, subsequent market developments and a liquidity crunch in the cloud infrastructure sector have revealed that the company’s solvency position is significantly weaker than reflected in those historical accounts.

### 2. Legal Analysis: Director’s Duty and Dividend Distribution

The core of your inquiry concerns whether the directors breached their duty of care and acted contrary to proper directorship by approving this distribution.

**2.1. The Distribution Test under Article 2:216 DCC**

Under Article 2:216 DCC, a legal entity may only distribute profits if the balance sheet attached to the annual accounts shows sufficient retained earnings after covering losses. Furthermore, the distribution must not impair the company’s ability to pay its due debts (the "solvency test").

In this matter, the dividend amount of € 480.000 was declared based on accounts that did not adequately reflect subsequent events occurring between 31 December 2024 and the date of distribution in March 2025. Specifically, two major cloud provider contracts were terminated by clients due to service outages in January 2025, leading to a projected revenue decline of 40% for Q1 2025. These facts constitute new information that should have been considered when assessing the availability of distributable profits.

If it is established that the distribution impaired or threatened to impair the company’s ability to meet its due debts, the distribution is deemed unlawful under Article 2:216 DCC. The fact that the nominal retained earnings appeared sufficient does not absolve the directors from conducting a forward-looking solvency assessment. Given the immediate liquidity pressure, the distribution of € 480.000 likely violated this provision.

**2.2. Breach of Duty under Article 2:9 DCC**

Article 2:9 DCC imposes a duty on directors to manage the legal entity in the interest of its enterprise and the continuity thereof. This duty includes care for creditors when the company is near insolvency or when actions may jeopardize solvency.

By approving a substantial cash outflow of € 480.000 while aware (or should have been aware) of the deteriorating contractual position, the directors may have failed to act as a proper director would in comparable circumstances. The failure to reserve sufficient liquidity for operational continuity and creditor protection constitutes a breach of Article 2:9 DCC. This breach is particularly relevant if the distribution leads to insolvency or if it prejudices creditors who extended credit relying on the company’s stable financial position.

### 3. Personal Liability Exposure

If the dividend distribution is found unlawful under Article 2:216 DCC, the directors may be held jointly and severally liable for the amount unlawfully distributed. According to established case law, directors can be required to repay the € 480.000 to the company’s estate to restore the assets available for creditors.

Additionally, if the breach of duty under Article 2:9 DCC results in damages to the company or its creditors, the directors may face personal liability for those damages. The causal link between the decision to distribute and the current financial distress is a key evidentiary point. Given that the distribution occurred shortly before the severe liquidity crisis, the court would likely find a strong presumption of causation.

### 4. Prospects for Inquiry Proceedings

We also assess the feasibility of initiating inquiry proceedings (wederkerig onderzoek) under Book 2 DCC to have the General Meeting’s resolution concerning the dividend declared void or to remove the directors.

**4.1. Standing and Requirements**

Inquiry proceedings require a petitioner with standing, typically shareholders representing at least € 90.000 in capital or shares worth at least € 90.000. As Maaslicht Cloud B.V. is the sole shareholder via its parent structure, this threshold is met. The petitioner must demonstrate sufficient interest, which is satisfied by the alleged mismanagement and financial loss.

**4.2. Substantive Prospects**

The prospects for success are moderate to strong, provided that evidence can be secured showing that the directors ignored clear warning signs regarding solvency. The key to a successful inquiry will be demonstrating that the approval of the € 480.000 dividend was not merely an error in judgment but a gross negligence contrary to Article 2:9 DCC and Article 2:216 DCC.

The court may order:
1. The nullification of the General Meeting resolution approving the distribution;
2. The removal of the directors for serious misconduct;
3. The appointment of an independent administrator to take over management.

However, the court will also consider whether restitution of the dividend is sufficient remedy or if removal is necessary to protect the enterprise’s continuity. Given the current instability, removal is a likely outcome if gross negligence is proven.

### 5. Recommendations and Next Steps

1. **Immediate Recovery:** Attempt to recover the € 480.000 from Mr. J. van der Meer amicably to mitigate further damage to creditor interests and reduce liability exposure.
2. **Internal Investigation:** Commission an independent forensic accounting review of the Q4 2024 accounts and the Q1 2025 liquidity forecasts to establish exactly when the solvency test was failed.
3. **Documentation:** Gather all board meeting minutes from February 2025 to assess whether any dissenting opinions were recorded or if due diligence was performed.
4. **Litigation Strategy:** Prepare a draft petition for inquiry proceedings, focusing on the breach of Article 2:9 DCC and Article 2:216 DCC, while negotiating with the shareholder for voluntary repayment to avoid protracted litigation costs.

We await your further instructions regarding the drafting of the recovery letter and the procedural strategy for the inquiry.
