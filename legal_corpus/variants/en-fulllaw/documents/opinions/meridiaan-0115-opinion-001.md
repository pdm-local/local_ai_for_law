**SUBJECT:** Legal Opinion regarding the recovery of the outstanding claim of € 320.000 against Polderzicht Media B.V.

### 1. Introduction and Mandate

Nimbus Interactive B.V. (hereinafter: "Nimbus") has instructed Meridiaan Advocaten B.V. to provide legal advice concerning the enforcement of a receivable amounting to exactly € 320.000 against Polderzicht Media B.V. (hereinafter: "Polderzicht").

Following the declaration of bankruptcy of Polderzicht by the District Court of Rotterdam on 10 April 2025, Nimbus has sought to recover this debt. Polderzicht’s insolvency trustee, mr. J. de Groot, has formally opposed the enforcement of this claim, invoking a right of set-off (*verrekening*) based on alleged prior payments made by Polderzicht to Nimbus in the period leading up to the bankruptcy filing.

The purpose of this opinion is to evaluate the legal merits of the trustee’s defence and to outline the prospects for successfully recovering the full amount of € 320.000.

### 2. Factual Background

The relationship between the parties is governed by a Service Level Agreement dated 15 January 2024. Under this agreement, Nimbus provided cloud hosting services to Polderzicht. The total invoice issued on 1 February 2025 for services rendered in January 2025 amounted to € 320.000. This invoice remains unpaid as of the date of this opinion.

Polderzicht filed a voluntary petition for bankruptcy, which was granted by the court on 10 April 2025. On 20 April 2025, mr. J. de Groot was appointed as trustee (*curator*).

In his response dated 28 April 2025, the trustee asserts that Polderzicht had a counterclaim against Nimbus arising from three payments made in March 2025:
1. € 50.000 on 3 March 2025;
2. € 75.000 on 14 March 2025;
3. € 95.000 on 28 March 2025.

The trustee claims these payments were for services that Nimbus failed to deliver adequately in February 2025, resulting in damages equal to or exceeding the sum of € 220.000. Consequently, the trustee argues that the remaining balance of € 100.000 is extinguished by set-off, leaving only a residual claim for the remainder. However, the primary dispute concerns whether these payments can be avoided under insolvency law, thereby restoring the full creditor status for the € 320.000.

### 3. Legal Analysis

#### 3.1 The General Rule of Set-Off in Insolvency

Under Dutch law, a creditor may generally set off a mutual debt against a claim owed to the bankrupt estate, provided certain conditions are met at the time the bankruptcy petition is filed. However, the Bankruptcy Act (*Faillissementswet*) imposes strict limitations to prevent the erosion of the bankruptcy estate through preferential transfers or late-setoff strategies.

#### 3.2 Avoidance of Payments under Article 42 Bankruptcy Act

The core of our defence lies in the avoidance of the payments made by Polderzicht to Nimbus in March 2025. If these payments are avoided, they must be returned to the bankruptcy estate. Consequently, Nimbus would no longer have a valid counterclaim to assert against the invoice debt, and the full € 320.000 would become payable by the trustee.

**Article 42 Bankruptcy Act** provides that acts or transactions performed by the debtor within six months prior to the declaration of bankruptcy may be avoided if they constitute fraudulent preference (*fraudeus preferentia*) or if they occur under specific conditions where the counterparty knew or should have known of the debtor's impending insolvency.

Key arguments for avoidance include:
1. **Knowledge of Insolvency:** Polderzicht was in severe financial distress in early March 2025, as evidenced by its failure to pay suppliers other than Nimbus and subsequent legal threats received from other vendors. If Nimbus continued to accept payment during this period while being aware of Polderzicht's precarious position, the payments may be deemed fraudulent preferences.
2. **Lack of Legal Obligation:** At the time of the March payments, there was a legitimate dispute regarding the quality of services in February 2025. By accepting partial payments without a clear settlement agreement, Polderzicht may have engaged in conduct that disadvantages other creditors if those funds are later clawed back or if set-off is disallowed.

If the court accepts that Nimbus should have suspected insolvency or that the payments were made under duress to avoid immediate service termination, we can argue that these payments are voidable under Article 42 Bankruptcy Act.

#### 3.3 Restrictions on Set-Off under Article 53 Bankruptcy Act

Even if the payments cannot be fully avoided, the right of set-off itself is restricted by **Article 53 Bankruptcy Act**. This article limits set-off if:
1. The claim against the bankrupt estate was acquired after the bankruptcy petition was filed; or
2. The debtor (Polderzicht) acquired the claim with knowledge of the bankruptcy petition or within six months prior thereto, under circumstances that suggest an intent to obtain a preferential position.

In this matter, the counterclaim arises from payments made in March 2025. If we can demonstrate that Polderzicht made these payments specifically to create a set-off position against Nimbus while knowing that bankruptcy was imminent (the petition was filed on 10 April 2025), Article 53 Bankruptcy Act may invalidate the set-off right. The temporal proximity of the March payments to the April bankruptcy filing strongly supports the argument that Polderzicht acted in bad faith to preserve liquidity for itself at the expense of general creditors, or specifically to reduce its exposure to Nimbus.

Furthermore, if the counterclaim was disputed at the time of payment, it does not qualify as a "certain and liquid" claim required for set-off under insolvency principles unless validated by a court judgment prior to bankruptcy. No such judgment exists.

### 4. Prospects of Success

Our strategy rests on two parallel pillars:
1. **Avoidance Action:** Initiating a lawsuit to avoid the March payments under Article 42 Bankruptcy Act, arguing that Nimbus benefited from payments made while Polderzicht was insolvent and that Nimbus should have been aware of this status.
2. **Opposition to Set-Off:** Arguing that any set-off is inadmissible under Article 53 Bankruptcy Act because the counterclaim was not certain, liquid, or enforceable at the time of the bankruptcy petition, and was potentially manufactured through late payments intended to manipulate creditor ranking.

The probability of success for recovering the full € 320.000 is assessed as **moderate to high**. Dutch courts are increasingly strict regarding set-off defences in the context of imminent insolvency, particularly when the counterparty (Nimbus) continued to extend credit or accept payments during a period of visible distress.

### 5. Recommendations

1. **File an Objection:** Immediately file a formal objection against the trustee’s confirmation of the set-off defence in the creditors' meeting.
2. **Initiate Avoidance Proceedings:** Prepare a petition to avoid the three March 2025 payments under Article 42 Bankruptcy Act. Gather evidence regarding Polderzicht's financial state in February and March 2025 (e.g., public notices of default, communications with other creditors).
3. **Negotiation Leverage:** Use the threat of avoidance proceedings as leverage in settlement negotiations. The trustee may prefer to settle for a reduced amount (e.g., € 200.000) rather than risk the costs and uncertainty of litigation that could result in the recovery of the full € 320.000 plus legal costs.

### 6. Conclusion

While Polderzicht’s trustee has raised a valid procedural hurdle through the set-off defence, the legal grounds for challenging this defence under Article 42 and Article 53 Bankruptcy Act are substantial. By pursuing avoidance of the prior payments and contesting the validity of the counterclaim at the time of bankruptcy, Nimbus Interactive B.V. has a strong basis to claim the full € 320.000.

We recommend proceeding with the avoidance action while simultaneously engaging in settlement talks, leveraging our legal position to maximize recovery.
