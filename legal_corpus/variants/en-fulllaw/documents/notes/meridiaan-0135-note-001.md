### 1. Purpose of the Meeting

The primary objective of this intake was to assess the legal liability of Aurora Media B.V. ("Aurora") as an operator of a digital platform hosting user-generated content. Specifically, Aurora seeks to determine whether it qualifies for the intermediary exemption from liability under Dutch law and EU regulations, particularly in light of its contractual relationship with Helix Online B.V. ("Helix"), which provides the underlying technical hosting infrastructure.

### 2. Facts Established During Intake

During the discussion, ms. van den Berg provided the following factual background:

1.  **Nature of the Platform:** Aurora operates a social media aggregation platform where users can upload videos, images, and text posts. The platform allows for public commenting and sharing.
2.  **Role of Helix Online B.V.:** Aurora has entered into a Service Level Agreement with Helix Online B.V., dated 15 January 2025. Under this agreement, Helix provides the server space, bandwidth, and basic content delivery network (CDN) services required to host Aurora’s data.
3.  **Knowledge and Control:** Aurora asserts that it does not actively monitor all user uploads prior to publication due to volume constraints (approx. 50,000 uploads per day). However, Aurora employs a moderation team of ten employees who review content *after* it has been reported by users. Upon confirmation of illegal content (e.g., hate speech or copyright infringement), Aurora removes the content within 4 hours.
4.  **Financial Interest:** Aurora generates revenue primarily through targeted advertising displayed on its platform. Ms. van den Berg confirmed that the ad algorithms are optimized based on user engagement metrics derived from the hosted content, but Aurora does not select specific content for promotion beyond this algorithmic targeting.

### 3. Legal Analysis

#### 3.1. The Hosting Exemption under Article 6:196c DCC

The core of our advice revolves around Article 6:196c of the Dutch Civil Code (DCC), which implements the e-Commerce Directive. This article provides a liability exemption for "information society services" that consist of the storage of information provided by a recipient of the service, provided that:
*   The provider does not have actual knowledge of illegal activity or information; and
*   Upon obtaining such knowledge, the provider acts expeditiously to remove or disable access to the information.

**Analysis:**
Aurora acts as an intermediary. Since Aurora relies on Helix for the physical storage, we must determine if Aurora is considered the "hosting service" or if Helix is. Given that Aurora exercises editorial control over the presentation and moderation of content (via its post-publication review team), Aurora is likely viewed as having a role beyond pure technical automation. However, the mere act of moderating *after* notification does not automatically disqualify Aurora from the exemption, provided the initial hosting was passive.

The critical threshold is "actual knowledge." Our intake suggests that without specific reports, Aurora lacks actual knowledge. Therefore, the exemption likely applies to content that has not yet been reported. However, once a report is filed and verified by Aurora’s moderation team, Aurora gains actual knowledge. At this point, the duty to act expeditiously arises. The 4-hour removal window established by Aurora complies with the requirement for expeditious action under current case law standards.

#### 3.2. Impact of the Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065)

As a provider of a platform service, Aurora falls under the scope of the Digital Services Act (DSA). While the DSA introduces stricter obligations regarding risk assessment and content moderation transparency, it does not abolish the liability exemptions established by the e-Commerce Directive. However, under the DSA, as a Very Large Online Platform (VLOP) candidate if its monthly active users exceed 45 million in the EU, Aurora would face additional due diligence requirements. Based on current user metrics (approx. 200,000 MAU), Aurora is not currently a VLOP but remains subject to general platform obligations under Title III of the DSA.

#### 3.3. Relationship with Helix Online B.V.

The contract with Helix Online B.V. is crucial. Article 6:196c DCC applies to the party that stores the information. If Helix merely provides raw storage without any involvement in the content’s formatting or moderation, Helix may also benefit from the exemption. However, because Aurora controls the user interface and the moderation workflow, Aurora is the entity liable for the *presentation* of the service. The indemnification clauses in the contract with Helix should be reviewed to ensure that if Helix’s infrastructure fails to prevent illegal content (e.g., due to security flaws), liability does not inappropriately shift back to Aurora.

#### 3.4. Advertising and Algorithmic Targeting

The fact that Aurora uses user data for ad targeting raises questions under Article 10 of the e-Commerce Directive regarding "active role." Case law suggests that merely using algorithms to optimize ads based on stored content does not automatically negate the hosting exemption, provided the provider does not modify the content itself or play an active role in *selecting* specific illegal items for promotion. The distinction lies between passive storage and active involvement. Aurora’s current model appears to remain within the safe harbor, but any future feature allowing users to "promote" specific posts for a fee would require immediate legal reassessment.

### 4. Recommendations and Next Steps

1.  **Contractual Review:** We recommend a thorough review of the Service Level Agreement with Helix Online B.V. dated 15 January 2025. Specifically, we must ensure that the definition of "hosting" clearly distinguishes technical storage from editorial control to preserve the exemption for both parties where possible.
2.  **Notice-and-Takedown Procedure:** Formalize the internal procedure for handling reports. While the 4-hour window is compliant, a standardized template for notifying users of takedowns and allowing appeals is required under the DSA. We will draft this policy.
3.  **Transparency Reports:** Aurora must prepare to publish annual transparency reports regarding content moderation efforts, as required by Article 24 of the Digital Services Act.
4.  **Risk Assessment:** Although not yet a VLOP, Aurora should conduct an initial risk assessment under Article 26 DSA to identify systemic risks related to its algorithmic amplification features.

### 5. Conclusion

Aurora Media B.V. currently qualifies for the intermediary exemption under Article 6:196c DCC provided it maintains its passive stance prior to notification and acts expeditiously thereafter. The partnership with Helix Online B.V. is legally sound regarding technical hosting, but the contractual boundaries must be clearly delineated. Immediate action is required to draft compliant takedown procedures and transparency reporting mechanisms in anticipation of stricter enforcement under the Digital Services Act.
