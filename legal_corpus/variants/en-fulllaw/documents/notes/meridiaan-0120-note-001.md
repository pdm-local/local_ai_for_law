**1. Subject of the Meeting**
The purpose of this intake was to assess the legal situation regarding a potential infringement of Cobalt Software B.V.’s intellectual property rights by Tinexus Digital B.V., specifically concerning a recently launched user interface design. Additionally, the client sought clarification on the implications of a prior settlement involving a penalty payment of € 5.000.

**2. Facts Presented by Client**
Ms. Vance reported that during a routine market surveillance audit conducted in early March 2025, Cobalt Software B.V. identified that Tinexus Digital B.V. has released a new digital product ("Tinexus Flow") which incorporates a visual design and layout strikingly similar to Cobalt’s proprietary interface registered under copyright.

The client asserts the following points:
1.  **Substantial Similarity:** The graphical user interface (GUI) of Tinexus Flow replicates the distinctive color scheme, iconography, and hierarchical structure of Cobalt’s software, which has been in use since January 2021.
2.  **Prior Settlement Context:** During preliminary negotiations held in November 2023 between the parties’ external counsel, a dispute regarding minor UI overlaps was resolved via a settlement agreement. The client explicitly references this agreement, noting that Tinexus Digital B.V. had agreed to pay a penalty of € 5.000 for any future non-compliance or recurrence of similar design elements within a twenty-four-month period.
3.  **Current Breach:** Cobalt Software B.V. considers the current launch by Tinexus Digital B.V. to be a clear violation of the terms established in the November 2023 settlement, as well as a direct infringement of copyright under Article 1 of the Dutch Copyright Act.

**3. Legal Analysis and Advice**
Mr. Reinders provided the following legal assessment based on Dutch intellectual property law:

*   **Copyright Protection:** Under Article 1 of the Dutch Copyright Act, original works of literature, science, or art are protected upon creation. While individual UI elements may often be considered functional, the specific artistic arrangement and distinctive design choices can qualify for protection. Mr. Reinders advised that Cobalt Software B.V. must demonstrate that the copied elements possess a sufficient degree of originality (*eigen karakter*) to warrant protection, rather than being dictated solely by technical function or standard industry practices.
*   **Enforcement of Previous Settlement:** The reference to the € 5.000 penalty is legally significant but requires careful examination. Under Article 7:900 DCC, settlement agreements are binding contracts. If the November 2023 agreement contained a specific clause defining "similar design" and linking it to the penalty for recurrence, Cobalt Software B.V. may be entitled to claim this amount as liquidated damages (*boetebeding*). However, Mr. Reinders warned that enforcing this requires proof that the new design falls squarely within the scope of the definition used in 2023.
*   **Next Steps:** To strengthen the position, Cobalt Software B.V. should immediately preserve evidence of the Tinexus Flow interface (including notarized screenshots or time-stamped logs) to prevent deletion by the counterparty. A formal letter of demand (*ingebrekestelling*) should be drafted, citing both the copyright infringement and the breach of the settlement agreement.

**4. Action Items**
1.  Cobalt Software B.V. to provide all source code comparisons and design files from 2021 alongside current screenshots of Tinexus Flow for expert analysis.
2.  Meridiaan Advocaten to review the full text of the November 2023 settlement agreement to verify the exact wording of the penalty clause and the definition of infringing acts.
3.  Upon confirmation of actionable grounds, Mr. Reinders will draft a letter of demand to Tinexus Digital B.V., invoking Article 1 of the Dutch Copyright Act and the contractual obligations from the prior settlement.

**5. Conclusion**
The meeting concluded with an agreement that Cobalt Software B.V. has prima facie grounds for action. The specific reference to the € 5.000 penalty in the prior context provides a pragmatic leverage point, but the primary legal basis remains the protection of original copyrightable expression.
