**1. Background and Fact Pattern**

Nimbus Interactive B.V. ("Nimbus") is currently facing severe liquidity issues and has indicated an intention to file for bankruptcy within the coming weeks. During a review of the company’s banking statements for the period leading up to the insolvency, a significant irregularity was identified.

On 15 January 2025, Nimbus transferred the sum of **320.000** euros to Polderzicht Media B.V. ("Polderzicht"). At the time of this transfer, Nimbus was already in a state of over-indebtedness (*solvabiliteitscrisis*). The parties have no prior commercial relationship involving substantial cross-border services that would justify such a large outflow. Polderzicht is owned by Mr. H. van den Berg, who is also a minority shareholder in Nimbus.

The purpose stated in the invoice attached to this transfer was "consulting services," yet no deliverables or work reports corresponding to this value have been produced. Given the timing—mere weeks before the anticipated bankruptcy filing—and the connection between the recipient and the debtor’s management, this transaction raises strong suspicions of fraudulent preference (*frauduleus voorrecht*) or a voidable act under insolvency law.

**2. Legal Analysis**

We have assessed the possibility of recovering these funds for the benefit of the estate via avoidance proceedings. The primary legal basis lies in the Bankruptcy Act (*Faillissementswet*).

**Article 42 Bankruptcy Act** allows the trustee to request the court to declare acts voidable if they were performed by the bankrupt debtor with the intent to prejudice creditors, or if the other party was aware of this intent. Furthermore, under general civil law principles applied in insolvency contexts, transactions made at an undervalue or without reasonable consideration in the suspect period can be challenged.

Key considerations include:
1.  **The Suspect Period:** The transfer occurred well within the typical look-back period. Courts often scrutinize transactions in the three months preceding bankruptcy more heavily if a relationship of trust exists between the parties.
2.  **Lack of Consideration:** The absence of tangible deliverables for **320.000** euros suggests that Polderzicht received a benefit without providing equivalent value to Nimbus, thereby diminishing the assets available to other creditors.
3.  **Subjective Intent vs. Objective Circumstances:** While proving subjective intent (*opzet*) can be difficult, the objective circumstances—the close personal ties between the shareholders and the lack of commercial rationale—create a strong presumption of collusion to shield assets from creditors.

**Article 40 Bankruptcy Act** further supports our position by allowing the trustee to terminate contracts that are detrimental to the estate, but in this case, since the service was allegedly already "rendered" (or not), we seek restitution of the funds under Article 42 rather than mere termination.

**3. Strategic Advice**

It is our professional opinion that avoidance proceedings should be instituted immediately upon the formal declaration of bankruptcy for Nimbus Interactive B.V.

**Recommended Actions:**
1.  **Preserve Evidence:** Secure all emails, internal memos, and bank records related to the invoice and the transfer dated 15 January 2025. Ensure no further payments are made to Polderzicht or its associated entities.
2.  **Bankruptcy Filing:** Proceed with the voluntary bankruptcy filing for Nimbus by no later than 20 March 2025. Delaying the filing may expose the directors to liability for worsening the insolvency (*faillissementsdelict*).
3.  **Avoidance Claim:** Upon appointment, the trustee will file a petition with the Sub-district Court of Eindhoven to declare the payment of **320.000** euros voidable pursuant to Article 42 Bankruptcy Act. We expect Polderzicht to argue that the payment was for legitimate consulting work; however, the burden of proof regarding the provision of such services will shift heavily onto them given the lack of documentation.

**4. Risks and Costs**

Litigation in avoidance proceedings can be protracted. Polderzicht may contest the claim, arguing commercial necessity. However, given the clear conflict of interest and the absence of deliverables, the success probability is assessed as moderate to high. Legal costs for such proceedings are typically borne by the estate if successful, but initially may need to be advanced by Nimbus or its directors depending on the timing of the appointment.

**5. Conclusion**

The transfer of **320.000** euros to Polderzicht Media B.V. represents a clear risk of asset stripping. Immediate action is required to secure the bankruptcy filing and prepare for avoidance proceedings. We recommend authorizing the firm to draft the bankruptcy petition immediately.

**Next Steps:**
*   Drafting of the bankruptcy petition (*faillissementsverzoekschrift*).
*   Preparation of evidence package for potential Article 42 proceedings.
