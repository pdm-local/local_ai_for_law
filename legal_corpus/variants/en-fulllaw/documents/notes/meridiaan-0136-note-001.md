**1. Facts and Instructions**

Ms. van der Meer instructed the firm to terminate the commercial cooperation agreement between Nordlicht Networks B.V. ("Nordlicht") and Novael Technologies B.V. ("Novael"). The relationship has become untenable due to Novael’s repeated failure to deliver critical software updates as stipulated in the Service Level Agreement dated 12 January 2024.

Nordlicht wishes to end all ties immediately and seeks to minimize financial exposure. Specifically, Nordlicht is concerned about potential claims from Novael regarding early termination fees or compensation for lost profits. The client has been advised that no formal notice of termination has yet been sent to avoid prejudicing the legal position.

**2. Legal Analysis**

The primary legal challenge in this matter concerns the classification of the relationship. While Nordlicht views the contract as a standard service agreement, Novael may argue that the integration of their development team into Nordlicht’s workflow creates an employment-like relationship or a long-term partnership with strict dissolution clauses.

Under **Article 7:401 DCC**, the duty of care requires us to assess whether unilateral termination is justified by *force majeure* or fundamental breach. Given the documented delays in deliverables, we have grounds to argue that Novael has failed to perform its obligations properly. However, case law suggests that courts are hesitant to allow immediate termination without a formal warning period unless the breach is irreparable.

Furthermore, if the court reclassifies the relationship as an employment contract or finds that the termination violates **Article 6:248 DCC** (reasonableness and fairness), Nordlicht could be liable for significant damages. The risk is elevated by the fact that Novael has invested substantial resources in customizing its platform for Nordlicht’s specific needs, creating a reliance interest that may be protected under **Article 6:233 DCC**.

The file is deemed weak regarding the possibility of obtaining a full waiver of compensation from Novael. It is highly probable that any settlement will require a financial component to ensure finality and prevent prolonged litigation.

**3. Transition Payment and Financial Exposure**

During the discussion, we calculated the potential financial liabilities associated with a forced dissolution or a negotiated exit. The most critical figure identified relates to the transition payment (transitievergoeding) if the relationship is recharacterized or if a statutory settlement is required under **Article 7:673 DCC**.

Based on the monthly fee structure and the duration of the engagement, the estimated liability for the transition payment amounts to exactly € 21.300. This figure represents the lower bound of what Novael could successfully claim in court should they prove that Nordlicht’s termination was unlawful or insufficiently justified by prior warnings.

Ms. van der Meer confirmed that her company can allocate this budget internally to facilitate a quick settlement, provided that Novael waives all further claims regarding reputational damage and future lost revenue.

**4. Advice and Next Steps**

1.  **Do not unilaterally terminate yet.** Sending a termination notice without prior formal warning may strengthen Novael’s position under **Article 6:248 DCC**.
2.  **Issue a Formal Warning.** We will draft a letter citing **Article 6:74 DCC** for breach of contract, granting Novael a final fourteen-day period to cure the defects. This establishes the necessary groundwork for subsequent termination.
3.  **Prepare Settlement Offer.** Simultaneously, we will prepare a settlement proposal offering the transition payment of € 21.300 in exchange for a mutual release of claims. This neutralizes the risk of higher damages awards.
4.  **Document Preservation.** Nordlicht must preserve all internal communications regarding Novael’s performance failures to support the claim of fundamental breach.

**5. Conclusion**

The matter requires immediate action to mitigate reputational and financial risk. The weak legal position regarding unilateral termination necessitates a proactive settlement strategy centered on the calculated transition payment of € 21.300. Ms. van der Meer instructed us to proceed with drafting the formal warning and the parallel settlement offer.
