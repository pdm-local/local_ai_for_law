**1. Purpose of Meeting**
The meeting was held to conduct an initial intake regarding a potential civil liability claim against Veldhoven Software B.V. (“Veldhoven”) for damages suffered by Aurora Digital B.V. (“Aurora”) due to defective performance and subsequent breach of contract.

**2. Facts as Presented by Client**
Ms. van der Meer reported that Aurora Digital B.V. engaged Veldhoven Software B.V. on 15 March 2024 for the development and implementation of a custom customer relationship management (CRM) system. The agreement stipulated delivery by 30 November 2024.

Veldhoven failed to deliver a functioning system by the agreed deadline. Upon eventual delivery in January 2025, the software contained critical errors that prevented Aurora from processing client orders for a period of six weeks. During this downtime, Aurora suffered significant operational disruption and lost revenue.

Ms. van der Meer calculated the direct financial loss resulting from this failure, including:
*   Lost profit on unprocessed orders: € 18.500;
*   Additional costs incurred to hire emergency temporary staff and cloud-based workaround solutions: € 9.000.

The total claimed amount is **€ 27.500**.

**3. Legal Assessment**
I advised Ms. van der Meer on the legal basis for the claim. The primary ground lies in non-conforming performance under Book 7 of the Dutch Civil Code (DCC). Specifically:

*   **Article 7:401 DCC**: Veldhoven, as a professional provider, has a high duty of care (*zorgvuldigheidsnorm*). Delivering software with critical errors that halt business operations constitutes a breach of this duty.
*   **Article 6:248(2) DCC**: The principle of reasonableness and fairness requires Veldhoven to bear the risk of technical defects in their deliverable, especially given their professional status.

Regarding damages:
*   Under general tort principles (**Article 6:162 DCC**), liability arises from unlawful act, culpability, causation, and damage. Here, the contractual breach is the primary vehicle.
*   **Article 6:98 DCC** (Causation): The causal link between Veldhoven’s defective software and Aurora’s financial loss is direct and unbroken. Had the software functioned as contracted, the orders would have been processed.
*   **Article 6:107 DCC**: The additional costs for temporary staff and alternative solutions are considered reasonable third-party costs (*derdenkosten*) directly resulting from the breach.

**4. Risks Identified**
I highlighted the following risks to Aurora Digital B.V.:
*   **Defence of Veldhoven**: Veldhoven may argue that the delays were caused by Aurora’s failure to provide necessary API access or data cleansing in a timely manner (contributory negligence). We must gather evidence proving that all requested information was provided on time.
*   **Insolvency Risk**: Preliminary checks suggest Veldhoven Software B.V. has been reporting late financial statements. There is a non-negligible risk of insolvency proceedings. If bankruptcy is declared, claims for damages may be subordinated or difficult to enforce (**Article 1 Bankruptcy Act**).
*   **Quantification of Loss**: The calculation of lost profit requires robust documentation (order logs vs. actual processed orders) to withstand judicial scrutiny.

**5. Recommended Next Steps**
1.  **Formal Demand Letter**: Draft and send a formal *ingebrekestelling* to Veldhoven Software B.V., demanding payment of € 27.500 within fourteen days, citing breach of contract and potential litigation costs.
2.  **Evidence Preservation**: Secure all email correspondence, project management logs (Jira/Trello), and financial records from March 2024 to present.
3.  **Credit Check**: Commission a current credit report on Veldhoven Software B.V. to assess its solvency status immediately.

**6. Conclusion**
Ms. van der Meer instructed Meridiaan Advocaten to proceed with the preparation of the formal demand letter. The matter is classified as high priority due to the potential insolvency risk of the counterparty.
