**Attendance Note – Matter 2025.0125**

**Date:** 12 April 2025
**Time:** 14:00 – 15:30
**Location:** Meridiaan Advocaten B.V., Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven (via secure video link)

**Attendees:**
*   mr. F. Lindeman (Meridiaan Advocaten)
*   Ms. J. van den Berg, CFO of Kempen Labs B.V.

**1. Introduction and Mandate**
The meeting commenced with confirmation of the mandate to represent Kempen Labs B.V. ("the Client") in relation to the tax assessment issued by the Dutch Tax Administration on 28 March 2025. The Client’s primary concern is the substantial financial impact of the reassessment and the accompanying penalty.

**2. Facts of the Case**
Ms. van den Berg explained that the Client operates a technology consultancy firm based in Eindhoven. In December 2024, the Dutch Tax Administration conducted a desk audit concerning the fiscal year ending 31 December 2023. The administration concluded that certain research and development expenditures claimed under the Innovation Box regime did not meet the statutory requirements for qualification.

Consequently, the Tax Administration issued a reassessment notice on 28 March 2025. The key figures are as follows:
*   **Additional Principal Tax:** € 127.300
*   **Penalty Imposed:** 50% of the additional principal tax, amounting to € 63.650.
*   **Total Claimed Amount:** € 190.950

The administration argues that the Client failed to maintain adequate documentation regarding the link between the innovative activities and the resulting profits, citing a lack of reasonable care in filing.

**3. Legal Assessment and Strategy Discussion**
mr. Lindeman reviewed the reassessment notice alongside the supporting documentation provided by the Client. The discussion focused on two main pillars:

*   **Substantive Appeal:** We analyzed whether the Tax Administration correctly applied the criteria for the Innovation Box. While the documentation was incomplete, we identified technical reports and internal emails that suggest a genuine innovative process occurred. mr. Lindeman advised that we should argue against the substantive add-on tax by demonstrating that the activities met the core requirements of Article 3.8 Income Tax Act 2001, albeit with administrative shortcomings rather than fundamental non-compliance.
*   **Penalty Reduction:** The imposition of a 50% penalty is severe. mr. Lindeman noted that while the administration claims gross negligence, we can argue for a reduction based on mitigating circumstances. We discussed the possibility of invoking Article 67d AWR (State Taxes Act) to seek a waiver or significant reduction of the penalty, given that the Client acted in good faith and cooperated fully during the initial inquiry phase.

**4. Action Items**
1.  **Document Collection:** The Client will provide all internal technical reports, project timelines, and correspondence with R&D staff for the period January 2023 to December 2023 by 19 April 2025.
2.  **Objection Drafting:** mr. Lindeman will draft a formal objection letter targeting both the substantive add-on tax and the penalty. The strategy will be to first challenge the qualification of the activities and, subsidiarily, argue for the removal or reduction of the penalty due to lack of intent.
3.  **Deadline Monitoring:** The objection period expires on 26 April 2025 (Article 7:1 DCC applies to the procedural timeline for objections to tax assessments). We must file the objection before this deadline.

**5. Conclusion**
The Client understands the risks associated with the substantive position but remains hopeful that a settlement can be reached regarding the penalty amount if we successfully contest the core qualification issues. mr. Lindeman will proceed with drafting the objection immediately following receipt of the requested documents.
