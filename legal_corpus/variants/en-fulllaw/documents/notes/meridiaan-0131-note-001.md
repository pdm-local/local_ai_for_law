**Attendance Note**

**Matter:** 2025.0131
**Client:** Brightlane Software B.V. (on behalf of its director, Mr. Arjan van den Berg)
**Date of Intake:** 15 January 2025
**Lawyer:** mr. H. Yildiz

**1. Facts and Background**
On 15 January 2025, we held an intake meeting with Mr. Arjan van den Berg, director of Brightlane Software B.V., located at Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven. The purpose of the meeting was to address a letter received from the Immigration and Naturalisation Service (IND) dated 10 January 2025.

The IND has informed us that it intends to reject the application for a residence permit as a highly skilled migrant for Mr. Arjan van den Berg. The primary ground cited by the authority is that Brightlane Software B.V. no longer meets the criteria regarding the required gross monthly salary threshold for the applicant under the current regulations.

**2. Procedural Timeline**
We explained to Mr. van den Berg the procedural steps available in response to this intended rejection. Crucially, we highlighted that the client has a strict deadline of **6 weeks** from the date of the letter (10 January 2025) to submit a written objection and/or provide supplementary evidence to counter the IND’s grounds for rejection. This period expires on 7 March 2025. Missing this deadline will result in the automatic issuance of a final rejection, after which only limited appeal options remain under Article 8 ECHR or Article 29 Aliens Act 2000, depending on the specific outcome.

**3. Family Situation and Subsidiary Applications**
Mr. van den Berg informed us that his wife, Ms. Elena van den Berg (born 12 May 1985), and their two minor children are currently residing in Eindhoven. The application for derivative residence permits for these family members is dependent on the success of Mr. van den Berg’s primary application.

We discussed the implications of a potential rejection on the family unit. Specifically, we addressed the risk of separation if the primary permit is denied and whether there are grounds to invoke Article 8 ECHR regarding their private and family life in the Netherlands, given their length of stay and integration. We noted that even in the event of a rejection, the family may retain certain rights during the objection period, but urgent action is required to prevent immediate enforcement measures.

**4. Next Steps**
1. Draft a formal objection letter to be sent to the IND no later than 28 February 2025, allowing time for internal review and client feedback.
2. Gather financial evidence (audited accounts of Brightlane Software B.V. for 2024) to prove that the salary requirement was met at the time of application or has since been rectified.
3. Prepare a statement regarding the family situation to support any potential Article 8 ECHR arguments should the objection be denied.

**5. Conclusion**
The client understands the urgency of the **6 weeks** response period and has authorized Meridiaan Advocaten B.V. to proceed with the preparation of the objection. We will contact Mr. van den Berg next week to schedule a follow-up discussion once the draft objection is ready for review.
