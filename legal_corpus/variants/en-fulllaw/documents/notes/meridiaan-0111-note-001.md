**1. Background and Current Situation**

The client, Saffier Software B.V., received a formal notice of additional assessment on 28 February 2025. The primary issue concerns the fiscal year ending 31 December 2024. The Dutch Tax Administration has determined that a significant portion of the R&D tax credits (Wet Bevordering Speur en Ontwikkeling) claimed by the company were ineligible due to insufficient documentation of the technical progress achieved.

Consequently, the Administration has issued an additional assessment for the corporate income tax liability amounting to € 460.000. Furthermore, the Administration has applied a penalty of 50% on this additional amount, citing gross negligence in maintaining proper administrative records as required under Article 67d AWR. The total outstanding debt currently stands at € 690.000, inclusive of statutory interest accrued since the original due date.

**2. Discussion Points**

During the intake, we discussed the grounds for objection and the viability of challenging both the principal tax amount and the penalty.

**2.1. The Principal Amount (€ 460.000)**
Ms. de Vies presented the technical project files submitted to the Tax Administration in November 2024. She argued that the software development involved novel algorithms for data compression which met the criteria for qualifying R&D activities. The Tax Administration’s rejection was based on a misunderstanding of the technical documentation provided. We agree that there are strong factual arguments to contest the qualification of these specific projects as non-qualifying. However, the burden of proof lies with the taxpayer.

**2.2. The Penalty (50%)**
The most critical issue in this matter is the application of the 50% penalty. Under Article 67d AWR, a penalty of 100% can be imposed for intentional incorrectness, while 50% is standard for gross negligence. The Administration contends that the failure to document technical progress constitutes gross negligence.

We discussed whether this characterization holds up legally. While the documentation was incomplete, it was not entirely absent. We have internal emails from June 2024 where the development team explicitly discussed the innovative nature of the work. This suggests that while administrative carelessness occurred, it may not rise to the level of *gross* negligence required for the maximum penalty tier. We aim to reduce the penalty to 10% or argue for its complete waiver based on the principle of reasonableness and fairness (Article 6:248 DCC) given the client’s clean compliance history over the past ten years.

**3. Evidence Review**

We reviewed the following documents provided by Ms. de Vries:
1.  The additional assessment notice dated 28 February 2025.
2.  The penalty calculation sheet referencing Article 67d AWR.
3.  Internal R&D project logs from Q2 and Q3 2024.
4.  Correspondence with the external technical auditor who initially reviewed the projects.
5.  Bank statements confirming the company’s solvency, to demonstrate that the debt can be paid if the objection fails, though we advise against pre-emptive payment as it might complicate the interest calculation during appeal.

**4. Legal Strategy and Next Steps**

We have advised Saffier Software B.V. to file a formal objection against both the principal amount of € 460.000 and the 50% penalty within the statutory period. The objection deadline is 6 April 2025, so immediate action is required.

The strategy will be twofold:
1.  **Factual Defense:** Argue that the projects qualified under Article 8 Corporate Income Tax Act 1969 due to genuine technical uncertainty and advancement. We will submit supplementary technical reports drafted by an independent IT expert to support this claim.
2.  **Penalty Mitigation:** Argue that the omission was not gross negligence but rather a minor administrative error, warranting a lower penalty rate or waiver. We will emphasize the client's proactive cooperation during the initial audit phase.

**5. Action Items**

*   **Meridiaan Advocaten:** Draft the objection letter (bezwaarschrift) by 28 March 2025. Prepare a technical supplement opinion by an independent expert.
*   **Client:** Provide all raw code repositories and commit logs related to the disputed projects by 21 March 2025 to support the technical argument.
*   **Both Parties:** Schedule a follow-up call on 21 March 2025 to review the draft objection before submission.

**6. Conclusion**

The matter presents a moderate risk profile regarding the principal amount, which we estimate at a 40% chance of partial success. However, the penalty has strong grounds for reduction. We proceed with filing the objection on behalf of Saffier Software B.V.
