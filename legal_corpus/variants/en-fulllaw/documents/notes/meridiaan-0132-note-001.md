**Subject:** Intake regarding liability and service levels in contracts with Hanze Networks B.V.

### 1. Introduction and Purpose
The meeting was convened at the request of Mr. van der Meer to provide legal clarity on the current contractual relationship between Lumen Digital B.V. ("Client") and Hanze Networks B.V. ("Counterparty"). The Client has recently experienced three significant service disruptions in Q1 2025, resulting in alleged downtime exceeding the agreed Service Level Agreement (SLA) thresholds. The primary objective of this intake is to determine the enforceability of liability caps in the existing Master Service Agreement (MSA) and to evaluate potential claims for damages under Dutch law.

### 2. Factual Background
The Client and Counterparty entered into a Master Service Agreement on 15 September 2023, governed by Dutch law. The agreement covers cloud infrastructure hosting and API maintenance services. Key contractual terms discussed include:

*   **Service Level Agreement:** The MSA stipulates a guaranteed uptime of 99.9%. Failure to meet this threshold triggers service credits equivalent to 10% of the monthly fee per hour of downtime, capped at 25% of the annual contract value.
*   **Liability Cap:** Clause 14.2 of the MSA limits total liability for indirect damages and caps direct liability at twice the fees paid in the preceding twelve months (EUR 240,000).
*   **Recent Incidents:**
    *   Incident A: 12 January 2025 – 8-hour outage. Service credits issued as per contract.
    *   Incident B: 23 February 2025 – 24-hour outage. Service credits issued, but Client alleges consequential data loss not covered by the credit mechanism.
    *   Incident C: 05 April 2025 – 6-hour partial degradation. No credits issued due to a "Force Majeure" clause invoked by Counterparty.

### 3. Legal Assessment
Mr. Bergsma provided preliminary legal advice based on the provided contract drafts and incident reports. The core issues are analyzed under the following statutory framework:

**A. Breach of Contract and Damages (Article 6:74 DCC)**
The Client is entitled to damages if Counterparty is in breach of its obligations. The February incident likely constitutes a breach if the "Force Majeure" defense is invalid, as internal server failures generally do not qualify as force majeure under Dutch law. Under Article 6:74 DCC, the Client may claim compensation for actual losses suffered, provided causation can be established.

**B. Reasonableness and Fairness (Articles 6:248 DCC and 6:248(2) DCC)**
Mr. Bergsma highlighted that liability caps in standard terms are subject to the standards of reasonableness and fairness. If the cap is deemed unreasonably onerous for the Client—particularly given the critical nature of the infrastructure services—it may be declared null and void by a court. The exclusion of indirect damages (such as lost profits) is common but heavily scrutinized under Article 6:248 DCC.

**C. Duty of Care (Article 7:401 DCC)**
As the service provider, Counterparty has an unwritten duty to perform its obligations with the care expected from a reasonable service provider. The recurrence of outages may suggest a failure in maintenance protocols, potentially aggravating liability beyond the contractual cap if gross negligence is proven.

**D. General Terms (Article 6:233 DCC)**
If the MSA was not individually negotiated and relies on Hanze Networks' standard terms, Article 6:233 DCC applies. Clauses that deviate from statutory law to the detriment of the consumer or SME in a way that is unexpected may be considered invalid.

### 4. Strategic Options Discussed
1.  **Negotiation:** Send a formal letter of demand citing Articles 6:74 and 6:248 DCC, requesting an increase in service credits for Q1 2025 and waiving the liability cap for the February incident due to gross negligence.
2.  **Contract Review:** Conduct a full audit of the MSA to identify any non-enforceable clauses under Article 6:233 DCC before initiating formal proceedings.
3.  **Rescission:** Consider rescission of the contract under Article 6:265 DCC if the breaches are fundamental, though this is risky given the integration of services into the Client's workflow.

**Conclusion**
The Client has strong grounds to challenge the liability cap under Article 6:248 DCC, particularly regarding the February incident. Immediate formal communication is recommended to preserve legal rights and demonstrate diligence in mitigating losses.
