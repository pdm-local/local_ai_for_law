**1. Introduction and Context**

On 14 May 2025, I held an intake meeting with Ms. E. van der Meer, director of **Maaslicht Cloud B.V.** (“the Client”), regarding corporate governance disputes within the Maaslicht group. The Client holds a minority shareholding in **Maaslicht Systems B.V.** (“the Company”). The discussion focused on the legality and propriety of a recent dividend distribution approved by the Company’s general meeting, as well as the Client’s consideration of initiating inquiry proceedings.

**2. Factual Background**

The Maaslicht group comprises two primary entities: Maaslicht Cloud B.V., which provides cloud infrastructure services, and Maaslicht Systems B.V., which develops proprietary software solutions. The Client currently holds exactly **49%** of the issued share capital in Maaslicht Systems B.V. The remaining 51% is held by a private holding company controlled by Mr. J. de Vries, who also serves as the sole managing director of the Company.

On 10 May 2025, the general meeting of shareholders of Maaslicht Systems B.V. approved a dividend distribution of **€ 480.000**. The Client voted against this resolution, citing concerns regarding the Company’s liquidity position and the lack of adequate financial reserves for upcoming operational expenditures. Despite the Client’s opposition, the resolution passed due to the majority vote held by Mr. de Vries.

**3. Legal Assessment**

**3.1. Distributability of Dividend (Article 2:216 DCC)**
Under Article 2:216 of the Dutch Civil Code (DCC), a dividend may only be distributed if it does not impair the net assets as shown in the previous annual balance sheet, nor does it result in net assets falling below the sum of the paid-up and called-up portion of the share capital plus the statutory reserves.

During our review of the Company’s latest audited financial statements (as of 31 December 2024), we identified that the retained earnings were sufficient to cover the distribution on paper. However, Mr. de Vries has not provided updated interim management accounts for Q1 2025. Given the significant cash outflows related to a recent server migration project undertaken by Maaslicht Cloud B.V., there is a risk that the Company’s solvency ratio has deteriorated materially since the balance sheet date. If the dividend impairs the legal capital or statutory reserves, it constitutes an unlawful distribution under Article 2:216 DCC, potentially rendering Mr. de Vries personally liable for repayment.

**3.2. Director’s Duty of Care (Article 2:9 DCC)**
Mr. de Vries, as managing director, is bound by the duty to act in the interest of the Company and its associated enterprises (Article 2:9 DCC). Approving a large dividend distribution while ignoring warnings from the minority shareholder regarding liquidity risks may constitute a breach of this duty if it prioritizes the personal financial gain of the majority shareholder over the long-term viability of the Company.

**3.3. Conflict of Interest and Abuse of Power**
The fact that the Client holds **49%** of the shares creates a deadlock scenario in certain contexts, but here the majority shareholder is using its position to enforce a distribution despite minority opposition. This raises questions under Article 2:239 DCC regarding conflicts of interest if Mr. de Vries benefits personally from this cash flow without adequate justification.

**4. Strategic Options: Inquiry Proceedings (Article 2:345 DCC)**

The Client is seriously considering filing for inquiry proceedings (*enquête*) against Maaslicht Systems B.V. under Article 2:345 DCC.

*   **Grounds:** The Client alleges that the management of the Company has conducted its affairs in a manner that is seriously detrimental to the interests of the Company and its associated enterprises, specifically by disregarding the financial health of the entity for the benefit of the majority shareholder.
*   **Procedure:** An inquiry can be requested by any shareholder who holds at least 1% of the share capital or shares with a nominal value of at least €22.500. The Client comfortably meets this threshold given its **49%** stake and the nominal value of its holdings.
*   **Remedies:** If the court finds the grounds sufficient, it may appoint an interim administrator, order the annulment of resolutions, or even dissolve the Company. In practice, the threat of an inquiry often forces a settlement or a change in management structure.

**5. Recommendations and Next Steps**

1.  **Immediate Demand Letter:** Draft a formal letter to Mr. de Vries demanding suspension of the dividend payment pending a review of the interim financial position. Cite Article 2:216 DCC and the risk of personal liability.
2.  **Financial Forensic Review:** Engage an independent accountant to verify the solvency position of Maaslicht Systems B.V. as of April 2025. This will determine if the distribution was already unlawful at the time of approval.
3.  **Preparation for Inquiry:** If Mr. de Vries refuses to suspend payment, prepare a petition for inquiry proceedings under Article 2:345 DCC. The Client should gather all email correspondence where warnings about liquidity were ignored by the Board.

**6. Conclusion**

The Client has a strong procedural position to challenge the distribution if it is found to be unlawful under Article 2:216 DCC. However, the more strategic route may be to use the threat of inquiry proceedings to force a renegotiation of the group’s financial policies. The Client retains **49%** voting power, which gives them significant leverage in any future shareholder disputes or potential sale scenarios.

We await the Client’s instructions on whether to proceed with the demand letter or file for inquiry directly.
