**1. Background and Instruction**

Ms. van der Meer instructed Meridiaan Advocaten to provide legal clarity regarding the current contractual relationship between Maaslicht Online B.V. ("Maaslicht") and Vesting Systems B.V. ("Vesting"). Maaslicht is a software development firm that relies on Vesting for cloud infrastructure hosting and API integration services.

The primary concern is the ambiguity of liability clauses and service level agreements (SLAs) in the Master Service Agreement signed on 15 January 2023. Maaslicht has experienced three significant outages in the last six months, causing reputational damage and direct revenue loss. Ms. van der Meer seeks to understand:
1.  The enforceability of Vesting’s limitation of liability clauses.
2.  Whether Vesting has breached its duty of care under general contract law principles.
3.  The viability of claiming damages beyond the service credits currently offered.

**2. Fact Pattern Analysis**

The parties are bound by the Master Service Agreement (MSA) dated 15 January 2023. Key contractual points identified during the intake:
*   **Service Credits:** The MSA caps Vesting’s liability at the amount of fees paid in the preceding twelve months, excluding indirect damages.
*   **SLA Guarantees:** Vesting guarantees 99.9% uptime. During outages, Vesting provides service credits equal to 10% of the monthly fee.
*   **General Terms:** The MSA incorporates Vesting’s standard general terms and conditions, which contain broad exclusion of liability for consequential loss.

Ms. van der Meer reported that on 10 March 2025, a failure in Vesting’s API caused Maaslicht’s client portal to be offline for 14 hours, resulting in an estimated loss of €45,000 in direct transaction fees and €120,000 in anticipated contract cancellations.

**3. Legal Assessment**

As counsel for the ICT/technology practice area, I provided the following advice based on Dutch contract law:

**A. Breach of Contract and Duty of Care**
Vesting’s failure to maintain 99.9% uptime likely constitutes a breach of contract. Furthermore, under **Article 7:401 DCC**, Vesting has a duty of care towards Maaslicht as the service provider. If the outages were due to negligence in maintenance or security protocols, this reinforces the claim for damages. However, proving causation for indirect losses (such as lost profits) remains difficult under Dutch law unless specifically contracted otherwise.

**B. Reasonableness and Fairness**
We must assess whether Vesting’s liability cap is reasonable and fair. Under **Article 6:248 DCC** and specifically **6:248(2) DCC**, a standard term that unreasonably disadvantages the other party may be deemed void. If the limitation of liability effectively allows Vesting to perform negligently without consequence, a court may strike down this clause. Given the significant disparity in bargaining power (SME vs. Large Enterprise), there is a strong argument that the exclusion of consequential damages is unreasonable under **Article 6:248 DCC**.

**C. General Terms and Conditions**
The incorporation of Vesting’s general terms must be verified for compliance with **Article 6:233 DCC**. If these terms were not reasonably accessible or if they deviate unfairly from the specific MSA, they may be unenforceable. Maaslicht should review whether the liability cap was explicitly acknowledged in a manner compliant with this article.

**D. Rescission and Termination**
If Vesting’s performance is fundamentally deficient, Maaslicht may consider rescinding the contract under **Article 6:265 DCC**. However, given the ongoing dependency on their infrastructure, immediate termination poses operational risks. A formal notice of default (*ingebrekestelling*) citing the breach and demanding adherence to the SLA or compensation for damages is the recommended first step.

**4. Recommendations and Next Steps**

1.  **Evidence Collection:** Maaslicht must gather all logs, communication emails, and financial records related to the March 2025 outage and previous incidents.
2.  **Formal Letter of Demand:** We will draft a letter citing the breach of duty under **Article 7:401 DCC** and the unreasonableness of the liability cap under **Article 6:248(2) DCC**. The letter will demand compensation for direct losses and propose a renegotiation of the SLA.
3.  **Contract Review:** A full review of the MSA against **Article 6:233 DCC** is required to identify any procedural flaws in the incorporation of Vesting’s general terms.

**5. Conclusion**

Maaslicht has a plausible claim for damages, primarily by challenging the reasonableness of the liability cap rather than proving direct consequential loss. The focus should be on **Article 6:248 DCC** to render the limitation clause void. Immediate action is required to preserve evidence and assert rights before the statute of limitations expires.
