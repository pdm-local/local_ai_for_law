**1. Purpose of the Intake**
Aurora Systems B.V. (“the Client”) operates a digital platform that connects independent service providers with end-users. The Client seeks legal clarity on the extent to which it qualifies for the intermediary liability exemptions under Dutch law and the EU Digital Services Act (DSA). Specifically, the Client wishes to determine whether its role is classified as a mere technical host or if active involvement in content curation triggers liability.

**2. Facts Presented by the Client**
The Client provided the following operational details regarding its platform:
*   **Platform Model:** The platform allows third-party vendors to list services. Aurora Systems does not create the underlying content (service descriptions, user reviews) but provides the technical infrastructure for listing and communication. Zenith Networks B.V. acts as the primary technology partner providing the backend hosting infrastructure for this platform.
*   **Content Moderation:** The Client employs an automated algorithm that flags listings for potential policy violations before they are published. Human moderators review flagged items within 24 hours. Approved listings remain live until reported or expired.
*   **Monetization:** Revenue is generated through a commission fee on completed transactions and optional "promoted listing" fees paid by vendors to increase visibility in search results.
*   **Knowledge of Illegal Content:** The Client has no actual knowledge of specific illegal activities occurring on the platform prior to receipt of a notice, except where automated systems detect obvious statutory violations (e.g., copyright-infringing material identified via hash matching).

**3. Legal Analysis**

**3.1. Dutch Civil Code – Hosting Exemption**
Under Article 6:196c DCC, an intermediary service provider is not liable for information stored at the request of a recipient of the service, provided that:
a) The provider does not have actual knowledge of illegal activity or information and, upon obtaining such knowledge, acts expeditiously to remove or disable access to the information; or
b) In case of liability for damages, the provider was not aware of facts or circumstances on which an obligation to monitor or actively search for illegal facts should be based.

The Client’s use of automated filtering does not automatically constitute "knowledge" if the system operates generally rather than targeting specific content without factual basis. However, the active selection and promotion of listings via paid "promoted listing" options may challenge the passive nature required for the exemption under Article 6:196c DCC, as it implies a degree of intervention in the distribution of information.

**3.2. EU Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065)**
As an Online Platform within the meaning of the DSA, Aurora Systems B.V. must comply with specific obligations. The classification as a "hosting service" is crucial for liability limitations. The DSA requires that the provider does not play an active role such that it has knowledge or control over the data stored. If the platform optimizes or promotes specific content in a way that implies endorsement, it may lose the safe harbor. The current model of "promoted listings" requires careful structuring to ensure it is viewed as advertising rather than editorial activity.

**3.3. Information Duties**
Pursuant to Article 3:15d DCC, the Client must provide clear information regarding the identity of the service provider and, where applicable, the professional body or similar registration with which the intermediary is enrolled. The platform's terms of service and user interface must clearly disclose the commercial nature of any promoted listings.

**4. Risk Assessment**
*   **High Risk:** The integration of paid promotion tools creates a significant risk that courts may deem the Client to have an "active role," thereby negating the immunity under Article 6:196c DCC for specific listings that are commercially boosted.
*   **Medium Risk:** The automated pre-screening could be interpreted as creating actual knowledge if the algorithm is tuned to detect specific illegal acts with high precision, potentially triggering a duty to monitor beyond what is legally required.

**5. Recommendations**
1.  **Contractual Safeguards:** Ensure that the Terms of Service explicitly state that Aurora Systems acts solely as a passive intermediary and does not control the content uploaded by users, except for the minimal moderation necessary to comply with legal obligations.
2.  **Promoted Listings Structure:** Differentiate "promoted listings" clearly as advertising services separate from the core platform functionality. Ensure that promotion algorithms do not alter the substantive content of the listing but only its visibility position.
3.  **Notice-and-Takedown Procedure:** Establish a robust, easily accessible mechanism for users to report illegal content. Upon receipt of a sufficiently reasoned notice, expeditious removal must be guaranteed to maintain the exemption under Article 6:196c DCC.
4.  **DSA Compliance:** Designate a point of contact for legal authorities and ensure compliance with all transparency reporting obligations required by Regulation (EU) 2022/2065.

**6. Next Steps**
Mr. Vrugt will draft a comprehensive advisory memorandum detailing the specific contractual clauses required to preserve the intermediary status. The Client will provide a copy of its current algorithmic logic for promoted listings by 19 April 2025 for further review.
