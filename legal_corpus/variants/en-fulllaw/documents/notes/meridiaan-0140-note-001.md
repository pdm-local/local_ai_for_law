**Attendance Note**

**Date:** 14 March 2025

- Mr. A. Koster (Meridiaan Advocaten)

**1. Introduction and Mandate**
Ms. van der Meer appeared to discuss the tenancy agreement concluded on 1 September 2024 between Nimbus Retail B.V. ("Landlord") and Stroomdal Media B.V. ("Tenant") regarding the commercial premises located at Keizer Karelshaven 45, 5611 TN Eindhoven. The Landlord instructs Meridiaan Advocaten to initiate eviction proceedings due to persistent non-payment of rent.

**2. Factual Background**
The parties signed a lease for business premises (*bedrijfsruimte*) with an initial term ending on 31 August 2029. The monthly contractual rent is €8,500.00, exclusive of VAT and operating costs.

Ms. van der Meer confirmed that Stroomdal Media B.V. has ceased regular payments since November 2024. As of today, the Tenant is in arrears for exactly **4 months** of rent (November 2024 through February 2025), totaling €34,000.00. Despite multiple informal reminders sent via email on 1 December 2024 and 5 January 2025, no payments have been received. The Tenant has not provided a formal explanation for the default, nor have they communicated an intention to vacate the premises voluntarily.

**3. Legal Assessment and Defects Defence**
We discussed the grounds for dissolution of the lease under Article 7:290 DCC. Non-payment of rent for four consecutive months constitutes a substantial breach of contract, justifying immediate request for termination without notice (*ontbinding zonder termijn*).

However, we noted that during the inspection on 15 February 2025, Ms. van der Meer observed significant defects in the premises, specifically regarding the HVAC system failure and water damage in the rear storage area. The Tenant has threatened to raise a *defects defence* (*gebrekennverweer*) in any potential court proceedings, arguing that the Landlord’s failure to maintain the property excuses their non-payment.

Under Article 7:206 DCC, the Landlord is responsible for major repairs. We advised Ms. van der Meer that while the defects may be real, they generally do not suspend the obligation to pay rent unless the premises are uninhabitable or the business cannot operate at all. Given that Stroomdal Media B.V. has continued limited operations during this period, a full defense based on defects is likely to fail, though it may delay proceedings slightly. We recommended documenting the current state of repairs and the Tenant's continued use of the space to counter any future claims of constructive eviction.

**4. Strategy and Next Steps**
Ms. van der Meer instructed us to proceed with the following steps:

1.  **Formal Notice:** Draft and send a formal letter of default (*ingebrekestelling*) to Stroomdal Media B.V., demanding payment of the €34,000.00 arrears plus statutory interest within 14 days, under threat of immediate legal action.
2.  **Litigation Preparation:** Prepare a petition for dissolution of the lease and eviction (*vordering tot ontbinding en ontruiming*) to be filed at the Enterprise Chamber of the Court of Appeal in Amsterdam or the competent sub-district court, depending on procedural strategy.
3.  **Evidence Preservation:** Secure all maintenance records showing attempts to repair the HVAC system and photographic evidence of the Tenant’s continued business activities to rebut the defects defence.

**5. Conclusion**
The matter is urgent due to the accumulating arrears and the risk of the Tenant becoming insolvent. Ms. van der Meer authorized Meridiaan Advocaten to act on her behalf in this matter. We will return with the draft formal notice within 48 hours for approval.
