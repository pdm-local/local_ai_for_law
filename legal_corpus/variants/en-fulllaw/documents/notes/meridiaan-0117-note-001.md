**ATTENDANCE NOTE**

**Date:** 14 March 2025
**Time:** 10:00 – 10:45 CET
**Location:** Meridiaan Advocaten B.V., Kennedyplein 200, Eindhoven (and via secure video link)
**Attendees:**
*   Mr. H. Yildiz, Attorney-at-Law, Meridiaan Advocaten B.V.
*   Ms. Elise van der Meer, Director, Aurora Software B.V.

**1. Introduction and Background**

The meeting was convened to discuss the recent notification received from the Immigration and Naturalisation Service (IND) concerning an intended rejection of residence permit applications for three key technical staff members of Aurora Software B.V. The clients are represented by Mr. H. Yildiz in this migration matter.

Ms. van der Meer confirmed that the IND issued its letter of intent on 10 March 2025, citing insufficient alignment with the highly skilled migrant criteria due to recent changes in the employees' job descriptions. She expressed significant concern regarding the continuity of the ongoing software development project for our client’s primary European partner.

**2. Legal Framework and Response Deadline**

I informed Ms. van der Meer that upon receipt of an intended rejection, there is a strict statutory period to submit a written response or objection. Specifically, we have **4 weeks** from the date of notification to file a formal objection (bezwaarschrift). The deadline for submission is therefore 14 April 2025.

I advised that failing to submit a substantive objection within this window will result in the intended rejection becoming final and enforceable, leading to the revocation of residence permits and subsequent removal orders. Given the tight timeline, immediate action is required.

**3. Substantive Arguments and Family Situation**

We discussed the specific grounds for appeal. The primary argument will focus on the proportionality of the measure under Article 8 ECHR (Right to respect for private and family life). Ms. van der Meer detailed the family situations of the affected employees:

*   **Mr. K. Al-Fayed:** His spouse and two minor children are Dutch nationals residing in Amsterdam. The family has been living in the Netherlands for eight years, and the children attend local primary schools. Removal would cause disproportionate disruption to their private and family life.
*   **Ms. J. Petrova:** Her partner is a citizen of the European Union, exercising treaty rights in Eindhoven since 2018. The integration of Ms. Petrova into Dutch society is well-established, as evidenced by her long-term residence history and tax contributions.

I explained that while Article 8 ECHR does not guarantee an automatic right to remain, it requires the IND to weigh the interest of immigration control against the individual’s family life. Given the strong ties demonstrated, this argument forms a critical part of our objection strategy.

**4. Next Steps**

We agreed on the following action plan:

1.  **Fact-Finding:** Aurora Software B.V. will provide updated job descriptions, organizational charts, and internal emails demonstrating that the employees’ roles remain consistent with the highly skilled migrant criteria within **4 weeks** to ensure we can meet the filing deadline.
2.  **Drafting Objection:** I will draft a comprehensive objection letter addressing both the factual errors regarding the job duties and the proportionality argument based on family life.
3.  **Submission:** The final objection will be submitted via the IND’s digital portal before 14 April 2025.

**5. Conclusion**

Ms. van der Meer understood the urgency of the **4 weeks** response period and confirmed that she would coordinate with the affected employees to gather the necessary supporting documentation promptly. She thanked Meridiaan Advocaten for the clear explanation of the legal risks and the family law implications under Article 8 ECHR.

This note serves as a record of our consultation and the instructions received. No further action is required from the client until the next update on document readiness.
