**1. Introduction and Background**

The meeting was convened to address the immediate financial irregularities identified in the accounts of Meridiaan Solutions B.V. following the filing for bankruptcy on 10 March 2025. The primary focus is a specific outgoing transfer executed by the former director of Meridiaan Solutions B.V. on 28 February 2025, precisely two weeks prior to the insolvency declaration.

**2. Factual Findings Regarding the Payment**

Our review of the banking statements reveals that on 28 February 2025, a single lump sum payment was transferred from the current account of Meridiaan Solutions B.V. to an external private account belonging to its former director. The exact amount of this transfer was **95.000** euros.

At the time of this transaction, Meridiaan Solutions B.V. was already in a state of actual insolvency (overindebtedheid), as evidenced by unpaid invoices to suppliers dating back to January 2025 and the inability to meet obligations as they fell due. The payment was not made for any apparent commercial necessity or standard operational cost, but rather appeared to be a preferential transfer of assets away from the company’s estate.

**3. Legal Assessment: Avoidance of Payments**

Under Dutch insolvency law, the trustee (curator) has the right and duty to challenge transactions that undermine the equal treatment of creditors. Specifically, we must consider the provisions regarding fraudulent preference and avoidance under the Bankruptcy Act (Faillissementswet).

The payment in question falls within the suspect period (verdagte periode). According to established jurisprudence, payments made shortly before bankruptcy by a debtor who is already insolvent are highly susceptible to being annulled. The relevant statutory basis for challenging this transfer lies in Article 42 of the Bankruptcy Act, which allows the curator to avoid acts performed by the debtor with the intent to prejudice creditors or that constitute an undue preference.

Furthermore, the timing—two weeks prior to the formal bankruptcy decree—strongly suggests that the former director was aware of the impending insolvency and sought to secure personal assets at the expense of the general body of creditors. This constitutes a clear violation of the principle of par condicio creditorum (equal treatment of creditors).

**4. Strategic Recommendations**

We recommend the following immediate steps:

1.  **Formal Demand:** Aurora Logistics B.V., as the primary creditor and likely party interested in the bankruptcy estate, should formally request that the court-appointed curator institute avoidance proceedings against this specific transfer under Article 42 of the Bankruptcy Act.
2.  **Evidence Preservation:** Secure all internal correspondence between the former director and any third parties regarding this **95.000** euro payment to establish intent.
3.  **Creditors' Meeting:** Prepare for the initial creditors’ meeting scheduled for 21 March 2025, where we will advocate for the aggressive pursuit of asset recovery. The amount involved is significant enough to justify the legal costs associated with avoidance litigation.

**5. Conclusion**

The transfer of **95.000** euros from Meridiaan Solutions B.V. to a private account in February 2025 is legally vulnerable and likely voidable. We advise Aurora Logistics B.V. to cooperate fully with the curator to ensure this asset is returned to the bankruptcy estate for the benefit of all creditors.
